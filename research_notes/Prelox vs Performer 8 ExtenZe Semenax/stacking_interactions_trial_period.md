# Stacking Prelox, Performer 8, ExtenZe and Semenax: ingredient overlap, interactions, and how long a fair trial takes

Scope note on sourcing: human data only unless explicitly labelled. Label amounts were taken from the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database (DSLD), the Life Extension product page, and company press releases; where a figure comes only from retailer/review sites it is labelled as such. Three of the four products hide some or all amounts inside proprietary blends, and two (Performer 8, ExtenZe) have been reformulated, so some totals cannot be computed at all. Europe PMC and several PubMed abstract pages were blocked or unreachable from this sandbox; abstracts were retrieved through the PubMed structured-data provider and PMC instead.

## Q1. If a man took Prelox + Performer 8 + Semenax at label doses, what would his daily totals be, and which totals exceed studied or recommended limits?

### Takeaway
Only Semenax discloses every amount; Prelox hides all three actives in a 720 mg "proprietary blend" and ExtenZe hides 18 of 20 ingredients in three blends, so a complete stack total is impossible to compute. Of what can be totalled, the amounts that actually exceed studied or recommended ceilings are pine bark extract (~600–680 mg/day vs 80–120 mg in the Prelox trials), Panax ginseng (legacy Performer 8's reported 6,000 mg vs 2,700–3,000 mg/day in the ginseng trials), and — depending on how Semenax's "30 mg zinc" is read — zinc at or above the 40 mg/day upper limit. Total L-arginine stays far below the ~9 g GI threshold; iron is supplied to men who almost never need it.

**The limits the totals are judged against**
- Arginine: "Most study results suggest that up to 9 g/day arginine for several days or weeks is safe and well tolerated. At doses of 9–30 g/day, the most commonly reported adverse reactions are gastrointestinal discomfort, such as diarrhea and nausea, and slightly reduced blood pressure." — [NIH ODS, Dietary Supplements for Exercise and Athletic Performance (health professional)](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/)
- Arginine duration of evidence: "Studies have used 2 to 20 grams a day of arginine for up to 3 months." and "Arginine supplements seem safe when users take up to 9 grams a day for several days or weeks. Taking more can cause GI discomfort and can slightly lower blood pressure." — [NIH ODS consumer fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-Consumer/)
- Citrulline: "There isn't enough research on citrulline to know for sure whether it's safe. Some users have reported that it can cause stomach discomfort." Studies used "up to 9 grams of citrulline for 1 day or 6 grams per day for up to 16 days." — [NIH ODS consumer fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-Consumer/)
- Zinc: "The Tolerable Upper Intake Level for zinc is 40 mg for adults"; RDA for men 19+ is 11 mg; "If used for weeks, doses of 50 mg zinc or more… can interfere with copper absorption (which can cause low copper status), reduce immune function, and lower HDL cholesterol levels"; "High zinc intakes can cause nausea, dizziness, headaches, gastric distress, vomiting, and loss of appetite." — [NIH ODS Zinc fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/)
- Iron: RDA "Adult men 19–50 years 8 mg"; UL "Adults 19+ years 45 mg"; "In healthy people, taking high doses of iron supplements (especially on an empty stomach) can cause an upset stomach, constipation, nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting, and diarrhea… High doses of iron can also decrease zinc absorption." and "Some people have an inherited condition called hemochromatosis… Without medical treatment, people with hereditary hemochromatosis can develop serious problems such as liver cirrhosis, liver cancer, and heart disease. People with this disorder should avoid using iron supplements and vitamin C supplements." — [NIH ODS Iron fact sheet (consumer)](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-Consumer/)
- Glucuronolactone: EFSA's ANS Panel confirmed "a No Observed Adverse Effect Level… of 1,000mg per kilogram of bodyweight per day for both substances" (taurine and d-glucuronolactone) and concluded exposure from energy drinks "was not of safety concern" — [EFSA news release, 12 Feb 2009](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/news/efsa-adopts-opinion-two-ingredients-commonly-used-some-energy-drinks)
- Caffeine (relevant to the current Performer 8): "the U.S. Food and Drug Administration states that 400 mg of caffeine per day is an amount not generally associated with negative effects for most healthy adults" — [USA Today press release, Performer 8](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/43980/performer-8-reviews-wolfson-brands-announces-u-s-launch-of-current-11-ingredient-mens-wellness-formula/)
- Ginseng doses actually studied in ED: 600 mg three times daily for 4–12 weeks (Choi trials), 900 mg three times daily for 12 weeks (Kim & Paick) and 8 weeks (Hong), 1,000 mg three times daily for 12 weeks (de Andrade) — [Jang 2008 systematic review, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2561113/)
- Pycnogenol doses studied in ED/fertility: 60–120 mg/day in the Prelox trials, 200 mg/day for 90 days in a sperm-morphology study — [OA Text review](https://www.oatext.com/review-influence-of-combinations-of-pycnogenolr-and-l-arginine-aspartate-on-mens-virility-and-fertility.php); the highest dose with published safety laboratory follow-up found here is "Pycnogenol® (100 mg twice daily…) for 3 months" in 67 women, where "the activities of the liver enzymes aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) remained stable… No significant changes were observed regarding blood count, kidney function… and inflammation" — [Scherg 2026, Front Nutr](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13696226/)
- Maca doses studied: "1,000 mg of Maca or placebo, two pills at a time, three times per day for 12 weeks" in 80 eugonadal men with late-onset-hypogonadism symptoms, with AMS, IIEF and IPSS significantly improved — [Shin 2023, World J Mens Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36593713/); a 12-week RCT of "1,500 mg and 3,000 mg of maca per day… found no significant differences between maca and placebo for the measured hormone levels" — [USA Today/Leading Edge Health ExtenZe guide](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/45414/extenze-reviews-leading-edge-health-publishes-2026-label-literacy-guide-for-current-mens-wellness-formula/); 3.5 g/day for 6 weeks in postmenopausal women — [Brooks 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18784609/)
- DHEA in sport: "DHEA is prohibited in athletic competition by the NCAA and World Anti-Doping Agency" (NIH ODS, as quoted) — [USA Today/Leading Edge Health ExtenZe guide](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/45414/extenze-reviews-leading-edge-health-publishes-2026-label-literacy-guide-for-current-mens-wellness-formula/); "DHEA… is considered a performance-enhancing substance by several organizations, including the International Olympic Committee" — [Innerbody ExtenZe review](https://www.innerbody.com/extenze-reviews)
- Pregnenolone human dosing data are thin: a double-blind crossover in 17 normal volunteers used "15 mg PO per day x2 weeks followed by 30 mg PO per day x2 weeks"; "Pregnenolone was generally well-tolerated but, by itself, had no significant effects on mood, memory, self-rated sleep quality or subjective well-being" — [Meieran 2004, Psychoneuroendocrinology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14749094/)

### Inferences
- **Stacking Prelox + Performer 8 (legacy) + Semenax at label doses gives these computable daily totals** (showing only what labels or the cited sources disclose):
  - **L-arginine-containing material:** Prelox ~2.8 g/day of arginine HCl + aspartic acid at the 4-tablet loading dose (720 mg × 4 = 2,880 mg of blend, of which Pycnogenol is ~80 mg), + Performer 8 500 mg L-arginine + Semenax 250 mg L-arginine HCl ≈ **3.6 g/day of arginine salts**. Because Prelox's label declares *L-arginine HCl plus aspartic acid* and the Aoki trial's equivalent material was 690 mg arginine with 552 mg aspartic acid (≈55% arginine by weight), the actual free L-arginine delivered is roughly **2.0–2.3 g/day**, i.e. well under the ~9 g single-dose GI threshold but also **below the 5–6 g/day used in the positive arginine monotherapy trials**. This arithmetic is my inference from the cited component figures, not a label statement.
  - **Pine bark extracts:** 300 mg (Performer 8 legacy) + 300 mg (Semenax) + ~80 mg Pycnogenol (Prelox, 4 tablets) ≈ **680 mg/day**, about 6–8× the 80–120 mg Pycnogenol dose used in every positive Prelox trial and above any dose with published safety labs found here (200 mg/day). More is not better here, and the antiplatelet effect is dose-related.
  - **Zinc:** Semenax contributes **~6 mg elemental zinc** if DSLD's "Zinc Aspartate 30 mg, 20% elemental Zinc, 40% DV" is read literally (6 mg is 40% of the pre-2016 15 mg DV, so the arithmetic is internally consistent); marketing and review sites instead present Semenax as "30 mg zinc", which would be 30 mg elemental. Adding ExtenZe's 25 mg elemental zinc gives **31 mg (literal reading) or 55 mg (marketing reading)**; adding the current Performer 8's 10 mg gives **41 mg or 65 mg**. The high reading exceeds the 40 mg UL and reaches the ≥50 mg/weeks range NIH links to impaired copper absorption, reduced immune function and lower HDL. A buyer cannot resolve this from the label alone.
  - **Maca:** 30 mg (Performer 8 legacy) + 400 mg (Semenax) = **430 mg/day**, roughly one-third to one-seventh of the 1,500–3,000 mg/day used in maca trials — a dosing shortfall, not a safety ceiling.
  - **Epimedium:** 1,000 mg (Performer 8 legacy) + 150 mg (Semenax) = **1,150 mg/day**, plus an undisclosed amount inside ExtenZe's 469 mg blend. No human dose-ranging or toxicity data were found to judge this against.
  - **Panax ginseng:** supplied only by legacy Performer 8. If the 6,000 mg figure is right, that is roughly **double the highest dose used in any ED trial** (2,700–3,000 mg/day); if the 1,000 mg figure is right it is well below trial doses. The two figures conflict and neither comes from a company label, so this total is unreliable.
  - **Iron:** 12 mg ferrous bisglycinate (legacy Performer 8 only) = **150% of the 8 mg male RDA**, below the 45 mg UL, but supplied to a population that is rarely iron-deficient and for whom NIH flags hemochromatosis as a reason to avoid iron supplements.
  - **Hidden and therefore un-totalable:** all of Prelox's three actives individually; yohimbe/yohimbine, DHEA, pregnenolone, L-arginine, maca, Epimedium, Tribulus, Cnidium, muira puama, nettle, astragalus, Fo-Ti, hops and boron in ExtenZe. **A stack containing ExtenZe cannot be dose-checked at all.**
- Stacking also multiplies redundancy rather than coverage: Prelox + Performer 8 + Semenax supply three separate nitric-oxide precursor/eNOS routes, two-to-three pine bark extracts, two Epimedium sources, two Tribulus sources, two muira puama sources and hawthorn — i.e. the same two or three mechanisms paid for several times.
- Because Performer 8's US formula was replaced in 2026 with an 11-ingredient, caffeine-containing formula and ExtenZe's and Semenax's DSLD records are both marked off-market, **any stack calculation must be redone against the physical Supplement Facts panel in hand**. The company itself says so: "The Supplement Facts panel supplied with the product should remain the primary source for current serving instructions, ingredient quantities and warnings because formulations may change."

### Gaps
- No manufacturer-published Supplement Facts panel for either Performer 8 formula could be retrieved (performer8.com returned no supplement-facts text; no DSLD record for Performer 8 was found). All legacy per-ingredient amounts rest on retailer/review sites and two of them disagree on ginseng by sixfold.
- Semenax's official ingredient page gives no amounts, so the per-ingredient figures rest on an off-market DSLD label and a review site; whether current product matches is unverified.
- Whether Semenax's "30 mg zinc" is 30 mg elemental or 30 mg of zinc aspartate (≈6 mg elemental) cannot be resolved from available sources.
- No current DSLD record for ExtenZe was located, so the 9 mg yohimbine-alkaloid figure comes from a 2011 label that went off market in 2022; the current yohimbe amount is undisclosed.
- No human dose-ranging, upper-limit or toxicity data were found for Epimedium, muira puama, catuaba, Butea superba or Swedish flower pollen, so those totals cannot be judged against anything.

## Q2. Which ingredient carries the highest interaction or toxicity risk, and with which common medications?

### Takeaway
Yohimbe/yohimbine (ExtenZe) is clearly the highest-risk ingredient: NCCIH ties it to arrhythmia, blood-pressure problems, heart attacks and seizures, MSKCC lists it among the supplements with the most documented contraindications and includes six deaths attributed to yohimbine, and its content in supplements is frequently mislabelled. Second tier: DHEA and pregnenolone (hormonal, prostate-cancer caution), pine bark + vitamin E (bleeding with anticoagulants/antiplatelets), and the nitric-oxide precursors with nitrates and antihypertensives.

### Cited Findings

**Yohimbe / yohimbine**
- "Yohimbine, a component of yohimbe, has been associated with cardiac arrhythmia (irregular heartbeat), blood pressure problems, heart attacks, and seizures." "Because of inaccurate labeling and potential for serious side effects, yohimbe supplements have been restricted or banned in many countries." "Yohimbe caused stomach problems, tachycardia…, anxiety, and high blood pressure, according to a study… that compared calls made to the California Poison Control System over a 7-year period. People calling about yohimbe were generally more likely to need medical care than other callers." "Most yohimbe products don't say how much yohimbine they contain. The amount may vary a lot among products, according to a 2015 analysis of 49 brands of supplements labeled as containing yohimbe or yohimbine for sale in the United States." "Do not use yohimbe if you are taking types of antidepressant medications called monoamine oxidase inhibitors or tricyclic antidepressants." — [NCCIH, Yohimbe](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yohimbe)
- "It is illegal in the United States to market an over-the-counter product containing yohimbine as a treatment for erectile dysfunction without getting approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to do so." — [NCCIH, Yohimbe](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yohimbe)
- MSKCC contraindications: "Cardiovascular disease, hypertension, liver disease, kidney disease, PTSD, anxiety, bipolar disorder, depression, mania, schizophrenia, benign prostate hypertrophy, pregnancy." Herb–drug interactions: "Antianxiety agents: Yohimbine may reduce their therapeutic effects. Antidepressants: Yohimbine may augment side effects. Antihypertensives: Yohimbine may diminish their effects. Bupropion: Coingestion with yohimbe products resulted in toxic effects. CYP2D6 substrate drugs: Yohimbine inhibits CYP2D6… Opioids: Yohimbine may induce withdrawal and anxiety symptoms." — [MSKCC About Herbs, Yohimbe](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/yohimbe)
- MSKCC case reports: "Severe poisoning and death: Attributed to quantified yohimbine concentrations in 6 cases"; "Hypertensive urgency: In a 49-year-old man who presented… with palpitations and severely elevated blood pressure that was not responding to treatment"; "Acute neurotoxic effects: Malaise, vomiting, loss of consciousness, and repeated seizures in a 39-year-old bodybuilder following ingestion of yohimbine 5g"; "Severe priapism: In a 42-year-old man… after ingestion of yohimbe extract. Treatment required included a surgical procedure." And: "yohimbe products are among supplements with the largest number of documented contraindications, toxic effects, and severe events requiring hospitalization." — [MSKCC About Herbs, Yohimbe](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/yohimbe)
- Efficacy for context: meta-analysis of seven placebo-controlled trials found "yohimbine is superior to placebo in the treatment of erectile dysfunction (odds ratio 3.85, 95% confidence interval 6.67 to 2.22). Serious adverse reactions were infrequent and reversible." — [Ernst & Pittler 1998, J Urol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9649257/)

**DHEA and pregnenolone**
- "Taking high doses of DHEA or using it for a long time might raise the risk of hormone-sensitive cancers, such as prostate and breast cancers… If you have a type of hormone-sensitive cancer, don't use DHEA." "Consider avoiding use of DHEA if you have high cholesterol or a condition that affects the supply of blood to the heart (ischemic heart disease). DHEA might reduce high-density lipoprotein (HDL)… cholesterol levels." "Use of DHEA also might worsen mental health conditions and raise the risk of mania in people who have mood disorders." Interactions: "Estrogen. Don't use DHEA with estrogen." "Antidepressant drugs. Use of DHEA with certain antidepressants might cause manic symptoms." "Testosterone. Don't use DHEA with testosterone." "Triazolam. Using DHEA with this sedative might raise the effects of this drug, causing excessive sedation and affecting your breathing and heart rate." — [Mayo Clinic, DHEA](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-dhea/art-20364199)
- MSKCC: "DHEA should not be used with tamoxifen as it can lead to tamoxifen resistance." "A case of cancer flare-up was reported in a patient with advanced prostate cancer undergoing DHEA treatment." "In patients receiving androgen depletion treatment for recurrent prostate cancer, increases in serum DHEA and androsterone (AST) were associated with shorter time to castration-resistant prostate cancer." Adverse reactions: "Increased acne… Mania secondary to supplementation with high doses of DHEA." Interactions: "Serum concentrations of DHEA and DHEA-S can be increased by alprazolam, amlodipine, diltiazem, and metformin… decreased by dexamethasone, insulin, and morphine." — [MSKCC About Herbs, Dehydroepiandrosterone](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/dehydroepiandrosterone)
- Pregnenolone: chronic 15–30 mg/day "antagonizes certain acute effects of benzodiazepines and may enhance arousal via antagonist or inverse agonist actions at the benzodiazepine/GABA(A) receptor complex" — pregnenolone-pretreated subjects "showed significantly less sedation following diazepam (p<0.03)" — [Meieran 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14749094/)

**Pine bark / Pycnogenol (bleeding)**
- "Anticoagulant and/or antiplatelet drugs: Pine bark extract can inhibit platelet aggregation and may increase risk of bleeding when used with these drugs." Adverse reactions: "May cause irritability and lower energy levels." — [MSKCC About Herbs, Pine Bark Extract](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/pine-bark-extract)

**Muira puama and catuaba**
- Human efficacy evidence is minimal and mostly in women or in combination products: a 202-woman study of "a unique herbal formulation of Muira puama and Ginkgo biloba (Herbal vX)" — [Waynberg & Brewer 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11186145/); a healthy-volunteer toxicology study of a Paullinia/Trichilia/Ptychopetalum/ginger extract reported "No severe adverse reactions or haematological and biochemical changes" — [Oliveira 2005, Phytother Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15798997/); a review of psychiatric and neurological adverse effects of herbal sexual enhancers covering Ptychopetalum olacoides, Panax ginseng, Lepidium meyenii, Pausinystalia johimbe and Withania somnifera concluded "Most of the selected sexual enhancers appeared to be safe at therapeutic doses, although mild to severe adverse effects may occur in cases of overdosing or self-medication with unstandardized products. Drug interactions are more concerning, considering that herbal aphrodisiacs are likely used together with other plant extracts and/or pharmaceuticals." — [Brunetti 2020, Pharmaceuticals](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7602496/)

### Inferences
- Ranked by the strength and severity of the documented human risk, the stack's danger concentrates in **ExtenZe**: it carries the yohimbe (cardiovascular, seizure, death reports; drug interactions with MAOIs, TCAs, bupropion, antihypertensives, opioids, CYP2D6 substrates), the DHEA/pregnenolone hormonal load with a prostate-cancer caution, *and* the undisclosed amounts that make the risk unquantifiable — plus a documented history of an ExtenZe-labelled product containing undeclared sildenafil.
- The medication classes most likely to matter for a middle-aged male buyer map onto the stack as follows: **nitrates** (arginine/citrulline/Pycnogenol additive vasodilation; yohimbe's own BP effects; and the sildenafil-adulteration precedent) — the highest-consequence pairing; **alpha-blockers such as tamsulosin** (additive hypotension with arginine, per Drugs.com's moderate rating, on top of the PDE5i–alpha-blocker caution); **antihypertensives** (arginine lowers BP; yohimbine may *diminish* antihypertensive effect, i.e. the interaction runs in both directions within one product); **warfarin and antiplatelets** (pine bark ×2–3, vitamin E, ginseng); **metformin/insulin** (arginine and ginseng both lower glucose; metformin raises DHEA-S); **SSRIs/TCAs/MAOIs and bupropion** (yohimbine, ginseng, DHEA → mania/toxicity signals); **finasteride** — no interaction evidence was found, but DHEA and Epimedium are hormonally active and would work against the pharmacological intent of 5-alpha-reductase inhibition, which is a mechanistic caution, not a documented interaction.
- The icariin-as-PDE5-inhibitor story is the most over-sold claim in this category: the only supporting data found are in vitro, so Epimedium should not be assumed to add PDE5 inhibition on top of a prescription PDE5 inhibitor — but neither should it be assumed harmless, given the hormone-sensitive-cancer and cardiac-disease contraindications and the arrhythmia/hypomania case reports.

### Gaps
- No interaction evidence of any kind was found for finasteride with any ingredient in these four products.
- No human interaction studies were found for Epimedium, muira puama, catuaba, Butea superba or Swedish flower pollen with any prescription drug; the CYP3A4 and aromatase statements for Epimedium are explicitly flagged by MSKCC as of unknown clinical relevance.
- Drugs.com states that of 45 known L-arginine drug interactions "1 is major, and 44 are moderate" but the page retrieved did not name the major one.
- No LiverTox page could be retrieved (ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books was unreachable), so the formal hepatotoxicity likelihood score for ashwagandha is not captured here; the case series above stand in its place.

## Q3. What did the arginine + PDE5-inhibitor combination trials actually record, and how does that square with consumer-site interaction warnings?

### Takeaway
The deliberate-combination trials recorded more nuisance side effects with the combination than with either drug alone (Gallo: 67 vs 53 vs 11 adverse events per 100 men) but **no serious adverse events, no reported hypotension, and no dropouts attributable to the combination**; a meta-analysis found no significant difference in adverse events between combination and PDE5i alone. Drugs.com itself rates arginine + sildenafil as **moderate**, with the advice "Monitor", not major — so the strong "major interaction" framing seen on consumer sites is not supported by the trial record, though the biological basis (additive vasodilation) is real and matters most for nitrate users.

### Cited Findings

**Gallo 2020 (L-arginine 2,500 mg + tadalafil 5 mg daily, 12 weeks, 300 men, 3 arms of 100)**
- Design: "Group A received daily L-Arginine 2,500 mg, group B received daily tadalafil 5 mg, and group C received both daily L-Arginine 2,500 mg plus daily tadalafil 5 mg. The duration of therapy in all 3 groups was 12 weeks. Safety was assessed by evaluating all reported treatment-emergent adverse events." — [Gallo 2020, Sex Med (PMC7261690)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)
- Efficacy: "IIEF-EF score increased in group A from 15 ± 7 to 18.1 ± 9.2, in group B from 14.8 ± 6.9 to 20.8 ± 7.3, and in group C from 14.9 ± 7.1 to 22 ± 7.5" (i.e. +3.1, +6.0, +7.1 points) — [Gallo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)
- Adverse events: "We report a total of 11, 53, and 67 cases of adverse events in group A, B, and C respectively… The most common AEs were insomnia in the arginine group with a rate of 5% and dyspepsia in both group B and C reported in the 11% and in the 14% of the survey" (chi-square p<.0001). The AE table lists, for combination vs tadalafil vs arginine: headache 11/8/2, dyspepsia 14/11/2, myalgia 9/8/0, insomnia 7/0/5, flushing 7/5/2, back pain 6/7/0, nasal congestion 6/5/0, dizziness 4/3/0 — [Gallo 2020, Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)
- Tolerability: "All patients completed the treatment except one included in group B"; "it is remarkable that even if the safety profile was lower than in the other 2 groups, all 100 patients included in the combination group completed the treatment." — [Gallo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)
- No blood-pressure measurements are reported in the paper's results or AE table; nitrates were an exclusion ("The following medications were contraindicated during the trial: nitrates, estrogens, antiandrogens, anxiolytic drugs, LH-RH analogs, and tricyclic antidepressants"), as were coronary heart disease, peripheral or cerebrovascular disease and kidney disease — [Gallo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)
- Authors' own safety framing: "arginine was reported to have a very high safety profile because only 2% of patients treated with this supplement experienced AEs, none of them severe. The safety of arginine was demonstrated even at the highest dosage of 8 grams… individuals treated with L-arginine reported a lower incidence of AEs than men who received tadalafil 5 mg and/or combination therapy." — [Gallo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)

**El-Wakeel 2020 (sildenafil 50 mg every other day ± L-arginine 3 g/day, 8 weeks)**
- "Compare the efficacy and safety of oral sildenafil 50 mg alone or in combination with l-arginine 3 g/day orally on the treatment of erectile dysfunction… Efficacy was assessed using International Index of Erectile Function-5 score at the baseline and after 8 weeks. Side effects were evaluated across the two groups."
- "The score was slightly better in combination group in which the average International Index of Erectile Function-5 score was (19.8 ± 2.2) vs. (18.5 ± 2.3) in sildenafil group with p value 0.05. **Side effects were more or less the same between the two groups except for gastritis which was more common on combination group.**" — [El-Wakeel 2020, Andrology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31267684/)

**Xu 2021 meta-analysis (4 RCTs, 373 patients)**
- "Patients who received the combination of L-arginine and PDE5Is showed significant improvement compared to those treated with L-arginine and PDE5Is alone, as assessed by sexual function index (p <0.00001 and p =0.005, respectively) and total testosterone… **However, the combination of L-arginine and PDE5Is had no obvious difference relative to PDE5Is alone in terms of various adverse events (AEs).**… compared with monotherapy, the combination of L-arginine and PDE5Is showed a greater improvement of sexual function and total testosterone, and did not significantly increase the AEs. Besides, PDE5Is alone revealed a better effect than those treated with L-arginine alone." — [Xu 2021, Andrologia](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33587304)

**Other combination trials (partial data only)**
- Abu El-Hamd & Hegazy 2020, single-blind placebo-controlled trial in elderly men: "Oral daily use of L-arginine (5 g), tadalafil (5 mg), combined L-arginine with tadalafil…"; outcomes were "Q1-5, total scores of SHIM and total testosterone" — [PubMed 32441833](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32441833/) (only these fragments were retrievable)
- El Taieb 2019, double-blind RCT in 108 diabetic men with mild-moderate ED: "Daily use of l-arginine with tadalafil significantly increased the International Index of Erectile Function scores and total [testosterone]"; "Erectile functions were significantly improved in all patients after treatment as compared with baseline and placebo (P < .001)" — [PubMed 31326304](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31326304/) (only these fragments were retrievable)
- Adjuvant use with shockwave therapy: "Adjuvant daily therapy with L-arginine 2,500 mg and tadalafil 5 mg was safe and effective in increasing the efficacy and the duration of benefits of Li-ESWT." — [Gallo 2022, Investig Clin Urol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8756145/)

**High-dose arginine monotherapy safety with measured blood pressure**
- Menafra 2022, randomised double-blind placebo-controlled trial, L-ARG 6 g/day for 3 months (n=51 vs 47): "In l-ARG and placebo groups, no significant changes were detected from T0 to T1, regarding clinical and biochemical parameters, except for HDL cholesterol levels, which were significantly increased in l-ARG group (p = 0.001)". Systolic BP 136 ± 17 → 133 ± 12 (NS); diastolic 80 ± 7 → 81 ± 4 (NS); heart rate 73 → 75 (NS). "In l-ARG group, 5.88% of patients experienced adverse events, including gastric pyrosis, urticarial reaction and scrotal itching, none of which was manifested as clinically relevant. In placebo group, 4.26% of patients experienced adverse events" — [Menafra 2022, J Endocrinol Invest (PMC8995264)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8995264/)
- Rhim 2019 meta-analysis of 10 RCTs, 540 men: "arginine supplements with dosage ranging from 1,500 to 5,000 mg significantly improved ED compared with placebo or no treatment (odds ratios, 3.37 [1.29, 8.77], P = .01)… **The adverse effect rate in the arginine-treated group was 8.3%, and that in the placebo group was 2.3%, none of which were severe.**" — [Rhim 2019, J Sex Med](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30770070)

**What the drug-interaction references actually say**

- Interaction count: "There are 45 drugs known to interact with L-Arginine (arginine), along with 1 disease interaction. Of the total drug interactions, 1 is major, and 44 are moderate." - Arginine + tamsulosin is also **moderate**: "Coadministration of L-arginine or L-citrulline may potentiate the hypotensive effects of…" / "Using arginine together with tamsulosin may have additive effects on dilating your blood vessels and lowering blood pressure." — [Drugs.com, L-Arginine with Tamsulosin](https://www.drugs.com/drug-interactions/l-arginine-with-tamsulosin-232-4042-2146-0.html?professional=1)
- Mayo Clinic's arginine interaction list: "Nitrates. Use of this chest pain medication with L-arginine might cause your blood pressure to become too low." "Sildenafil (Revatio, Viagra). Use of this erectile dysfunction medication with L-arginine might cause your blood pressure to become too low." "Blood pressure drugs, herbs and supplements… might increase the risk of blood pressure becoming too low." - Drugs.com monograph: "Nitrates: Caution is warranted… L-arginine may potentiate the effects of isosorbide mononitrate and other nitric oxide donors, such as glyceryl trinitrate (ie, nitroglycerin) and sodium nitroprusside. Insulin: Caution is warranted… effects on insulin are unpredictable." "Due to L-arginine's vasodilatory properties, hypotension may occur." "Nausea, diarrhea, dyspepsia, palpitations, headache, and numbness have been reported." ### Inferences
- The honest reading of the evidence is: **in men without cardiovascular contraindications and not on nitrates, adding ~2.5–3 g/day of arginine to a daily PDE5 inhibitor increased mild side effects (dyspepsia, headache, flushing, insomnia) by roughly 25% in relative terms in the one large three-arm trial, produced no serious events and no dropouts, and bought about 1 extra IIEF-EF point over tadalafil alone (7.1 vs 6.0)** — which is below the 4-point minimal clinically important difference threshold for the domain as a whole (see Q5), i.e. a gain most men would not perceive.
- The "Major interaction" framing on consumer interaction checkers is not what the primary reference databases say for arginine + a PDE5 inhibitor (Drugs.com: moderate/monitor). The framing is better understood as a legitimate warning that becomes genuinely serious in two specific scenarios: (a) the man is on or may need **nitrates**, where arginine potentiates NO donors and where the consequence is the same catastrophic hypotension that makes nitrates an absolute PDE5i contraindication; and (b) the product is **adulterated with an undeclared PDE5 inhibitor**, which the FDA has documented for an ExtenZe-labelled product.
- Every trial that deliberately combined arginine with a PDE5 inhibitor excluded exactly the men for whom the interaction would matter most (nitrate users, coronary/cerebrovascular disease, kidney disease). So "the trials recorded no hypotension" cannot be generalised to a man with cardiovascular disease, and certainly not to a stack that also contains yohimbe.

### Gaps
- None of the combination trials retrieved reported blood-pressure measurements in the combination arms; the only trial with measured BP used arginine monotherapy (6 g/day) and found no change. So there is **no direct human blood-pressure dataset for arginine plus a PDE5 inhibitor**.
- The Stokes 2003 study that Drugs.com cites as the sole reference for the arginine–nitrate/vasodilator interaction was not retrieved, so the magnitude of the pulse-wave/BP effect it reported is not captured here.
- Abu El-Hamd 2020 and El Taieb 2019 abstracts were only partially retrievable; their adverse-event data are not captured.
- No trial was found that combined any of these four *commercial blends* with a PDE5 inhibitor; all combination evidence is for single-ingredient arginine.

## Q4. Is adding extra L-arginine or L-citrulline to Prelox to reach the trial dose pharmacologically reasonable, and does any stack have evidence of additive benefit?

### Takeaway
Yes for Prelox specifically, and for a precise reason: at the label's maintenance dose (1 tablet twice daily) Prelox delivers only about **half** the Pycnogenol and arginine used in every positive Prelox trial, so topping up to 80 mg Pycnogenol + ~2.8–3 g arginine aspartate per day is simply reaching the studied dose — and L-citrulline raises plasma arginine more efficiently than arginine itself. But there is **no evidence that stacking two or more of these commercial blends beats one evidence-dosed ingredient**; no trial of any such combination exists, and the EAU's own appraisal is that these supplements produce improvements patients may not perceive.

### Cited Findings
- Prelox trial doses, all four tablets per day: Ledda 2010 "20 mg of Pycnogenol® plus 700 mg of L-arginine aspartate [per tablet]… two tablets in the morning and two in the evening"; Stanislavov 2008 "80 mg of Pycnogenol® and 3 g of L-arginine aspartate" — [Tian 2023 meta-analysis, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)
- Life Extension's label instructs four tablets daily only "for the first two weeks", then "one (1) tablet twice daily thereafter" — [Life Extension Prelox](https://www.lifeextension.com/vitamins-supplements/item01373/prelox-enhanced-sex)
- The dose-escalation pilot that established the combination: 40 men received "3 ampoules Sargenor a day… (equivalent to 1.7 g L-arginine per day)"; after month 1 on arginine alone "2 patients (5%) experienced a normal erection"; adding 40 mg Pycnogenol twice daily in month 2 "increased the number of men with restored sexual ability to 80%"; increasing to three 40-mg tablets (120 mg/day) in month 3 gave "92.5% of the men experienced a normal erection" — [Stanislavov & Nikolova 2003, J Sex Marital Ther](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12851125/)
- Pycnogenol alone at 120 mg/day for 3 months was also studied in men with ED and raised cholesterol — [OA Text review](https://www.oatext.com/review-influence-of-combinations-of-pycnogenolr-and-l-arginine-aspartate-on-mens-virility-and-fertility.php)
- Citrulline pharmacology: "L-Citrulline dose-dependently increased AUC and C(max) of plasma L-arginine concentration more effectively than L-arginine (P < 0.01). The highest dose of citrulline (3 g bid) increased the C(min) of plasma L-arginine and improved the L-arginine/ADMA ratio from 186 ± 8 to 278 ± 14… urinary nitrate and cGMP were increased… No treatment improved FMD over baseline." Rationale: "L-Arginine treatment is hampered by extensive presystemic elimination due to intestinal arginase activity. In contrast, L-citrulline is readily absorbed and at least in part converted to L-arginine." — [Schwedhelm 2008, Br J Clin Pharmacol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17662090/)
- Review of BP effects: "Both Arg and Cit are effective at increasing plasma Arg. Cit is approximately twice as potent, which is most likely due to a lower first-pass metabolism. The current data suggest that oral Arg supplementation can lower BP by 5.39/2.66 mmHg… The antihypertensive properties of Cit are more questionable, but are likely in the range of 4.1/2.08 to 7.54/3.77 mmHg." — [Khalaf 2019, Nutrients](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6683098/)
- Citrulline in ED, human: "men with mild ED (erection hardness score of 3) received a placebo for 1 month and L-citrulline, 1.5 g/d, for another month. The improvement in the erection hardness score from 3 (mild ED) to 4 (normal erectile function) occurred in 2…" — [Cormio 2011, Urology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21195829/) (abstract truncated in the retrieved snippet)
- Citrulline added to PDE5i therapy: "Oral L-citrulline and Transresveratrol Supplementation Improves Erectile Function in Men With Phosphodiesterase 5 Inhibitors: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Crossover Pilot Study" — [Shirai 2018, Sex Med](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6302103/)
- A Prelox-family product already combines both precursors: "80 mg Pycnogenol, 1.92g L-arginine, 1.2g citrulline and 40mg French oak wood extract (Prelox®R) a day was tested on 50 men with confirmed erectile dysfunction (ED scores 11-17). L-citrulline was added because it increases plasma L-arginine concentrations more effectively than L-arginine itself" — [OA Text review](https://www.oatext.com/review-influence-of-combinations-of-pycnogenolr-and-l-arginine-aspartate-on-mens-virility-and-fertility.php); sperm-quality trial of the same combination — [Stanislavov & Rohdewald 2014, Minerva Urol Nefrol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25531191/)
- Total arginine the literature supports for ED: Rhim's pooled range was "1,500 to 5,000 mg" — [Rhim 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30770070); the arginine + Pycnogenol meta-analysis cites "a daily dose ranging from 2.8 to 8 g, with supplementation schedules from 2 weeks to 6 months… conversely, a very low dose (1.5 g) of l-ARG administered daily for a very short time (17 days) was ineffective" — [Menafra 2022, citing a 10-RCT meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8995264/); 5 g/day for 6 weeks — [Chen 1999, BJU Int](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10233492/); 6 g/day for 3 months — [Menafra 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8995264/); Gallo's authors note safety "even at the highest dosage of 8 grams" — [Gallo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)
- Evidence on whether blends beat single ingredients — the EAU's assessment: "A meta-analysis including 18 trials comparing the effect of antioxidants alone or in combination with PDE5Is versus placebo or PDE5Is alone showed a small but significant positive effect of L-arginine with a mean increase in IIEF-EF score of 2.74 (95% CI: 1.67; 3.81) compared to placebo, but no difference in terms of sexual satisfaction score. Similar results were reported for antioxidants alone including folic acid, myoinositol, vitamin E, L-carnitine, nicotinic acid, pine bark extract, transresveratrol, L-citrulline and ginseng. **This small improvement may not be perceived as significant by patients.**… In another meta-analysis, the combination of PDE5Is plus antioxidants… was associated with better EF improvement compared to PDE5Is alone. Overall, these data suggest that antioxidant supplements and herbal compounds alone or in combination with PDE5Is may have a small positive effect on EF, although further studies are needed to assess the clinical significance of these findings." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of Erectile Dysfunction](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- Herbal-blend evidence quality: of 24 RCTs in 2,080 men with ED, "12 evaluated monopreparations (five ginseng [n = 399], three saffron… two Tribulus terrestris… and one each Pinus pinaster [n = 21] and Lepidium meyenii [n = 50]), seven evaluated formulations (n = 544)… Ginseng significantly improved erectile function (IIEF-5 score… SMD 0.43; 95% CI 0.15-0.70; P < 0.01)… Several herbal formulations were associated with a decrease of IIEF-5 or IIEF-15, although the results were preliminary. The quality of the included studies varied, with only seven having a prevalent low risk of bias." — [Borrelli 2018, Drugs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29633089/)
- Wolfson Brands' own disclaimer on its blend: "studies conducted on individual ingredients do not establish the effects of the complete Performer 8 formula"; "No individual-ingredient study cited in this announcement evaluated the complete Performer 8 formula." — [USA Today press release](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/43980/performer-8-reviews-wolfson-brands-announces-u-s-launch-of-current-11-ingredient-mens-wellness-formula/)
- Leading Edge Health's own disclaimer on ExtenZe: "No independent, peer-reviewed clinical trial evaluating the current retail formula and current serving conditions was identified in the sources reviewed for this guide." The only study in its research history is "a historical U.S. patent filing describing a limited pilot investigation… a group of 20 volunteers receiving one tablet daily for eight weeks… A patent filing, however, is not equivalent to an independently published, peer-reviewed clinical trial." — [USA Today press release, ExtenZe guide](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/45414/extenze-reviews-leading-edge-health-publishes-2026-label-literacy-guide-for-current-mens-wellness-formula/)

### Inferences
- **Topping up Prelox is reasonable and arguably necessary.** The 4-tablets-a-day loading dose matches the trial dose (80 mg Pycnogenol + ~2.8 g arginine aspartate); the label then halves it, taking the user below every dose that produced the published results. A buyer who wants the trial exposure should either stay on four tablets daily (acknowledging ~3× the cost) or stay on two tablets and add arginine or citrulline to make up the arginine shortfall — but that only restores arginine, not Pycnogenol, so the Pycnogenol half of the "synergy" claim is still halved.
- Citrulline is the pharmacologically better top-up per gram (roughly twice as potent at raising plasma arginine, because it escapes intestinal arginase), and Horphag's own Prelox R formulation already took that route (1.92 g arginine + 1.2 g citrulline). A pragmatic target consistent with the literature is **total 3–5 g/day of arginine-equivalent** (arginine 1.5–5 g is the pooled effective range; 2.8–8 g in the broader meta-analysis), achieved either as arginine or with part replaced by citrulline at roughly half the gram amount.
- **There is no evidence of additive benefit from stacking any two of these four products.** Not one trial of any such combination exists; both Wolfson Brands and Leading Edge Health say so about their own products. What stacking reliably adds is: cost, duplicate pine bark/Epimedium/Tribulus/muira puama exposure, an unquantifiable yohimbe and DHEA load if ExtenZe is in the mix, and the iron and (in the new formula) 200 mg caffeine from Performer 8.
- The cleanest comparison the evidence supports is one evidence-dosed single ingredient versus a blend: ginseng at 2.7–3 g/day has the best single-ingredient signal in ED meta-analysis (SMD 0.43), arginine at 1.5–5 g/day has OR 3.37 for improvement, and arginine + Pycnogenol at trial doses has three concordant RCTs — whereas the four commercial products here have, between them, zero published RCTs of the finished formulas.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head trial of a commercial multi-ingredient blend versus an evidence-dosed single ingredient was found.
- The Cormio 2011 citrulline abstract was truncated, so the exact responder numbers (citrulline vs placebo) are not captured here.
- Prelox's actual free-L-arginine content per tablet is not published; the ~55% conversion used in Q1 is inferred from the Aoki trial's arginine:aspartic-acid ratio.

## Q5. How long is a fair trial, how should the effect be measured, and what should prompt stopping?

### Takeaway
Across the Prelox, arginine and ginseng trials, benefit was measured at **4 weeks at the earliest, 8–12 weeks typically, and Prelox kept improving to 6 months** — so a fair trial is 8–12 weeks minimum at the studied dose, re-assessed with the IIEF-5/SHIM or Erection Hardness Score, judged against the 4-point minimal clinically important difference for the IIEF erectile-function domain (2 points in mild ED). Both AUA and EAU make validated questionnaires and follow-up explicit, and EAU points out that non-response to treatment is most often a dosing/education failure rather than true failure.

### Cited Findings

**Onset and duration in the trials**
- Prelox, 6 months: "the erectile domain of the IIEF (questions 1-5 plus 15) improved with Prelox from a baseline mean (sd) score of 15.2 (6.6) to **25.2 (2.1) after 3 months and 27.1 (2.1) after 6 months** of treatment. In the placebo group there was an increase from a baseline score of 15.1 (7.0) to 19.1 (3.0) and 19.0 (3.1) after 3 and 6 months… This study shows that Prelox is effective for improving erectile function, and that this effect persists on continuous therapy for up to 6 months." Also "total plasma testosterone levels increased significantly from 15.9 (2.3) to 18.9 (2.6) nmol/L (P < 0.05) after 6 months" — [Ledda 2010, BJU Int](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20184576/)
- Prelox, 1 month per crossover period: "50 patients with mild to moderate erectile dysfunction (ED) were treated for 1 month with placebo or a combination of L-arginine aspartate and Pycnogenol (Prelox)… Intake of Pycnogenol for 1 month restored erectile function to normal. Intercourse frequency doubled. e-NOS in spermatozoa and testosterone levels in blood increased significantly. **Cholesterol levels and blood pressure were lowered. No unwanted effects were reported.**" — [Stanislavov 2008, Int J Impot Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17703218/)
- Prelox-type supplement, 8 weeks: "Subjects were instructed to take a supplement (Pycnogenol® 60 mg/day, L-arginine 690 mg/day and aspartic acid 552 mg/day) or an identical placebo for 8 weeks… **Eight weeks of supplement intake improved the total score of the IIEF-5.** In particular, a marked improvement was observed in 'hardness of erection' and 'satisfaction with sexual intercourse'. **No adverse reactions were observed during the study period.**" — [Aoki 2012, Phytother Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21618639/)
- Month-by-month onset in the original pilot: arginine alone for 1 month → 5% normal erections; + Pycnogenol 80 mg/day in month 2 → 80%; + 120 mg/day in month 3 → 92.5% — [Stanislavov & Nikolova 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12851125/)
- The three Prelox RCTs in the meta-analysis ran 1 month, 2 months and 6 months, and "No adverse events were reported in any of the three studies." — [Tian 2023, Front Endocrinol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)
- Arginine monotherapy: 6 g/day for **3 months** (mean treatment duration 89.9 days), IIEF-6 20 → 24 (p<0.0001), "74% of patients improved ED degree category, although only 24% of patients… reached IIEF-6 scores compatible with absence of ED" — [Menafra 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8995264/); 5 g/day for **6 weeks** — [Chen 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10233492/); the pooled range was "2 weeks to 6 months" with results "consistent across different dosage and duration", while "a very low dose (1.5 g) of l-ARG administered daily for a very short time (17 days) was ineffective" — [Menafra 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8995264/)
- Arginine + tadalafil: 12 weeks — [Gallo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/); arginine + sildenafil: 8 weeks — [El-Wakeel 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31267684/)
- Korean red ginseng: "double-blind, placebo controlled, crossover study (**8 weeks on treatment, 2 weeks of washout and 8 weeks on treatment**)… The ginseng dose was 900 mg. 3 times daily… In response to the global efficacy question 60% of the patients answered that Korean red ginseng improved erection (p <0.01). Among other variables penile tip rigidity on RigiScan showed significant improvement" — [Hong 2002, J Urol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12394711/)
- Korean red ginseng, **12 weeks**: 60 men, "either 1,000 mg (3 times daily) of KRG or a placebo… IIEF-5 score after the treatment was significantly higher in the KRG group… (from 16.4 ± 2.9 to 21.0 ± 6.3, P < 0.0001)… 20 patients (66.6%) reported improved erection… Scores on questions 2 (rigidity), 3 (penetration), 4 and 5 (maintenance) were significantly higher for KRG… after 12 weeks of each treatment" — [de Andrade 2007, Asian J Androl](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16855773/)
- Ginseng trial durations and adverse effects across the evidence base: 4, 8 and 12 weeks; reported adverse effects were "None", "Gastric upset (RG: 1; P: 1)", "Headache, insomnia (RG:3)", "Constipation: (RG, 2) Gastric upset (RG, 2; P, 3)"; pooled risk ratio 2.40 (95% CI 1.65–3.51), but "the total number of RCTs included in the analysis, the total sample size and the methodological quality of the primary studies were too low to draw definitive conclusions" — [Jang 2008, Br J Clin Pharmacol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2561113/)
- Maca: 12 weeks — [Shin 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36593713/). Pine bark extract (as a sperm/fertility intervention): 90 days — [OA Text review](https://www.oatext.com/review-influence-of-combinations-of-pycnogenolr-and-l-arginine-aspartate-on-mens-virility-and-fertility.php). Semenax's own marketing claims "Beneficial effects have been found to kick in within 90 days of supplementation" for pine bark — [Semenax ingredients](https://www.semenax.com/ingredients/)

**How to measure, and what counts as a real change**
- AUA Guideline Statement 2: "For the man with ED, **validated questionnaires are recommended to assess the severity of ED, to measure treatment effectiveness, and to guide future management.**" Statement 1: "Men presenting with symptoms of ED should undergo a thorough medical, sexual, and psychosocial history; a physical examination; and selective laboratory testing." Statement 10: "For men who are prescribed PDE5i, the dose should be titrated to provide optimal efficacy." — [AUA Erectile Dysfunction Guideline (2018)](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/erectile-dysfunction-(ed)-guideline)
- EAU: "Validated psychometric questionnaires (e.g. IIEF, EHS) are reliable tools to assess ED severity." And: "**Follow-up is important to assess efficacy and safety of the treatment provided.** It is also essential to assess patient satisfaction, as successful treatment for ED goes beyond efficacy and safety." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of Erectile Dysfunction](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- IIEF-5/SHIM severity bands: "ED was classified into five severity levels, ranging from none (22-25) through severe (5-7)"; cut-off score of 21 discriminated best (sensitivity 0.98, specificity 0.88) — [Rosen 1999, Int J Impot Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10637462/); the full banding as used clinically: "22–25 No ED; 17–21 Mild ED; 12–16 Mild-to-moderate ED; 8–11 [moderate]; 5–7 [severe]" — [Kaiser Permanente SHIM form](https://mydoctor.kaiserpermanente.org/ncal/Images/Sexual%20Inventory%20for%20Men_tcm75-217040.pdf); also [BAUS SHIM leaflet](https://www.baus.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/Patients/Leaflets/SHIM.pdf)
- Minimal clinically important difference: "The ROC-based MCID for the EF domain was **4**, with estimated sensitivity and specificity of 0.74 and 0.73… MCIDs varied significantly (p<0.0001) according to baseline ED severity (**mild: 2; moderate: 5; severe: 7**)", derived from 17 tadalafil RCTs in 3,345 patients treated for 12 weeks — [Rosen 2011, Eur Urol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21855209/)
- Erection Hardness Score: "The Erection Hardness Score (EHS) is a single-item, patient-reported outcome (PRO) for scoring erection hardness" — [Mulhall 2007, J Sex Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17888069/); it is "a one-item questionnaire that assesses rigidity on a 4-point scale" — [García-Cruz 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21091886/); used as the primary endpoint in the citrulline trial, where success was defined as moving "from 3 (mild ED) to 4 (normal erectile function)" — [Cormio 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21195829/)
- Non-response is usually not treatment failure: "The management of non-responders depends upon identifying the underlying cause. Clinicians should begin by ensuring that the medication has been properly prescribed, is being correctly used by the patient and that the patient has been using a licensed medication. Timing is important, and patients may be waiting either too short or too long after the medication before attempting sexual intercourse. Studies suggest that patient education can help salvage an apparent non-responder to a PDE5I… After emphasising the importance of dose, timing and sexual stimulation to the patient, EF can be effectively restored following re-administration of the relevant PDE5I." EAU recommendation: "Fully inform patients of the mechanism of action and the ways in which PDE5Is should be taken, as incorrect use/inadequate information is the main causes of a lack of response to PDE5Is." — [EAU Guidelines](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- For reference on how many attempts a drug trial needs: "approximately 80% of patients achieved successful sexual intercourse within eight attempts at 10 mg and within four attempts at 20 mg"; and the PDE5i-nonresponder definition used in shockwave trials was "sildenafil (100 mg), tadalafil (20 mg), or vardenafil (20 mg) with a trial of at least four attempts, or tadalafil (5 mg) with a trial of at least 28 days" — [Practical Approaches to Treat ED in PDE5i Nonresponders](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7505261/)
- EAU's actual recommendation on these supplements: "**Use supplements with L-arginine or ginseng daily in men with mild ED who refuse pharmacological treatment after counselling them that the improvement of EF could be mild.** (Weak)" — [EAU Guidelines](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)

### Inferences
- **A fair trial is 8–12 weeks at the dose actually studied**, measured with the same instrument before and after. One month is enough only for Prelox at the full four-tablet dose (two trials measured at 1 month), 8 weeks is the shortest duration that produced results for both the Pycnogenol/arginine supplement and Korean red ginseng, and 12 weeks is the modal duration (Gallo, de Andrade, Menafra, Shin, Ledda's first endpoint). Ledda's 3-month → 6-month gain means a 3-month null result is not necessarily final for Prelox, but a 3-month null result at a *half* dose tells the buyer nothing at all.
- **The bar for "it worked" should be the MCID, not any change**: +4 points on the IIEF erectile-function domain overall, +2 if baseline ED is mild, +5 if moderate. This is the single most useful number for a buyer, because it exposes how small the supplement effects are — the EAU's pooled arginine effect is +2.74 points, i.e. clears the bar only for mild ED, and Gallo's arginine-alone gain was +3.1 overall (but +5.4 in mild ED and +0.1 in severe ED). Practically: record a SHIM/IIEF-5 score and an EHS grade before starting, repeat at 8 and 12 weeks, and treat anything short of the MCID as a negative result.
- **What should prompt stopping**, drawn from the sources above rather than from any single guideline statement: any chest pain, palpitations, rapid or irregular heartbeat, fainting or severe dizziness (yohimbe arrhythmia/hypertensive urgency; Epimedium tachyarrhythmia case; ExtenZe's own label says to stop and call a physician for "rapid heartbeat, dizziness, blurred vision"); jaundice, dark urine, itching or right-upper-quadrant pain (ashwagandha cholestatic liver injury, latency 2–12 weeks — exactly the window of a fair trial); unusual bruising or bleeding (pine bark ×2–3 plus vitamin E); new agitation, insomnia, mania or psychosis (yohimbine, ginseng + MAOI, DHEA, Epimedium case reports); acne, breast tenderness or mood change (DHEA); a rise in PSA or any new prostate symptom (DHEA, Epimedium); and persistent GI upset (arginine ≥9 g, zinc, iron).
- Severe ED is the clearest futility signal: arginine moved severe-ED IIEF-EF by +0.1 points and yielded 0% successful intercourse, versus +4.9 for tadalafil — so a man with severe ED should not spend 12 weeks on supplements.

### Gaps
- Neither AUA nor EAU states an explicit number of weeks after which an ED treatment should be declared a failure; the AUA page retrieved contains dose-titration and questionnaire statements but no follow-up interval, and the EAU follow-up section says only that follow-up is important. The 8–12 week figure here is derived from trial durations, not from a guideline.
- No guideline or trial was found that specifies a stopping rule for any of these four commercial products; the stopping triggers above are assembled from per-ingredient safety sources.

## Q6. Who must not take these at all?

### Takeaway
Six groups are excluded by the sources themselves: nitrate users (and anyone on a guanylate-cyclase stimulator such as riociguat); men within 6 months of a myocardial infarction, stroke or life-threatening arrhythmia; men with uncontrolled hypertension or resting hypotension, or unstable angina; men on anticoagulants/antiplatelets or with a bleeding disorder; men with hormone-sensitive cancer (particularly prostate) or on tamoxifen/aromatase inhibitors; and men with new, unexplained ED who have not yet been evaluated — because ED is a cardiovascular risk marker.

### Cited Findings
- Nitrates, absolute: "VIAGRA was shown to potentiate the hypotensive effects of nitrates, and its administration to patients who are using nitric oxide donors such as organic nitrates or organic nitrites in any form either regularly and/or intermittently is therefore contraindicated. After patients have taken VIAGRA, it is unknown when nitrates, if necessary, can be safely administered." "Do not use VIAGRA in patients who are using a GC stimulator, such as riociguat." — [Viagra prescribing information](https://www.drugs.com/pro/viagra.html)
- The same product label's cardiovascular exclusions, which define the group for whom any vasoactive stack is unwise: "Patients who have suffered a myocardial infarction, stroke, or life-threatening arrhythmia within the last 6 months; Patients with resting hypotension (BP <90/50 mmHg) or hypertension (BP >170/110 mmHg); Patients with cardiac failure or coronary artery disease causing unstable angina." Plus: "Patients should be stable on alpha-blocker therapy prior to initiating a PDE5 inhibitor. In those patients who are stable on alpha-blocker therapy, PDE5 inhibitors should be initiated at the lowest dose." And: "VIAGRA has systemic vasodilatory properties and may further lower blood pressure in patients taking anti-hypertensive medications." — [Viagra prescribing information](https://www.drugs.com/pro/viagra.html)
- Recent MI and arginine specifically: "L-arginine isn't recommended for people who have had a recent heart attack due to concerns that the supplement might increase the risk of death." — [Mayo Clinic, L-arginine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-l-arginine/art-20364681). The trial behind this: "In a randomized clinical trial designed to evaluate the effect of oral L-arginine 9 g/day supplementation for 6 months following an acute ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction, no significant change in vascular stiffness or left ejection fraction was observed. However, 8.6% of patients in the L-arginine group died, while no deaths occurred in the control group (P=0.01), and the trial was terminated. Researchers concluded that L-arginine should not be given to patients following an acute myocardial infarction" — [Drugs.com L-arginine monograph](https://www.drugs.com/npp/l-arginine.html); primary report: "6 participants (8.6%) in the L-arginine group died during the 6-month study period vs none in the placebo group (P = .01)" — [Schulman 2006, VINTAGE MI, JAMA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16391217/)
- Uncontrolled hypertension / heart disease and yohimbe: "Do not use if you have high blood pressure, heart disease, arrhythmias, Parkinson's disease, seizures, kidney, thyroid, or liver disease, sexual organ inflammatory disorders, ulcers, or psychiatric disorders." "You have high blood pressure: Yohimbine can raise blood pressure so taking it may compound the problem." "You have an enlarged prostate or sex organ inflammation: Yohimbine may make your condition worse." — [MSKCC, Yohimbe](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/yohimbe)
- Bleeding risk: "Anticoagulant and/or antiplatelet drugs: Pine bark extract can inhibit platelet aggregation and may increase risk of bleeding when used with these drugs." — [MSKCC, Pine Bark Extract](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/pine-bark-extract); "Anticoagulants and anti-platelet drugs, herbs and supplements… Taking L-arginine with them might increase the risk of bleeding." — [Mayo Clinic, L-arginine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-l-arginine/art-20364681); "Asian ginseng may increase your risk of bleeding" and "Asian ginseng may worsen autoimmune disorders and interfere with blood clotting" — [MSKCC, Ginseng (Asian)](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/ginseng-asian), [NCCIH, Asian Ginseng](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng)
- Hormone-sensitive cancer: "If you have a type of hormone-sensitive cancer, don't use DHEA." — [Mayo Clinic, DHEA](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-dhea/art-20364199); "Epimedium may affect hormone levels, and should be avoided in patients with hormone-sensitive cancers or those with heart disease." — [MSKCC, Epimedium](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/epimedium); "DHEA should not be used with tamoxifen as it can lead to tamoxifen resistance" — [MSKCC, DHEA](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/dehydroepiandrosterone); ExtenZe's own archived label: "Consult a physician… if you have, or have a family history of prostate cancer, prostate enlargement, heart disease, low 'good' cholesterol (HDL)" — [DSLD label 1968](https://dsld.od.nih.gov/label/1968)
- New or unevaluated ED: "Men should be counseled that **ED is a risk marker for underlying cardiovascular disease (CVD)** and other health conditions that may warrant evaluation and treatment." and "Men presenting with symptoms of ED should undergo a thorough medical, sexual, and psychosocial history; a physical examination; and selective laboratory testing." — [AUA ED Guideline (2018)](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/erectile-dysfunction-(ed)-guideline)
- Diabetes drugs: "L-arginine might decrease blood sugar levels in people with diabetes. If you're taking diabetes drugs, herbs or supplements, your dosage might need to be adjusted." — [Mayo Clinic, L-arginine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-l-arginine/art-20364681); "In humans, P. ginseng may increase the hypoglycemic effect of insulin and sulfonylureas." — [MSKCC, Ginseng (Asian)](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/ginseng-asian)
- Antidepressants: "Do not use yohimbe if you are taking types of antidepressant medications called monoamine oxidase inhibitors or tricyclic antidepressants." — [NCCIH, Yohimbe](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yohimbe); "Use of DHEA with certain antidepressants might cause manic symptoms." — [Mayo Clinic, DHEA](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-dhea/art-20364199)
- Potassium-sparing diuretics: "Don't take L-arginine with amiloride (Midamor), spironolactone (Aldactone, Carospir) or triamterene (Dyrenium)… increasing the risk of developing a higher than normal level of potassium in your blood (hyperkalemia)." — [Mayo Clinic, L-arginine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-l-arginine/art-20364681)
- Other arginine cautions: "L-arginine can worsen allergies or asthma. Use the supplement with caution if you have these conditions." — [Mayo Clinic, L-arginine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-l-arginine/art-20364681)
- Hemochromatosis and iron-containing products: "People with this disorder should avoid using iron supplements and vitamin C supplements." — [NIH ODS Iron](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-Consumer/)
- Athletes: DHEA "is prohibited in athletic competition by the NCAA and World Anti-Doping Agency" — [USA Today/Leading Edge Health ExtenZe guide](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/45414/extenze-reviews-leading-edge-health-publishes-2026-label-literacy-guide-for-current-mens-wellness-formula/)

### Inferences
- The absolute exclusions for this stack track the PDE5-inhibitor label almost exactly, which is logical: the products act, or claim to act, through the same NO/cGMP vasodilatory pathway, and in at least one documented case an ExtenZe-labelled product *contained* a PDE5 inhibitor. A nitrate user who assumes a "natural" supplement is safer than Viagra has the risk exactly backwards, because the supplement's contents are not verified.
- Because ExtenZe combines an alpha-2 antagonist (yohimbe, which raises BP and can blunt antihypertensives) with vasodilators (L-arginine, Epimedium, Cnidium), its net haemodynamic effect in any given man is unpredictable — and unquantifiable, since the amounts are hidden. That is a sufficient reason on its own for anyone with treated hypertension, arrhythmia or coronary disease to avoid it entirely.
- The single highest-value action for a buyer with new ED is not choosing between these products but getting the cardiovascular and endocrine work-up AUA calls for first: ED precedes cardiovascular events, and spending 12 weeks on a blend is 12 weeks of an unexamined risk marker.
