# ExtenZe (Biotab / Global Product Management / CB Distributors) and Semenax (Leading Edge Health): product-level fact file

All prices below were captured on 7–8 November 2026 unless a different date is stated. Label data for ExtenZe comes from the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database (DSLD) because the brand site publishes Supplement Facts only as images; Semenax label data comes from DSLD and the brand's posted Health Canada licence.

## What is in a daily dose of each ExtenZe variant, which variants still contain yohimbe and DHEA, and what does the label say about nitrates or heart medication?

### Takeaway
Every current ExtenZe tablet/gelcap label on file (The Original 2023, ExtenZe 2022, Extended Release 2024, Testosterone Boost 2025) lists yohimbe bark extract plus DHEA and pregnenolone inside proprietary blends whose per-ingredient amounts are not disclosed; the one exception is a 2021 Extended Release 30-softgel label that lists pregnenolone and yohimbe but no DHEA. The directed dose is one tablet/gelcap daily; labels warn about heart disease, blood pressure, prostate conditions and antidepressants but never mention nitrates — the nitrate warning appears only in FDA's sildenafil notices.

### Inferences
- At the directed one-tablet dose, a user of The Original gets 25 mg zinc oxide-zinc, up to 60 mg combined DHEA + pregnenolone, and an undisclosed yohimbe extract dose inside a 468–600 mg blend; the 2014 Biotab label's declared 50 mg DHEA, 10 mg pregnenolone and 12.5 mg yohimbine alkaloids per tablet is the only quantitative benchmark ever printed, and the unchanged 60 mg blend weight suggests the hormone content may be similar today, but this cannot be confirmed from current labels.
- The Extended Release line is inconsistent across label versions: the 2021 30-softgel label omits DHEA while the 2024 15-gelcap label lists DHEA twice (in both blends) and adds isolated yohimbine HCl, so "Maximum Strength" gelcaps are the variant most likely to deliver pharmacologically meaningful yohimbine.
- Despite yohimbine's known blood-pressure and heart-rate effects, no ExtenZe label addresses nitrate drugs; the only nitrate language associated with ExtenZe is FDA's warning about undeclared sildenafil (see below).

### Gaps
- ExtenZe Plus and the Cherry Liquid Shot are not in the DSLD, and the brand's Supplement Facts images could not be downloaded through this sandbox, so their per-serving blend weights and complete ingredient order are unverified; Health Canada's 2013 advisory confirms only that Plus lists DHEA, pregnenolone and yohimbine.
- Current per-tablet amounts of DHEA, pregnenolone and yohimbine alkaloids are not disclosed by the manufacturer anywhere I could find.
- The "Original Advanced Men's Formula" and "Advanced Men's Formula 15ct Softgel" names seen on Amazon were not matched to a DSLD label, so whether they differ from The Original/Extended Release labels above is unknown.

## Does ExtenZe have any placebo-controlled trial of its own formula, what does FDA's 2018 notification say verbatim, and has the formula, manufacturer or owner changed since?

### Takeaway
No published clinical trial of any ExtenZe formula exists in the biomedical literature (a Europe PMC search returns only case reports); the only "study" ever cited is an unpublished marketing document. FDA's 31 Aug 2018 notices (one for "Extenze Nutritional Supplement", one for "Extenze Plus, LOT H061084; EXP 12/2020") say the products, found in international mail shipments, contained sildenafil, without stating whether they were genuine or counterfeit. Since then labels have been re-issued under a new Monrovia distributor name (Global Product Management, Inc.), the trademark has passed from Biotab to an individual and then to Dish Direct, Inc., and the brand website now says CB Distributors, Inc. owns ExtenZe.

### Cited Findings

This product was identified during an examination of international mail shipments. FDA laboratory analysis confirmed that Extenze Nutritional Supplement contains sildenafil, the active ingredient in the FDA-approved prescription drug Viagra, used to treat erectile dysfunction. This undeclared ingredient may interact with nitrates found in some prescription drugs, such as nitroglycerin, and may lower blood pressure to dangerous levels. People with diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, or heart disease often take nitrates." No lot number is given in this notice — [FDA Public Notification: Extenze Nutritional Supplement](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/public-notification-extenze-nutritional-supplement-contains-hidden-drug-ingredient)

- Whether the 2018 sildenafil finding involved genuine or counterfeit ExtenZe is genuinely unresolved: FDA's two 2018 notices, unlike its 2011 and 2013 notices, do not use the word counterfeit and name a specific Plus lot, but the "international mail shipments" source and the brand's long counterfeit history leave both readings open; no public statement from Biotab or its successors about the 2018 notices was found.
- The brand has been restructured several times (Biotab → trademark to an individual → Dish Direct/Maximizer → labels under Global Product Management → website operated by CB Distributors), all keeping the same Monrovia/Pasadena phone number and web domain, which suggests continuity of the same operators under new corporate names rather than an arm's-length acquisition; this is an inference, not a documented fact.
- A buyer cannot assume the current formula matches anything ever studied, because no study exists and the proprietary blend composition has changed at least twice since 2014.

### Gaps
- No public corporate filing, press release or assignment record was found confirming when or how CB Distributors/CB Global Marketing acquired ExtenZe from Dish Direct, nor the relationship between Dish Direct, Global Product Management and Biotab beyond the shared address/phone data.
- No FDA follow-up (warning letter, import alert or recall) tied to the 2018 notices was found; FDA's Health Fraud Product Database was not directly queried because the sandbox could not reach fda.gov interactively.

## What is ExtenZe's complete regulatory, legal and published-harm record, and what is the regulatory status of its yohimbe, DHEA and pregnenolone?

### Takeaway
ExtenZe's record includes a 2006 $300,000 Orange County DA settlement (Dish Direct dba Maximizer Health Products; false 27% enlargement claims; lead), a 2010 class settlement (Williams v. Biotab; $6 million cash plus $6 million in NASCAR merchandise), a July 2011 $1.75 million OCDA settlement against Biotab for violating the 2006 injunction (Prop 65 lead, refund failures, unordered shipments), counterfeit-related FDA alerts in 2011 and 2013, two FDA sildenafil notices in 2018, Health Canada seizures in 2010 and 2013, and at least four published case reports of harm (autoimmune hepatitis, priapism ×2, reverse takotsubo cardiomyopathy). DHEA is a legal U.S. supplement ingredient only because it was exempted from the 2004 Anabolic Steroid Control Act (it is a controlled drug in Canada and banned in sport); yohimbine is prescription-only in Canada and Australia and cannot legally be marketed OTC for ED in the U.S.

### Cited Findings
- 2006: OCDA (24 Jul 2006) — "Dish Direct, Inc., operating under the name Maximizer Health Products, Inc., has agreed to pay $300,000 in civil penalties related to making guarantees about ExtenZe ... claims that their product can provide up to a 27% penile enlargement ... An additional basis of the settlement includes the elimination of lead from the pills, which were originally higher in lead content than is legally permitted. ### Inferences
- Two of the four published harm cases (takotsubo cardiomyopathy, priapism) are mechanistically consistent with yohimbine's alpha-2-blocking effects, and the hepatitis case with hormone/herbal exposure; the hepatitis report has confounders (heavy binge drinking, CT suggestion of pre-existing cirrhosis), so all remain associations, not proof of causation.
- Taking an ExtenZe tablet plus a Testosterone Boost tablet (both 25 mg zinc) would reach 50 mg zinc/day, above the UL; a single product does not.
- Because the formula contains ingredients that are prescription-only or controlled in Canada and Australia, ExtenZe is lawful to sell in the U.S. mainly because of DSHEA and the 2004 DHEA carve-out, not because any regulator has reviewed its safety.

## What is Semenax's full ingredient list with per-serving amounts, how do its zinc and hormone-active ingredients rate, and which ingredients have human evidence for semen volume versus erectile function?

### Takeaway
The current Semenax label (DSLD, entered Aug 2025) is a 4-capsule daily serving containing 16 botanical/amino-acid actives totalling about 2,980 mg plus 60 IU vitamin E and 30 mg zinc aspartate (20% elemental = 6 mg zinc), with no zinc oxide despite the marketing copy; zinc exposure is far below the 40 mg UL and no DHEA/pregnenolone-type hormones are present, though Butea superba (500 mg) has a published hyperandrogenemia case. - The marketing roster is not the label: the brand's ingredients page lists 16 headings (counting "Zinc Oxide & Zinc Aspartate" as one) and omits Epimedium and Tribulus, both of which appear on the regulated label (150 mg and 50 mg per serving); the label and the Canadian licence show a single zinc source (aspartate) and no zinc oxide. - Hormone risk is low compared with ExtenZe (no steroid hormones), but Butea superba at 500 mg/day is the one ingredient with a documented androgen-raising case and is the largest component.

- Swedish flower pollen (Cernilton-type extracts), catuaba, epimedium, hawthorn, cranberry, avena and sarsaparilla were not individually searched for human semen-volume trials; I found no such trials in the searches run, but absence here is not proof of absence.
- Full texts of the maca and zinc meta-analyses were not read; their direction of effect on ejaculate volume specifically is not confirmed in these notes.

## What were the design, results, funding and publication status of the Semenax study?

### Takeaway
The study was a six-site Indian trial (June 2011–March 2012) run by the contract research organisation Vedic Lifesciences for sponsor Leading Edge Marketing (Nassau, Bahamas), randomising 78 men aged 30–60 with erectile dysfunction and perceived low ejaculate to 4 capsules twice daily or placebo for two months; in the 63 per-protocol completers ejaculate volume rose 0.49 mL versus a 0.21 mL fall on placebo and 50% versus 16% achieved a ≥20% increase, while sperm parameters, IIEF scores and orgasm-intensity responder rates did not differ between arms. It was unpublished for a decade, then appeared in May 2022 in an iMedPub (OMICS) journal with the company's COO as first author and company funding.

### Inferences
- The "20% increase" headline is a responder threshold (share of men with ≥20% gain), not a mean 20% rise; the mean gain was about 0.5 mL (roughly 20% of a 2.5 mL baseline) and was significant only because the placebo arm's volume fell.
- The trial population (men with ED and self-perceived low ejaculate, in India, recruited through Ayurvedic clinics) and its exploratory, arbitrarily sized design limit generalisation; "no reported side effects" in marketing contradicts the report's 15 AEs.
- Whether a buyer following today's 4-capsule label gets the trial dose is unclear: the CSR presents the amounts as the content of 8 capsules (the trial's daily intake), whereas the current label prints the same amounts for 4 capsules; either the capsules were reformulated to be twice as concentrated or the label dose is half the trial dose. The paper's wording cannot resolve this.
- Publication a decade later in an OMICS-group journal with a one-month turnaround and the sponsor's COO as first author means the "peer reviewed" label should be treated as nominal.

### Gaps
- The CTRI registration record (CTRI/2011/09/001995) was not retrieved to check pre-specified endpoints.
- Pages 41–69 of the CSR (safety listings, descriptive statistics) were not extracted; adverse-event detail is taken from the synopsis and the 2022 paper.

### Inferences
- Semenax's legal record is far cleaner than ExtenZe's, but its evidence and quality assurances are all company-generated (self-funded trial, self-commissioned lab screen, self-hosted licence), so a buyer comparing "third-party tested" claims should treat both brands as uncertified; Semenax at least has a documented adulterant screen and a Canadian licence, ExtenZe has neither.
- The review ecosystem for Semenax is monetised end-to-end (paid Trustpilot subscription, affiliate newswire "reviews", commission-earning review sites with vendor coupon codes), so aggregate scores overstate independent satisfaction; the 18% one-star share on Walmart and the Trustpilot "no results" complaints are the more informative signal.
