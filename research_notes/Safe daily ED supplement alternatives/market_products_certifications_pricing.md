# Market products, certifications and pricing: daily supplements marketed for erectile function (US focus, with UK/EU/Gulf notes)

Research date: 2026-11-07. Unless a different date is stated, every price below was read from the retailer or brand page on 2026-11-07. "Rx telehealth" products (Hims, Ro, BlueChew) are prescription PDE5 inhibitors, not supplements, and are listed only as a cost benchmark. Access limitations: the session network blocked direct fetches of consumerlab.com, fda.gov, lifeextension.com, thorne.com, hims.com and others, so those pages were read through a remote scraper; ConsumerLab's members-only Sexual Enhancers review and iHerb product pages could not be opened; verywellhealth.com and menshealth.com block the crawler entirely.

## Key question 1: Which specific products carry a verifiable third-party certification (and which one), and what do they cost per month?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of products in this space carry a real, searchable third-party certification: Kaged Citrulline, NOW Sports Arginine & Citrulline and Podium L-Citrulline appear on Informed Sport's certified list; Momentous Tongkat Ali is Informed Sport-certified per batch; MST Testofen is BSCG Certified Drug Free; and ConsumerLab has approved (unnamed in the free preview) ginseng and L-arginine products. No single-ingredient L-citrulline or L-arginine product appears in the NSF Certified for Sport directory (the only arginine hit is a Pure Power endurance drink mix), none carries the USP Verified Mark, and the heavily marketed "male enhancement" blends (VigRX Plus, Male Extra, Performer 8, Max Performer, ExtenZe, Semenax, Boostaro, EndoPeak, Alpha Tonic) offer only cGMP/"clinically tested"/"doctor recommended" marketing language. Evidence-aligned single ingredients cost roughly $5–25/month; certified or branded-extract products $15–45/month; the marketed blends $30–70/month; prescription daily tadalafil via telehealth $24–89/month.
