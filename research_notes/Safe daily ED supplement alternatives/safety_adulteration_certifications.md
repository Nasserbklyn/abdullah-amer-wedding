# Safety Landscape of OTC "Male Enhancement" / Sexual-Performance Supplements: Hidden-Drug Adulteration, Ingredient Safety, and Third-Party Certification (state of warnings as of November 2026)

Research notes compiled 2026-11-07. All FDA page counts and dates were read directly from fda.gov on that date. Earlier events are dated in-line.

## Key Question 1: How large is the adulteration problem, and what is its pattern? (FDA database counts, proportion of warnings, 2023–2026 notifications with product names and hidden drugs, marketplace/gas-station enforcement)

### Takeaway
FDA's "Sexual Enhancement and Energy Product Notifications" database held 441 entries on 2026-11-07 (most recent dated 10/15/2026) versus 228 weight-loss, 66 pain/arthritis and 10 "other" notifications, so sexual-enhancement/energy products account for roughly 59% (441/745) of FDA's categorized hidden-ingredient notifications; FDA itself states the list "covers only a small fraction of the contaminated products on the market." The dominant pattern (2007 to 2026) is undeclared sildenafil and/or tadalafil, often both together, increasingly in "honey packs," coffees and candies sold on eBay/Amazon and in gas stations, frequently combined with a second hidden drug (acetaminophen, diclofenac, sibutramine, dapoxetine, phenolphthalein).

It adds: "This list covers only a small fraction of the contaminated products on the market. If a product is not on the list, that does not mean it is safe." — [FDA Sexual Enhancement and Energy Product Notifications](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/sexual-enhancement-and-energy-product-notifications)

### Inferences
- The FDA list is best read as a lower bound on prevalence and a catalog of recurring patterns (brand names with huge numbers like "500K/700000/25000," "Rhino," "honey," "coffee," "candy," eBay/Amazon/gas-station channels) rather than a complete blocklist, because FDA says it cannot test products pre-market and that the list covers only a "small fraction."
- The 45.5% share of adulterated-supplement warnings in 2007–2016 (Tucker) appears to have risen to roughly 59% of FDA's categorized hidden-ingredient notifications by late 2026, consistent with FDA's own statement that these products "are likely to be contaminated."
- Repeat adulteration after warnings (28 products in Tucker; 72% of previously warned products still adulterated in the Sacramento survey) implies FDA warnings do not reliably clear a product from shelves.

### Gaps
- Per-year counts of FDA sexual-enhancement notifications for 2024, 2025 and 2026 could not be extracted (the FDA table is JavaScript-paginated; only the first 10 of 441 rows were retrievable), so the annual rate is unknown; the FDA "Export Excel" function was not accessible.
- Hidden ingredients for several 2026 entries (Miracle Power of King Kong Honey, LUZE Fit Intensity, Beauty 911, DEEP, Premium OrgaZEN 10000) and exact dates for X10, ENDUREA and VITAFER-L Gold were not retrieved.
- No FDA recall (as opposed to public notification) specific to a 2024–2026 sexual-enhancement product was identified in this search; Tucker found FDA increasingly uses notifications rather than recalls.

## Key Question 2: What have independent laboratory analyses of marketplace products found (percent adulterated, doses, analogues)?

### Takeaway
Independent surveys consistently find that one-third to over 80% of "herbal" sexual-enhancement products contain undeclared PDE5 inhibitors, that doses are frequently at or above prescription strength (sometimes many-fold higher), that two or more drugs are commonly combined, and that dozens of untested structural analogues (e.g., hydroxythiohomosildenafil, thiosildenafil, dimethylsildenafil, sulfoaildenafil) appear alongside or instead of the approved drugs; adulteration is concentrated in single-pill blister packs sold at gas stations and corner stores.

### Cited Findings
- California Department of Public Health survey (Fischer et al., Nutrition and Dietary Supplements, published 2 Dec 2023; samples bought Sept 2016–Nov 2017 within 15 miles of downtown Sacramento): 102 products from 28 retail locations; 67% (68/102) contained at least one of 19 PDE5 inhibitors screened by LC/MS; 88% (66/75) of products from gas stations, liquor stores, cigarette shops or mini-marts were adulterated versus 7% (2/27) from pharmacies, health/nutrition stores or supermarkets; 89% (65/73) of single-pill blister packs versus 10% (3/29) of bottled products were adulterated (OR 70.4, p<0.0001) — [Fischer et al. 2023](https://www.dovepress.com/cross-sectional-survey-of-adulterated-sexual-enhancement-products-sold-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDS)
- Same study: 60% (41/68) of positive samples contained two or more drugs; seven PDE5 inhibitors identified — sildenafil 74% (50/68), tadalafil 59% (40/68), dimethylsildenafil 15%, thiodimethylsildenafil 13%, hydroxythiohomosildenafil 7%, thiosildenafil 6%, hydroxyhomosildenafil 1%; 46% (31/68) contained both sildenafil and tadalafil; 42% (25/59) of sildenafil/tadalafil-adulterated products contained drug "at or exceeding the minimum recommended prescription dosages" (sildenafil 25–100 mg; tadalafil 5–20 mg); "The safety of the doses of analogues is unknown" — [Fischer et al. 2023](https://www.dovepress.com/cross-sectional-survey-of-adulterated-sexual-enhancement-products-sold-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDS)
- Same study: 18% of samples were already on FDA's Tainted Products list, and 72% (13/18) of those were still adulterated; 41% of labels declared horny goat weed, 40% maca, 25% yohimbe (declaration not statistically associated with adulteration); "many of the tested samples, including a majority of those that were found to be adulterated, indicated that they were made in the US"; 97% of packages carried some warning statement, and 76% warned against exceeding a maximum dose (65 of these 78 were adulterated) — [Fischer et al. 2023](https://www.dovepress.com/cross-sectional-survey-of-adulterated-sexual-enhancement-products-sold-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDS)
- Campbell et al. (J Sex Med 2013): of 91 purported "herbal/natural" OTC sexual-performance products, 74 (81%) contained PDE5-inhibitor pharmaceutical ingredients — tadalafil and/or sildenafil in 40 (18 of which contained >110% of the highest approved drug strength) and PDE5-inhibitor analogues in 34; none declared synthetic substances — [PubMed 23634714](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23634714/)
- Korean national laboratory screen for 94 sexual-enhancement compounds (Food Additives & Contaminants, 2021): 130 of 404 samples (32%) were adulterated with one or more PDE5 inhibitors, and "the amount of PDE-5i found in some samples far exceeded the prescribed dosage" — [PubMed 33818311](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33818311/); summary in [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/350643392_Detection_of_94_compounds_related_to_sexual_enhancement_including_sildenafil_tadalafil_vardenafil_and_their_analogues_in_various_formulations_of_dietary_supplements_and_food_samples_using_HPLC_and_LC-)
- Global review (Yéléhé-Okouma et al., Fundam Clin Pharmacol 2021;35:792-807) describes worldwide adulteration; one cited series found 17 adulterated products with total PDE5-inhibitor content ranging from 2.77 to 121.64 mg per sachet — [PubMed 33484004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33484004/); figure quoted in [ResearchGate abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348707314_Drug_adulteration_of_sexual_enhancement_supplements_a_worldwide_insidious_public_health_threat)
- International surveys cited by the Sacramento authors: 10 of 64 Czech-market products (2009–2015) and 34 of 62 Malaysian-market products (2014–2016) contained PDE5 inhibitors — [Fischer et al. 2023](https://www.dovepress.com/cross-sectional-survey-of-adulterated-sexual-enhancement-products-sold-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDS)
- Analogue count: a 2014 analytical review counted "more than 50 unapproved analogues of prescription PDE-5 inhibitors" found as adulterants — [Patel et al., J Pharm Biomed Anal 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S073170851300191X); a 2018 paper reported "Over 80 ED drug analogues have successfully been identified in adulterated dietary supplements" and screened 80 PDE-5i/analogues by LC-MS — [J Food Drug Anal 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1355030618300121); the Sacramento authors likewise state "Eighty PDE5 inhibitor analogues have been identified as adulterants in DS" — [Fischer et al. 2023](https://www.dovepress.com/cross-sectional-survey-of-adulterated-sexual-enhancement-products-sold-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDS)
- Rhino case report: Rhino pills were reported to contain sildenafil/tadalafil "at exceedingly high doses ranging between 14 - 200 times the recommended labeled dose," and DOJ findings described sildenafil/tadalafil powder smuggled from Hong Kong/China and repackaged in the US with contaminants such as talcum powder, inks and paint — [Deliwala et al., Cureus 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7212751/)
- Harvard Health summarizing a 2015 J Sex Med review: "one study... found 81% of tested over-the-counter products purchased in the U.S. and Asia contained PDE5 inhibitors" — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/beware-of-erectile-dysfunction-scams)
- Yohimbine content labeling (Cohen et al., Drug Test Anal 2015/2016): of 49 yohimbine supplement brands bought at seven major US retail chains, only 2/49 (4.1%) gave consumers accurate information about yohimbine quantity; 18% (9/49) of labels gave no adverse-effect information; only 22% listed yohimbine quantity at all — [Tennessee Poison Center/VUMC summary](https://www.vumc.org/poison-control/toxicology-question-week/feb-15-2022-yohimbine-or-no-himbine-potentially-fatal-over-counter); [PubMed 26391406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26391406/); [MedPage Today](https://www.medpagetoday.com/endocrinology/generalendocrinology/53660)
- Evidence base of "legitimate" ingredients in popular online ED supplements (Balasubramanian et al., J Sex Med 2019): 21 active ingredients were used across top-selling online ED supplements; 8 of 21 (38%) had no published studies at all; L-arginine was the most studied (256 articles, 36 human studies), ginseng had 41 human studies and zinc 32; only 26 human studies tested ingredients individually — [PMC9555224](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9555224/)

### Inferences
- The retail channel and packaging format are the strongest observable predictors of adulteration: single-serving blister packs at gas stations/convenience stores were adulterated ~9 of 10 times, bottled products at pharmacies/health stores ~1 in 10 to 1 in 14.
- Label claims ("made in USA," presence of warning text, botanical ingredient lists) carried no protective signal in the Sacramento data; the presence of multiple PDE5 inhibitors at prescription-level doses indicates deliberate, not accidental, adulteration.
- Because ~40% of adulterated products carried prescription-level doses and some Rhino lots carried many multiples of the maximum dose, a consumer cannot infer dose safety from "one pill" directions.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2024–2026 prevalence study of US online-marketplace (Amazon/eBay) sexual-enhancement products was located; the most recent US retail prevalence data are the 2016–2017 Sacramento samples (published 2023). A Nov 2025 Int J Impot Res paper on "royal honey packs" exists ([PubMed 41225045](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41225045/)) but its content could not be accessed.
- Dose figures for the 2024–2026 FDA-flagged products are not published in FDA notices (FDA reports presence, not quantity).

## Key Question 3: Why are hidden PDE5 inhibitors (and their analogues) dangerous?

### Takeaway
Undeclared sildenafil/tadalafil are hazardous because they are contraindicated with nitrates (severe hypotension), interact with alpha-blockers and other antihypertensives, arrive at unknown and often supra-therapeutic doses with no screening of the user's cardiac status, are frequently combined with each other and with other hidden drugs (acetaminophen, diclofenac, sibutramine, dapoxetine, phenolphthalein), and are increasingly replaced by structural analogues whose efficacy and toxicity "have not been clinically assessed."

### Cited Findings
- Standard FDA notification language (e.g., MAXMAN Coffee, 9/2/2026): sildenafil and tadalafil "may interact with nitrates found in some prescription drugs such as nitroglycerin and may lower blood pressure to dangerous levels. This is especially concerning for people with diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol or heart disease, who commonly take nitrate-containing medications"; FDA approval of Viagra/Cialis "is restricted to use under the supervision of a licensed health care professional" — [FDA MAXMAN Coffee notice](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/maxman-coffee-may-be-harmful-due-hidden-drug-ingredients)
- Viagra prescribing information lists nitrates as a contraindication (Section 4.1) — [FDA Viagra label, 2014](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2014/20895s039s042lbl.pdf); "Sildenafil should be used with caution in patients who take alpha-blockers because coadministration may lead to symptomatic hypotension in some individuals" — [Cardiovascular safety of sildenafil citrate, PubMed 17011375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17011375/); the Revatio (sildenafil) label notes that in interaction studies with alpha-blockers "cases of symptomatic hypotension consisting of dizziness and lightheadedness" occurred — [FDA Revatio label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2009/021845s006lbl.pdf)
- "Men who are prescribed nitrates are contraindicated to take PDE5 inhibitors and may turn to an all-natural dietary supplement to manage erectile dysfunction, unaware that they are consuming active pharmaceutical ingredients"; the drugs "are present at unknown concentrations and have not been characterized as safe and effective by the FDA"; dapoxetine (an unapproved SSRI) also appears, "risking interaction with other medications or preexisting health conditions" — [Tucker et al., JAMA Netw Open 2018](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2706496)
- Sacramento authors: PDE5 inhibitor–nitrate interactions "have the potential to lower blood pressure to a dangerous level and can lead to fatal cardiovascular collapse"; "It is unknown how these PDE5 inhibitors react when combined together"; people on nitrates "would not be prescribed PDE5 inhibitors if experiencing ED and may be inclined to take a DS to manage their ED" — [Fischer et al. 2023](https://www.dovepress.com/cross-sectional-survey-of-adulterated-sexual-enhancement-products-sold-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDS)
- Analogue safety: "the presence of analogues represents a serious health risk as their efficacy and toxic effects have not been clinically assessed yet" — [Drug Testing and Analysis 2020](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dta.2806); "The toxicity or pharmacological safety profile of these analogues is less specified and poses a significant risk to public health"; PDE5i interactions "with nitrates or α-blockers would bring about hypotension, syncope" — [J Food Drug Anal 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1355030618300121)
- FDA notices documenting analogues: Eyeful contained hydroxythiohomosildenafil, "a substance structurally similar to the active ingredient" in Viagra — [FDA Eyeful](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/public-notification-eyeful-contains-hidden-drug-ingredient); Alpha Male contained sulfosildenafil, sulfoaildenafil, hydroxythiohomosildenafil and dimethylsildenafil — [FDA Alpha Male](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/public-notification-alpha-male-contains-hidden-drug-ingredient); Lightning ROD and Fuel Up High Octane contained hydroxythiohomosildenafil — [FDA Lightning ROD](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/public-notification-lightning-rod-contains-hidden-drug-ingredient), [FDA Fuel Up High Octane](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/public-notification-fuel-high-octane-contains-hidden-drug-ingredient); FDA Import Alert 54-16 lists multiple Chinese-origin products detained because they contain hydroxythiohomosildenafil — [FDA Import Alert 54-16](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cms_ia/importalert_1141.html)
- Unknown/excess dosing: 18 of 40 sildenafil/tadalafil-containing products exceeded 110% of the highest approved strength (Campbell 2013) — [PubMed 23634714](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23634714/); 42% of adulterated Sacramento products were at/above minimum prescription dose — [Fischer et al. 2023](https://www.dovepress.com/cross-sectional-survey-of-adulterated-sexual-enhancement-products-sold-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDS); Rhino pills reported at 14–200 times labeled prescription limits — [Cureus 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7212751/)
- Co-hidden drugs and their stated risks: acetaminophen ("Taking too much acetaminophen can cause liver damage, ranging from mild liver problems to complete liver failure and death"; symptoms may be delayed) — [FDA 10 Day Forecast](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/10-day-forecast-may-be-harmful-due-hidden-ingredients); diclofenac (NSAID: "increased risk of cardiovascular events, such as heart attack and stroke, as well as serious gastrointestinal damage, including bleeding, ulceration, and fatal perforation") — [FDA HimGo](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/himgo-may-be-harmful-due-hidden-drug-ingredients); sibutramine (controlled substance removed from market October 2010; "known to substantially increase blood pressure and/or heart rate") and phenolphthalein ("may increase the risk of cancer") — [FDA WILDMAN](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/wildman-may-be-harmful-due-hidden-drug-ingredients)
- Health Canada: hidden PDE5 inhibitors "should not be used by individuals taking any kind of nitrate drug (e.g. nitroglycerine) as it can cause potentially life-threatening low blood pressure" — [Health Canada advisory](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/alert-recall/unauthorized-sexual-enhancement-products-may-pose-serious-health-risks)
- Mayo Clinic: hidden ingredients "may interact with other medications an individual takes and trigger dangerous side effects," and "in those products, the medications are not tested or regulated, so they may be tainted with contaminants" — [Mayo Clinic Q&A 2020](https://newsnetwork.mayoclinic.org/discussion/mayo-clinic-q-and-a-some-products-that-claim-to-treat-erectile-dysfunction-can-be-harmful/)

### Inferences
- The population most likely to seek an "all-natural" ED product (men with heart disease, diabetes, hypertension who are on nitrates or alpha-blockers, or whose physician declined to prescribe a PDE5 inhibitor) is precisely the population for whom a hidden PDE5 inhibitor is most dangerous.
- A product that acts as quickly and reliably as a prescription drug ("works in 30 minutes") is more plausibly a drug than a botanical; the "works like Viagra" marketing signal should be read as a probability signal for sildenafil-class content.

### Gaps
- No quantitative estimate exists of how many US nitrate users have been exposed to hidden PDE5 inhibitors; FDA MedWatch data for these products were not retrievable in aggregated form.

## Key Question 4: What specific harms have been documented (hospitalizations, deaths, emergency visits, case reports)?

### Takeaway
Documented harms include FDA-received reports of chest pain, severe headaches and prolonged erections requiring surgery and hospitalization from Rhino products; a published STEMI with complete heart block in a 40-year-old after three Rhino pills; an estimated 23,000 US emergency-department visits per year for supplements overall (2004–2013) with sexual-enhancement or bodybuilding products implicated in 14.1% of male visits and cardiac symptoms in 37.3% of sexual-enhancement visits; and, for yohimbine, 238 California poison-center cases (2000–2006) with severe outcomes almost six times more likely than average exposures, plus published fatal intoxications.

### Cited Findings
- FDA (Nov 2018): "The FDA has received reports of people experiencing chest pain, severe headaches and prolonged erections after taking a Rhino product that led to surgical intervention and hospitalization due to extreme drops in blood pressure" — [FDA release (PR Newswire)](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fda-warns-consumers-to-avoid-rhino-male-enhancement-products-found-at-retailers-because-of-undeclared-and-potentially-dangerous-drug-ingredients-300755962.html)
- Case report (Cureus 2020): a 40-year-old nitrate-free man developed unrelenting headache, nausea and chest pain after taking three 950 mg Rhino pills and intercourse; EMS ECG showed inferior STEMI with complete heart block (HR 48); angiography showed 100% right coronary occlusion; he received a drug-eluting stent and transvenous pacing; authors describe Rhino as containing 14–200× prescription limits of sildenafil/tadalafil and note FDA MedWatch citations against Rhino since 2015 — [Deliwala et al., Cureus 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7212751/)
- CDC/FDA surveillance (NEJM 2015): an estimated 23,005 ED visits per year (2004–2013) and ~2,154 hospitalizations per year were attributed to dietary-supplement adverse events; "Sexual-enhancement products or bodybuilding products were implicated in 14.1% (95% CI, 10.5 to 17.7) of emergency department visits for supplement-related adverse events among male patients"; cardiac symptoms (palpitations, chest pain, tachycardia) were documented in 37.3% (95% CI 25.3–49.3) of visits attributed to sexual-enhancement products — [Geller et al., NEJM 2015](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1504267)
- FDA states "These products pose a serious health risk and are not guaranteed to work. They can lead to severe health issues and hospitalization" — [FDA notifications page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/sexual-enhancement-and-energy-product-notifications)
- FDA's hidden-ingredients page states "More than 30 people have experienced serious health effects after using contaminated sexual enhancement products with 'Artri' or 'Ortiga' in the product name," with effects including GI bleeding/ulceration, raised blood sugar, liver toxicity and death (note: Artri/Ortiga products are marketed principally for joint pain and contain hidden diclofenac/dexamethasone; FDA's categorization on this page appears inconsistent) — [FDA Avoiding Products Contaminated with Hidden Ingredients](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/avoiding-products-contaminated-hidden-ingredients)
- Yohimbine poison-center series (Kearney et al., Ann Pharmacother 2010): 238 symptomatic adult cases reported to the California Poison Control System 2000–2006; annual prevalence rose from 1.8 to 8.0 per 10,000 adult exposures; 98.7% involved herbal (not prescription) yohimbine; sexual enhancement was the commonest reason for use (27.7%); adverse events were GI distress 46%, tachycardia 43%, anxiety/agitation 33%, hypertension 25%; yohimbine exposures were far more likely to have severe outcomes (OR 5.81, 95% CI 4.43–7.64) and to need health-care-facility management (OR 2.35) than average CPCS exposures — [PubMed 20442348](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20442348/)
- Tennessee Poison Center (2022): yohimbine "was FDA-approved for impotence and prescribed regularly" before PDE5 inhibitors but was removed "due to potential deleterious cardiac and neurologic adverse effects"; toxicity is a sympathomimetic toxidrome (tachycardia, hypertension, hyperthermia, seizures, psychosis); "Cardiac collapse with sudden death has been reported in multiple case reports"; "There is no antidote" — [VUMC/Tennessee Poison Center](https://www.vumc.org/poison-control/toxicology-question-week/feb-15-2022-yohimbine-or-no-himbine-potentially-fatal-over-counter)
- DoD Operation Supplement Safety cites "Two fatal case reports of acute yohimbine intoxication," "A fatal case report of acute yohimbine intoxication," and "Fatal poisoning by dietary supplements accompanied by massive rhabdomyolysis and multiple organ failure" — [OPSS yohimbe page](https://www.opss.org/article/yohimbe-and-yohimbine-dietary-supplement-products)
- FDA-prompted recall of "Business Pill" and "Branch Manager for Men": "FDA found dangerously high levels of yohimbine in the recalled products. Yohimbine can cause severe hypertension, tachycardia, and possibly cardiac arrhythmia and myocardial infarction... Death has been reported in cases of severe overdose" — [FDA recall notice](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/supplxcom-issues-voluntary-nationwide-recall-business-pill-and-branch-manager-men-due-undeclared); FDA notification: can cause "severe high blood pressure, irregular heartbeat, heart attack, stomach problems, seizures and mental health effects such as anxiety, confusion and tremors; in people with schizophrenia, it can cause mania or psychosis" — [FDA Branch Manager for Men](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/branch-manager-men-may-be-harmful-due-hidden-drug-ingredient)
- News report: Morocco's Anti-Poison and Pharmacovigilance Center issued an urgent warning after a 27-year-old man died after taking "Royal Honey" (press report; primary CAPM document not retrieved) — [Hespress](https://en.hespress.com/119391-27-year-old-man-dies-after-taking-royal-honey-to-boost-sexual-performance.html)
- Hepatotoxicity with declared botanicals (see Question 5): e.g., severe liver and renal injury after 2 months of daily Tribulus (2024), fatal acute liver failure after 2–3 months of Tribulus (case report), Tongkat ali-induced liver injury (2024), ashwagandha liver-injury case series (2020).

### Inferences
- Harm reports are almost certainly under-counted: NEJM authors note supplement use is under-reported and physicians under-recognize supplement adverse events; FDA relies on voluntary MedWatch reports.
- The cardiac profile of harms (chest pain, MI, hypotension, prolonged erection/priapism) matches the pharmacology of high-dose PDE5 inhibitors rather than the botanicals declared on labels.

### Gaps
- No US national estimate of deaths specifically attributable to adulterated sexual-enhancement supplements was found; FDA MedWatch summaries for this category were not retrievable in aggregate.
- Post-2013 national ED-visit estimates specific to sexual-enhancement products were not found.

## Key Question 5: Safety profiles and interaction warnings for common "legitimate" ingredients

### Takeaway
Most commonly declared ingredients have real but ingredient-specific risks: L-arginine and L-citrulline lower blood pressure and are flagged as "do not combine" with PDE5 inhibitors and nitrates (and L-arginine increased deaths after MI in a 2006 trial); yohimbine is the only common ingredient with repeated fatal intoxications and is labeled dangerous by NCCIH/Mayo/AUA; ashwagandha (LiverTox likelihood C), Tribulus, Tongkat ali and horny goat weed all have published liver-injury cases; DHEA is a hormone Mayo says to avoid; ginseng, fenugreek and ashwagandha lower blood sugar and interact with common drugs; zinc above 40 mg/day causes copper deficiency.

### Cited Findings
**L-arginine**
- Mayo Clinic (updated Mar 16, 2026): "L-arginine isn't recommended for people who have had a recent heart attack due to concerns that the supplement might increase the risk of death"; side effects include nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea, bloating, gout, headache, allergic response and worsening of asthma; interactions: anticoagulants/antiplatelets (bleeding), blood pressure drugs (BP too low), diabetes drugs (hypoglycemia), isoproterenol, nitrates ("might cause your blood pressure to become too low"), potassium-sparing diuretics (hyperkalemia) and sildenafil ("might cause your blood pressure to become too low") — [Mayo Clinic L-arginine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-l-arginine/art-20364681)
- VINTAGE MI trial (JAMA 2006): L-arginine 9 g/day added to standard post-MI therapy "does not improve vascular stiffness measurements or ejection fraction"; "6 participants (8.6%) in the L-arginine group died during the 6-month study period vs none in the placebo group (P = .01)"; the data safety board stopped enrollment — [JAMA 2006](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/202136); [PubMed 16391217](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16391217/); Harvard Health: "Men—especially those at risk for heart disease—should avoid these supplements" — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/beware-of-erectile-dysfunction-scams)
- Mayo Clinic ED-supplement table: "Don't take L-arginine with sildenafil" — [Mayo Clinic Dietary supplements for ED](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/erectile-dysfunction/in-depth/erectile-dysfunction-herbs/art-20044394)

**L-citrulline**
- Natural Medicines-derived monograph: interaction with PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil) rated "Major — Do not take this combination" (additive BP lowering); nitrates (nitroglycerin, isosorbide) "Major — Do not take this combination"; antihypertensives "Moderate"; rated "possibly safe"; pregnancy/breastfeeding safety unknown — [RxList L-citrulline](https://www.rxlist.com/supplements/l-citrulline.htm); WebMD lists the same interaction classes and notes that many "nitric oxide booster" products also contain undeclared-quantity caffeine — [WebMD L-citrulline](https://www.webmd.com/vitamins-supplements/l-citrulline)
- Review (Nutrients 2018): short-term citrulline reduces peripheral and aortic BP in pre-/hypertensive adults; "The safety and efficacy of long-term l-citrulline supplementation therefore requires further investigation," and long-term (>6 month) L-arginine studies "have demonstrated a concerning lack of safety and efficacy" — [PMC6073798](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6073798/)

**Yohimbine / yohimbe**
- NCCIH: yohimbine "has been associated with cardiac arrhythmia (irregular heartbeat), blood pressure problems, heart attacks, and seizures"; poison-center callers about yohimbe were more likely to need medical care than other callers — [NCCIH Yohimbe](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yohimbe)
- Mayo Clinic: yohimbe "has been linked with dangerous side effects... agitation, increased blood pressure, fast or irregular heartbeat, heart attack and seizures. Do not use yohimbe without a healthcare professional's guidance" — [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/erectile-dysfunction/in-depth/erectile-dysfunction-herbs/art-20044394)
- AUA guideline (2005 update): "Recommendation: Yohimbine is not recommended for the treatment of erectile dysfunction"; yohimbine "can cause elevations of blood pressure and heart rate, increased motor activity, irritability, and tremor" — [AUA Management of ED: An Update (PDF)](https://www.auanet.org/documents/education/clinical-guidance/erectile-dysfunction.pdf)
- Labeling is unreliable: only 4.1% of 49 retail yohimbine brands stated an accurate quantity — [VUMC summary of Cohen et al.](https://www.vumc.org/poison-control/toxicology-question-week/feb-15-2022-yohimbine-or-no-himbine-potentially-fatal-over-counter); [PubMed 26391406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26391406/)
- DoD OPSS: "Individuals who experience anxiety, depression, hypertension, hypotension, or PTSD should avoid use of yohimbine" — [OPSS](https://www.opss.org/article/yohimbe-and-yohimbine-dietary-supplement-products)

**Panax (Asian/Korean red) ginseng**
- NCCIH (Feb 2025): "Some research shows that taking oral Asian ginseng seems to improve sexual function in people with erectile dysfunction"; short-term use (up to 6 months) appears safe; insomnia is the most common side effect; uncommon reports of severe rash, liver damage and severe allergic reactions; "may worsen autoimmune disorders and interfere with blood clotting"; "might lower blood sugar levels"; "might interact with certain medications"; may be unsafe in pregnancy — [NCCIH Asian Ginseng](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng)
- Harvard Health: ginseng "is the most common ingredient in top-selling men's supplements," "can cause headaches, upset stomach, constipation, rash, and insomnia and can lower blood sugar levels, so men with diabetes should avoid it" — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/beware-of-erectile-dysfunction-scams)
- AUA 2005: one small RCT suggested Korean red ginseng may help ED but "Clinical efficacy of Korean red ginseng remains to be validated by larger trials" — [AUA PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/education/clinical-guidance/erectile-dysfunction.pdf)

**Maca (Lepidium meyenii)**
- 2024 comprehensive review: "Maca has a good safety profile... no fatal adverse effects have been reported"; human trials of 2–3 g/day for 12 weeks showed no serious adverse effects but transient GI upset, headache and irritability; two case reports of apparent adverse effects — vaginal bleeding in a 24-year-old woman and a manic episode in a 27-year-old man with no psychiatric history; a possible CYP3A4-mediated interaction raising mianserin levels in a 64-year-old man — [PMC10910417](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10910417/)
- Systematic review (2022): in people with metabolic syndrome, maca "increased aspartate aminotransferase and diastolic blood pressure levels in both sexes in addition to systolic blood pressure in females" in one trial — [ScienceDirect 2022 review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667031322001075)
- Memorial Sloan Kettering: "Talk to your healthcare provider if you have hormone-sensitive cancer, such as breast or uterine cancer. Maca may affect your treatment"; maca may interfere with testosterone immunoassays — [MSKCC Maca](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/maca)
- Harvard Health: "Side effects like a mild increase in liver enzymes and blood pressure are rare" — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/beware-of-erectile-dysfunction-scams)

**Tribulus terrestris**
- Case report (ACG Case Rep J, 2024): 46-year-old man developed severe liver and renal injury after taking tribulus supplements daily for 2 months — [PMC10849413](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849413/)
- Case report (Am J Case Rep 2024): 71-year-old man on long-term atorvastatin presented with rhabdomyolysis and transaminitis 2 weeks after starting Tribulus terrestris 500 mg daily; authors cite "2 documented reports of severe nephrotoxicity from acute tubular necrosis" in young men — [Am J Case Rep](https://amjcaserep.com/abstract/full/idArt/943492)
- Case report: a woman who used Tribulus for 2–3 months for weight loss developed acute liver failure and died — [Asia Pac J Med Toxicol](https://apjmt.mums.ac.ir/article_15318.html)
- LiverTox likelihood score for Tribulus listed as "E" (unlikely cause) as of 2023 — [MDPI review of HDS-induced liver injury](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4389/4/1/8); Harvard Health: "There is no evidence that this herb has any benefit in humans" — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/beware-of-erectile-dysfunction-scams)

**Tongkat ali (Eurycoma longifolia)**
- LiverTox: "Recently, there have been isolated reports of clinically apparent liver injury in patients taking tongkat ali" — [LiverTox NBK609015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK609015/); case report of Tongkat ali-induced liver injury in a 47-year-old man with jaundice and elevated enzymes — [Cureus 2024](https://www.cureus.com/articles/235047-a-rare-case-of-tongkat-ali-induced-liver-injury-a-case-report)
- DoD OPSS (2023): EFSA warned in 2021 that tongkat ali root extract "has the potential to cause DNA damage in animals and stated its overall safety has not been established"; "Products containing tongkat ali have been reported to be contaminated with heavy metals (such as lead and mercury) or adultered with sildenafil"; "Without laboratory testing there is no way to know the actual ingredients (or their amounts) in a product" — [OPSS Tongkat ali](https://www.opss.org/article/tongkat-ali-uses-and-safety-dietary-supplements); EFSA opinion noted adverse effects in liver and kidneys in a chronic toxicity study — [EFSA Journal 2021](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2021.6937)

**Fenugreek**
- NCCIH (Feb 2025): "Large doses may cause a harmful drop in blood sugar"; "can cause allergic reactions, including serious reactions"; GI side effects; "not safe for use during pregnancy in amounts greater than those found in food; its use has been linked to increased risks of birth defects"; "some herbs and medicines interact in harmful ways" — [NCCIH Fenugreek](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/fenugreek); LiverTox likelihood "E" — [MDPI review](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4389/4/1/8); Harvard: "other research has shown this herb to be safe over all" — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/beware-of-erectile-dysfunction-scams)

**Ashwagandha**
- NCCIH (Mar 2023): "may be safe when taken in the short term (up to 3 months)"; "Although it is rare, there have been a number of cases that link liver injury to ashwagandha supplements"; avoid in pregnancy/breastfeeding, before surgery, and with autoimmune or thyroid disorders; may interact with diabetes and blood-pressure medicines, immunosuppressants, sedatives, anticonvulsants and thyroid hormone; "Because ashwagandha may increase testosterone levels, people with hormone-sensitive prostate cancer should avoid its use" — [NCCIH Ashwagandha](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha)
- LiverTox likelihood score "C" (probable rare cause) as of 2023; Björnsson et al. (Liver Int 2020) case series from Iceland and the US Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network; injury typically cholestatic or mixed, presenting up to 12 weeks after starting — [MDPI review](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4389/4/1/8); [LiverTox NBK548536](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548536/)

**Horny goat weed (Epimedium)**
- Mayo Clinic: "not enough evidence to show that it helps for ED"; side effects "dizziness, dry mouth, nosebleed, thirst and vomiting. This herb also may affect heart or breathing functions" — [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/erectile-dysfunction/in-depth/erectile-dysfunction-herbs/art-20044394)
- Case reports: tachyarrhythmia and hypomania after two weeks of use (Partin & Pushkin, 2004) — [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Tachyarrhythmia-and-hypomania-with-horny-goat-weed.-Partin-Pushkin/65aa6f1719a5de24bd1012d1785262b22e890bbe); severe muscle spasms with elevated CK and creatinine (2025) — [PMC12182873](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12182873/); acute hepatotoxicity after prolonged use (2025) — [Spartan Med Res J](https://smrj.scholasticahq.com/article/144607-herb-induced-liver-injury-a-case-of-acute-hepatotoxicity-associated-with-horny-goat-weed-use); LiverTox likelihood "E" — [MDPI review](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4389/4/1/8)
- Horny goat weed was the most commonly declared botanical (41%) in the adulterated Sacramento products — [Fischer et al. 2023](https://www.dovepress.com/cross-sectional-survey-of-adulterated-sexual-enhancement-products-sold-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDS)

**Zinc**
- NIH ODS: adult Tolerable Upper Intake Level 40 mg/day from all sources; excess causes nausea, dizziness, headaches, vomiting; long-term excess causes lower immunity, low HDL and low copper (copper deficiency can cause "loss of coordination, numbness, and weakness"); interactions with quinolone and tetracycline antibiotics, penicillamine, and thiazide diuretics — [NIH ODS Zinc Consumer Fact Sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-Consumer/)

**DHEA**
- Mayo Clinic (May 12, 2026), rating "Avoid": DHEA "might raise levels of androgen and have a steroid effect"; high doses or long-term use "might raise the risk of hormone-sensitive cancers, such as prostate and breast cancers"; "If you have a type of hormone-sensitive cancer, don't use DHEA"; may reduce HDL (caution in ischemic heart disease/high cholesterol); may worsen mental-health conditions and raise mania risk; interactions with estrogen, antidepressants, testosterone and triazolam; "Studies have shown quality control of this supplement to often be low"; banned by the NCAA — [Mayo Clinic DHEA](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-dhea/art-20364199); DHEA (prasterone) is a WADA S1 anabolic agent on certification screening menus — [BSCG testing menu](https://www.bscg.org/bscg-banned-substance-testing-menu-v070725); Mayo ED table: "Long-term or heavy use of DHEA may be linked with some cancers" — [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/erectile-dysfunction/in-depth/erectile-dysfunction-herbs/art-20044394)

### Inferences
- For a man already taking a prescription PDE5 inhibitor, nitrates, alpha-blockers or antihypertensives, the "nitric oxide" ingredients (L-arginine, L-citrulline) are the most clinically relevant interaction risk among declared ingredients; for men with recent MI, L-arginine is specifically discouraged.
- Yohimbine is the one declared ingredient whose risk profile approaches that of a hidden drug (narrow margin, mislabeled doses, fatalities); products listing yohimbe/yohimbine deserve the same caution as suspected adulterated products.
- Hormone-active ingredients (DHEA, possibly ashwagandha, maca, tongkat ali via testosterone effects) warrant avoidance in men with prostate cancer or other hormone-sensitive conditions; this is explicit for DHEA (Mayo) and ashwagandha (NCCIH) and advisory for maca (MSKCC).

### Gaps
- A fenugreek–warfarin bleeding interaction and the reported 2023 Danish restriction on ashwagandha were not verified from primary sources in this search.
- LiverTox monographs for ashwagandha and tongkat ali could not be opened directly (access blocked); LiverTox likelihood grades were taken from a 2024 peer-reviewed review that tabulated them.
- No RCT-level safety data for multi-ingredient "proprietary blend" ED products were found; Balasubramanian et al. found 38% of popular ingredients had no published studies at all.

## Key Question 6: What do third-party certifications actually verify, which ones screen for undeclared drugs such as PDE5 inhibitors, and how can a consumer verify a certification online?

### Takeaway
Only the banned-substance/"drug-free" programs (BSCG Certified Drug Free, Informed Sport/Informed Choice, NSF Certified for Sport) screen finished products for drugs; BSCG's published panel explicitly names tadalafil, vardenafil, avanafil, udenafil, dapoxetine, phenolphthalein, sibutramine and dozens of sildenafil analogues (including hydroxythiohomosildenafil and thiosildenafil), whereas USP Verified, NSF/ANSI 173 Contents Certified, ConsumerLab and Labdoor verify identity, potency, GMP and contaminants (heavy metals, microbes, pesticides) but are not drug-screening programs. Every program says a logo alone is insufficient: the specific product and lot number should be looked up in the certifier's public database or app.

### Cited Findings
- NSF Certified for Sport verifies that "Products do not contain any of 290 substances banned by major athletic organizations," "The contents of the supplement actually match what is printed on the label," "There are no unsafe levels of contaminants," and the product "is manufactured at a facility that is GMP Certified and audited annually or bi-annually"; it is "the only independent third-party certification program recognized by the United States Anti-Doping Agency (USADA), Major League Baseball, the National Hockey League, and the Canadian Football League" — [NSF Certified for Sport: What Our Mark Means](https://www.nsfsport.com/our-mark.php); NSF elsewhere says it screens "for over 295+ substances" and that Certified for Sport products "must also be certified to either NSF/ANSI 173, NSF 229 or NSF 527 to ensure their contents match their claims" — [NSF Product and Ingredient Certification](https://www.nsf.org/nutrition-wellness/product-and-ingredient-certification)
- Verification: USADA tells athletes to "Scan the supplement label using the NSF Certified for Sport app to make sure it's a certified product and lot number" and recognizes NSF Certified for Sport as "the program best suited for athletes to reduce the risk from supplements" — [USADA Supplement Connect](https://www.usada.org/substances/supplement-connect/reduce-risk-testing-positive-experiencing-adverse-health-effects/); NSF: "consumers can use NSF's online directory to look up certified products and use the NSF Sport app to scan for products that have earned NSF Certified for Sport" — [NSF FAQs](https://www.nsf.org/about-nsf/faqs/dietary-supplements-functional-foods-faqs)
- NSF/ANSI 173 "Contents Certified": "the product has been submitted for technical review, product testing and contaminant testing. Products must be GMP certified"; NSF checks "for the presence of contaminants that impact health, including the toxic elements lead, mercury, arsenic, cadmium and chromium"; the Contents Certified mark "certifies that the contents have been tested to ensure they do not contain unsafe levels of contaminants and to verify label claims," while Certified for Sport additionally covers "prohibited substances or masking agents" — [NSF FAQs](https://www.nsf.org/about-nsf/faqs/dietary-supplements-functional-foods-faqs); [NSF Sport mark announcement](https://www.nsfsport.com/news-resources/news/new-contents-certified-certified-for-sport-certification-marks.php)
- USP Verified Mark means the product "Contains the ingredients listed on the label in the declared strength and amounts," "Does not contain harmful levels of specified contaminants, such as heavy metals and pesticides," "Has been made according to FDA Good Manufacturing Practices," and "Will break down and dissolve within a specified amount of time"; verified products are searchable in the USP Verified Product Finder at quality-supplements.org; no drug/banned-substance screening is described — [USP Verified Mark](https://www.usp.org/verification-services/verified-mark)
- Informed Sport: "the only global third-party testing and certification programme that tests every single batch of a product prior to it being released to market," using ISO 17025-accredited methods to test "for more than 285 substances banned in sport," with post-certification blind retail testing; developed in 2008 with UK Anti-Doping; consumers verify a batch at sport.wetestyoutrust.com or the Informed Sport app. Informed Choice uses the same methods but is "a retail monitoring programme completed through regular blind testing" (at least twelve lots/batches per year), so "it is not guaranteed that every batch has been tested" — [Informed Sport vs Informed Choice](https://sport.wetestyoutrust.com/about/sport-vs-choice); [Informed Sport](https://sport.wetestyoutrust.com/)
- BSCG Certified Drug Free "evaluates all finished product lots for over 450 substances, including about 400 WADA-banned drugs and over 50 prescription, over-the-counter, and illicit compounds, using ISO 17025 accredited labs," requires a GMP audit with annual compliance verification, and maintains a public lot database; BSCG's comparison lists panels of BSCG 450+, Informed Sport 285+, NSF Certified for Sport 290+, HASTA 250+, Cologne List ~150, and notes NSF's lot-testing frequency is "Variable by product and manufacturer" while BSCG and Informed Sport test every lot/batch — [BSCG comparison (June 2026)](https://www.bscg.org/blogs/single/independent-supplement-testing-what-it-is-and-who-does-it)
- BSCG's published testing menu (v070725) explicitly includes, under "Prescription, OTC, Illicit Drugs": tadalafil, vardenafil, avanafil, udenafil, mirodenafil, lodenafil carbonate, dapoxetine, phenolphthalein, fluoxetine, finasteride, dutasteride and numerous analogues such as hydroxythiohomosildenafil, thiosildenafil, thiohomosildenafil, homosildenafil, hydroxyhomosildenafil, aildenafil, acetildenafil, nortadalafil, aminotadalafil, carbodenafil, chlorodenafil, N-desmethylsildenafil, benzylsildenafil and propoxyphenyl sildenafil; sibutramine and its desmethyl metabolites appear under WADA S6 stimulants and DHEA (prasterone) under S1; BSCG states "All drugs included in Amazon dietary supplement compliance API testing requirements are covered" and that the list "is not exhaustive" — [BSCG Banned Substance Testing Menu](https://www.bscg.org/bscg-banned-substance-testing-menu-v070725); BSCG also states its menu covers "Drugs like PDE-5 inhibitors (viagra), muscle relaxants, pain-killers" — [BSCG 2023 menu page](https://www.bscg.org/dietary-supplement-drug-testing-menu-2023)
- Amazon now requires third-party testing of sexual-enhancement supplements; a contract lab advertises "Testing for Sildenafil, Tadalafil, Vardenafil, Sulfoaildenafil and Desmethyl carbodenafil for Amazon compliance. ISO 17025" — [Vivariant](https://vivariant.com/home/supplement-pde5-inhibitor-screening/); BSCG: "Platforms such as Amazon now require third-party verification of dietary supplements, especially for high-risk categories" — [BSCG](https://www.bscg.org/blogs/single/independent-supplement-testing-what-it-is-and-who-does-it)
- ConsumerLab tests products "whenever possible" for Identity, Strength (quantity) and Purity ("free of specified contaminants"), buys products on the open market, publishes its methods, and requires retesting every 12 months for products carrying the CL Seal; its purity testing covers heavy metals and pathogenic microbes plus disintegration — [ConsumerLab About](https://www.consumerlab.com/about/); [ConsumerLab heavy-metals page](https://www.consumerlab.com/answers/supplements-and-foods-that-may-be-contaminated-with-lead/lead-contamination-supplements/); a third-party comparison states "ConsumerLab does not screen for banned substances specifically" — [Nutrola comparison](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/usp-nsf-informed-sport-consumerlab-certifications-compared-2026)
- Labdoor offers "Quality (Purity + Accuracy)," "Sport," and "THC Free" certifications; Purity means heavy metals (lead ≤5, arsenic ≤15, cadmium ≤5, mercury ≤15 mcg/serving) and microbiological contaminants are below limits; Accuracy means active ingredients test at or above label; products are bought at retail — [Labdoor Certification Standards](https://labdoor.com/enterprise/certifications/standards); [Labdoor](https://labdoor.com/)
- Limits of all certifications: "No testing program guarantees that every marketplace product is free of all risks... Certification validity applies only to tested products and defined lots"; "Possession of a certification mark on packaging alone is insufficient evidence of compliance"; claims such as "WADA approved," "safe for all athletes," and "guaranteed drug free" "overstate the coverage of certification programs" (WADA does not approve supplements) — [BSCG comparison](https://www.bscg.org/blogs/single/independent-supplement-testing-what-it-is-and-who-does-it); Informed Sport: "A label stating 'Safe for Sports People', or 'Approved by WADA' is meaningless" — [Informed Sport](https://sport.wetestyoutrust.com/)
- NIH ODS: seals from ConsumerLab.com, NSF International and U.S. Pharmacopeia indicate "the product was properly manufactured, contains the ingredients listed on the label, and does not contain harmful levels of contaminants. These seals do not guarantee that a product is safe or effective" — [NIH ODS: Dietary Supplements: What You Need to Know](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/WYNTK-Consumer/)
- FDA: "Be cautious when a product is marketed as being manufactured in an FDA-approved facility; FDA does not approve facilities" — [FDA Avoiding Contaminated Products](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/avoiding-products-contaminated-hidden-ingredients)

### Inferences
- For the specific threat of hidden PDE5 inhibitors, a USP Verified, NSF Contents Certified, ConsumerLab-approved or Labdoor-certified seal is evidence of correct identity/potency and low contaminants but is not direct evidence that the product was screened for sildenafil-class drugs; a BSCG Certified Drug Free lot (published panel includes PDE5 inhibitors and analogues) or an every-batch Informed Sport/NSF Certified for Sport lot is stronger evidence, and the lot number should be confirmed in the certifier's database.
- Certified sexual-enhancement "blends" are rare in practice; the realistic consumer path is buying single-ingredient products (e.g., L-citrulline, Panax ginseng) from brands whose lots appear in NSF/USP/BSCG/Informed Sport databases rather than any product marketed as a male-enhancement pill.

### Gaps
- NSF's public materials do not enumerate whether sildenafil/tadalafil are on its 290+ substance panel (the nsfsport.com banned-substance page returned 404); Informed Sport's substance list was not checked for PDE5 inhibitors. Sildenafil itself does not appear by name in the retrievable portion of BSCG's menu (tadalafil, vardenafil and many sildenafil analogues do), though BSCG states the published list is not exhaustive and that Amazon's required API panel is covered.
- ConsumerLab's own statement on whether it screens for undeclared PDE5 inhibitors was not found; the "does not screen for banned substances" statement comes from a secondary comparison site.

## Key Question 7: What do FDA, NIH ODS/NCCIH, Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, Harvard Health and the AUA advise consumers?

### Takeaway
Every authoritative source converges on the same advice: assume nonprescription "male enhancement" products may contain hidden drugs, do not use them, treat ED as a medical symptom that warrants evaluation (including cardiovascular screening), and if medication is needed use FDA-approved generic sildenafil/tadalafil under supervision; the AUA's guideline language states herbal therapies and yohimbine are not recommended.

### Cited Findings
- FDA consumer steps: search the Health Fraud Product Database before buying; "Watch out for products that make claims that seem too good to be true. Even if these products labels include the words 'herbal' or 'all natural,' that does not mean it is true, or that the product is safe"; "Beware of products with multiple rave reviews"; "Be especially wary of products sold for sexual enhancement and energy, weight loss, pain, bodybuilding or sleep"; be cautious of "FDA-approved facility" claims; "Talk to a health care professional before buying or trying a new product" — [FDA Avoiding Products Contaminated with Hidden Ingredients](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/avoiding-products-contaminated-hidden-ingredients)
- FDA (2018 Rhino release): "Consumers should also be on alert for products that offer immediate or quick results and that sound too good to be true"; "Use common sense... search for information using noncommercial sites rather than depending on information provided by sellers"; report reactions to FDA MedWatch — [FDA release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fda-warns-consumers-to-avoid-rhino-male-enhancement-products-found-at-retailers-because-of-undeclared-and-potentially-dangerous-drug-ingredients-300755962.html); each FDA notice directs reporting to MedWatch online or fax 1-800-FDA-0178 and states "FDA is unable to test and identify all products marketed as dietary supplements that have potentially harmful hidden ingredients" — [FDA Anaconda Premium 500K](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/anaconda-premium-500k-may-be-harmful-due-hidden-ingredient)
- NIH ODS: "the term natural doesn't always mean safe"; "You are most likely to have side effects from dietary supplements if you take them at high doses, or instead of prescribed medicines, or if you take many different supplements"; "Get your health care provider's approval before taking dietary supplements in place of, or in combination with, prescribed medicines"; report reactions to FDA at 800-FDA-1088 — [NIH ODS WYNTK](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/WYNTK-Consumer/); NCCIH: "Unlike drugs, dietary supplements are not approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) before they are sold to the public" — [NCCIH Asian Ginseng](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng)
- Mayo Clinic (Dr. Gregory Broderick, urology, Mar 2020): nonprescription ED products "are frequently cited by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) as containing hidden ingredients that can be harmful. Do not use any of these products"; "there is no way for a consumer to reliably know what nonprescription erectile dysfunction products contain. They are not safe"; younger men with unexplained ED "should be screened for heart disease before starting any treatment"; generic sildenafil and tadalafil are available — [Mayo Clinic Q&A](https://newsnetwork.mayoclinic.org/discussion/mayo-clinic-q-and-a-some-products-that-claim-to-treat-erectile-dysfunction-can-be-harmful/); Mayo's treatment page: "Before taking any medicine for erectile dysfunction, get your healthcare professional's OK. This includes supplements and herbal remedies" — [Mayo Clinic ED Diagnosis and Treatment](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/erectile-dysfunction/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20355782); Mayo's ingredient table (reviewed Sept 2024) rates L-arginine, ginseng, propionyl-L-carnitine, DHEA, yohimbe, ginkgo and horny goat weed with safety cautions as quoted in Question 5 — [Mayo Clinic Dietary supplements for ED](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/erectile-dysfunction/in-depth/erectile-dysfunction-herbs/art-20044394)
- Cleveland Clinic (urologist Raevti Bole, MD, Aug 2026): for pills, tablets, gummies, creams and lotions sold for male enhancement, "These products come with a lot of claims, but there's no evidence they work... And worst of all, you could actually harm yourself trying to chase results that aren't likely to come"; "Talk to a healthcare provider about your concerns before trying male enhancement products" — [Cleveland Clinic Health Essentials](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/penis-enlargement)
- Harvard Health (Harvard Men's Health Watch, 2017): most products "have not been studied scientifically for ED, and the FDA does not regulate their use and dosage"; a 2015 J Sex Med review "found little or no evidence to support common health fraud claims they can improve ED"; "never take any over-the-counter supplement without first checking with your doctor" — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/beware-of-erectile-dysfunction-scams)
- AUA guideline (Management of ED: An Update, 2005/2007 PDF): "Recommendation: Herbal therapies are not recommended for the treatment of erectile dysfunction"; "Recommendation: Yohimbine is not recommended"; "Product potency and quality both within and between brands are inconsistent... one study found deliberate contamination of some herbal products with therapeutic levels of PDE5 inhibitors" — [AUA PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/education/clinical-guidance/erectile-dysfunction.pdf). The current AUA Erectile Dysfunction Guideline (Burnett et al., J Urol 2018;200:633) emphasizes shared decision-making with FDA-approved therapies and contains no guideline statement on herbal supplements — [AUA 2018 Guideline](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/erectile-dysfunction-(ed)-guideline)
- DoD Operation Supplement Safety: "check the label to see if it has been evaluated by an independent, third-party organization" because "Without laboratory testing there is no way to know the actual ingredients (or their amounts) in a product" — [OPSS](https://www.opss.org/article/tongkat-ali-uses-and-safety-dietary-supplements)

### Inferences
- The professional consensus is not "choose a safer male-enhancement pill" but "do not use the category"; the only supplement-type ingredients these sources describe neutrally-to-positively are single ingredients with some trial evidence (Panax ginseng, L-arginine/L-citrulline, propionyl-L-carnitine) taken with clinician awareness.

### Gaps
- The Urology Care Foundation (AUA's patient arm) ED page could not be retrieved, so its specific patient-facing wording on supplements is not captured.
- The AUA has not updated its herbal-therapy recommendation since the 2005/2007 document; the 2018 guideline is silent on supplements.

## Key Question 8: What practical safety checklist and red flags follow from the evidence?

### Takeaway
A consumer can sharply reduce (not eliminate) risk by avoiding the product types, channels and claims that predict adulteration, by checking FDA's database and a certifier's lot database before purchase, and by treating any product that works like a drug as a drug.

### Cited Findings
- Red flag — single-pill blister packs at gas stations, liquor/cigarette stores and corner markets near the register: 88–89% adulterated vs 7–10% for bottled products at pharmacies/health stores — [Fischer et al. 2023](https://www.dovepress.com/cross-sectional-survey-of-adulterated-sexual-enhancement-products-sold-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDS)
- Red flag — product names evoking drugs or potency: "Herb Viagra" contained sildenafil — [Chiang et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5313300/); "Rhino" variants (more than 25 flagged since 2007), "25000/5000/500K/700000"-style numbers, "honey," "coffee," "candy," "shot" formats recur across FDA 2018–2026 notices — [FDA Rhino release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fda-warns-consumers-to-avoid-rhino-male-enhancement-products-found-at-retailers-because-of-undeclared-and-potentially-dangerous-drug-ingredients-300755962.html); [FDA notifications index](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/sexual-enhancement-and-energy-product-notifications); [FDA honey page](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/health-fraud-scams/tainted-honey-based-products-hidden-active-drug-ingredients)
- Red flag — claims of immediate/quick results, "too good to be true," "herbal/all natural," many rave reviews, viral social-media sharing, "FDA-approved facility" — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/avoiding-products-contaminated-hidden-ingredients); [FDA Rhino release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fda-warns-consumers-to-avoid-rhino-male-enhancement-products-found-at-retailers-because-of-undeclared-and-potentially-dangerous-drug-ingredients-300755962.html)
- Red flag — "Made in USA" and label warnings are not protective: most adulterated Sacramento products claimed US manufacture and 97% carried warning statements — [Fischer et al. 2023](https://www.dovepress.com/cross-sectional-survey-of-adulterated-sexual-enhancement-products-sold-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDS)
- Red flag — undisclosed quantities/proprietary blends: only 22% of yohimbine products listed quantity and 4.1% were accurate — [MedPage Today](https://www.medpagetoday.com/endocrinology/generalendocrinology/53660); [VUMC](https://www.vumc.org/poison-control/toxicology-question-week/feb-15-2022-yohimbine-or-no-himbine-potentially-fatal-over-counter); 38% of ingredients in top online ED supplements had no published studies — [PMC9555224](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9555224/)
- Red flag — eBay/Amazon/Walmart marketplace listings: FDA says contaminated products "are increasingly appearing" there, and 2024–2026 notices repeatedly cite ebay.com — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/avoiding-products-contaminated-hidden-ingredients); [FDA 10 Day Forecast](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/10-day-forecast-may-be-harmful-due-hidden-ingredients); [FDA Amazon warning letter 2023](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/amazoncom-inc-662503-12202023)
- Green-flag steps — search the FDA Health Fraud Product Database first — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/health-fraud-scams/health-fraud-product-database); verify product and lot in a certifier database/app (NSF Certified for Sport app; sport.wetestyoutrust.com; BSCG lot search; USP Verified Product Finder) — [USADA](https://www.usada.org/substances/supplement-connect/reduce-risk-testing-positive-experiencing-adverse-health-effects/); [Informed Sport](https://sport.wetestyoutrust.com/about/sport-vs-choice); [BSCG](https://www.bscg.org/bscg-banned-substance-testing-menu-v070725); [USP](https://www.usp.org/verification-services/verified-mark); prefer certifiers whose panel explicitly covers PDE5 inhibitors and that test every lot — [BSCG comparison](https://www.bscg.org/blogs/single/independent-supplement-testing-what-it-is-and-who-does-it)
- Medical-interaction checks — do not combine L-arginine/L-citrulline with PDE5 inhibitors or nitrates; use caution with antihypertensives, anticoagulants and diabetes drugs — [Mayo L-arginine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-l-arginine/art-20364681); [RxList L-citrulline](https://www.rxlist.com/supplements/l-citrulline.htm); avoid yohimbine with hypertension, anxiety, stimulants — [OPSS](https://www.opss.org/article/yohimbe-and-yohimbine-dietary-supplement-products); avoid DHEA and ashwagandha with hormone-sensitive prostate cancer — [Mayo DHEA](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-dhea/art-20364199); [NCCIH Ashwagandha](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha); keep zinc ≤40 mg/day total — [NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-Consumer/)
- Clinical framing — ED can be an early sign of heart disease; men under 50 with unexplained ED should be screened before treatment — [Mayo Clinic Q&A](https://newsnetwork.mayoclinic.org/discussion/mayo-clinic-q-and-a-some-products-that-claim-to-treat-erectile-dysfunction-can-be-harmful/); report suspected reactions to FDA MedWatch — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/anaconda-premium-500k-may-be-harmful-due-hidden-ingredient)

### Inferences
- Checklist synthesized from the above: (1) Do not buy anything marketed as a male-enhancement/stamina pill, honey pack, coffee or candy, especially single-dose packs from gas stations, convenience stores, eBay or third-party Amazon sellers. (2) Treat "works in 30 minutes," "lasts days," "natural Viagra/Cialis," "no prescription needed," and huge potency numbers as evidence of hidden drugs. (3) Discount "all natural," "made in USA," "FDA-approved facility," and label warnings as safety signals. (4) Before buying any single-ingredient product, check FDA's Health Fraud Product Database and confirm the exact product and lot number in a certifier database (BSCG, Informed Sport, NSF Certified for Sport; USP/NSF Contents Certified/ConsumerLab/Labdoor for identity and contaminants). (5) Avoid proprietary blends and any product listing yohimbe/yohimbine or undisclosed quantities. (6) Ask for a lot-specific certificate of analysis from an ISO 17025 lab that includes a PDE5-inhibitor screen; absence of one is a red flag. (7) Do not combine nitric-oxide precursors with PDE5 inhibitors or nitrates; review all supplements with a clinician or pharmacist, particularly if taking antihypertensives, alpha-blockers, anticoagulants, diabetes drugs or hormone therapies. (8) Get a medical evaluation for ED rather than self-treating; FDA-approved generic sildenafil/tadalafil exist. (9) Report any chest pain, severe headache, fainting or prolonged erection after a product to emergency services and MedWatch.
- The "certificate of analysis" and "ISO 17025 PDE5 screen" items are inferences from certifier practices and Amazon's compliance requirement; no consumer-health authority was found that explicitly tells consumers to request a COA.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed validation exists of a consumer red-flag checklist for sexual-enhancement supplements; the predictors above (channel, packaging, naming) come from a single convenience-sample study (Sacramento, 2016–2017) and FDA's pattern descriptions.
- The onset-time heuristic ("works in 30 minutes" implies a drug) is a pharmacological inference, not a documented regulatory criterion.
