# Nitric-oxide-pathway supplements for erectile function: L-arginine, L-citrulline, Pycnogenol / Prelox, dietary nitrate (beetroot) and arginine combinations

Scope note: human clinical data only, with animal/in-vitro data labelled where mentioned. Research date: 2026-11-07. Several publisher sites (OUP, Elsevier) and PubMed were not reachable from this session, so abstracts were taken from Europe PMC / PMC mirrors; where only an abstract was available this is stated. "IIEF-EF" = erectile-function domain of the International Index of Erectile Function (range 1–30; mild ED 17–25, moderate 11–16, severe 6–10; minimal clinically important difference about 4 points). "IIEF-5/SHIM" = 5-item version (range 5–25).

## Key Question 1: What do the best meta-analyses and RCTs of L-arginine alone actually show, and how large is the IIEF improvement in points?

### Takeaway
L-arginine at roughly 2.5–6 g/day for 6–12 weeks produces a real but modest improvement in mild-to-moderate ED (pooled odds of "improvement" about 3.4-fold vs placebo; absolute IIEF-EF gains of about +3 points overall and +5 points in mild ED), whereas 1.5 g/day is no better than placebo; no meta-analysis reports a pooled IIEF-EF point estimate for arginine monotherapy, and the trials are small and heterogeneous.

### Cited Findings
- Rhim et al. 2019 (J Sex Med 16:223–234; Korean university urology group) pooled 10 RCTs / 540 men with mild-to-moderate ED comparing arginine supplements (alone or combined with other substances, with subgroup analyses for each) against placebo or no treatment; arginine at 1,500–5,000 mg/day "significantly improved ED" (OR 3.37, 95% CI 1.29–8.77, P = .01, I² = 44); IIEF subdomain scores for erectile function, orgasmic function, intercourse satisfaction and overall satisfaction improved significantly while sexual desire did not; adverse-effect rate 8.3% vs 2.3% placebo, none severe; authors conclude "Arginine supplements can be recommended to patients with mild to moderate ED" and note dose/duration heterogeneity as the main limitation; a correction was published in 2020 — [Rhim 2019, Europe PMC abstract](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30770070)
- Rhim 2019 abstract gives only odds ratios and "significant" weighted mean differences; the actual IIEF point estimates are in the paywalled full text (not retrievable here) — [Rhim 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30770070)
- Menafra et al. (2022) summarize Rhim 2019 differently from its own abstract: "supplementation with l-ARG at a daily dose ranging from 2.8 to 8 g, with supplementation schedules from 2 weeks to 6 months, significantly improved mild–moderate ED or overall ED… conversely, a very low dose (1.5 g) of l-ARG administered daily for a very short time (17 days) was ineffective" — [Menafra 2022 full text, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-021-01704-3); the Rhim abstract itself says "dosage ranging from 1,500 to 5,000 mg" — [Rhim 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30770070)
- Chen et al. 1999 (BJU Int; Tel Aviv): 50 men with confirmed organic ED, double-blind RCT, 5 g/day L-arginine for 6 weeks after a 2-week placebo run-in; 9/29 (31%) on arginine vs 2/17 (12%) on placebo reported significant subjective improvement; all objective variables (including penile haemodynamics) were unchanged; every responder had low baseline urinary NOx that doubled during treatment — [Chen 1999](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/10233492)
- Klotz et al. 1999 (Urol Int; Cologne): 32 men with mixed-type impotence, randomized placebo-controlled crossover, 3 × 500 mg/day (1.5 g/day) for 17 days per phase; 17% reported significant improvement on arginine vs 20% on placebo; "no statistical difference"; no drug-related adverse effects; conclusion: 1.5 g/day "is not better than placebo" — [Klotz 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10743698/)
- Menafra et al. 2022 (J Endocrinol Invest 45:941–961; Federico II University, Naples; multicentre double-blind RCT): 98 Caucasian men aged 20–73 with duplex-confirmed vasculogenic ED randomized to L-arginine 6 g/day (2 g vials three times daily after meals, Bioarginina, Farmaceutici Damor; n = 51) or placebo (n = 47) for 3 months; IIEF-6 median rose from 20 (16–22) to 24 (19–25.3) on arginine (p < 0.0001) and was unchanged on placebo; prevalence of moderate ED fell from 16/51 (31.4%) to 4/50 (8%) and 12/50 (24%) reached a no-ED score vs 0 at baseline; 74% of arginine-treated vs 17.8% of placebo-treated men improved at least one ED category; in mild–moderate vasculogenic ED, IIEF-6 rose 21 → 25 (+19%) and cavernous peak systolic velocity 27.2 → 33.6 cm/s; in severe vasculogenic ED IIEF-6 rose 18 → 21 (+16.7%) but PSV did not improve; drop-out 3.06%; funding "No funds, grants, or other support was received"; one author (RP) reports occasional consultant honoraria from Damor (the arginine manufacturer) outside the work — [Menafra 2022, Springer full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-021-01704-3); abstract also at [PMC8995264](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8995264/)
- Examine.com's summary of the Menafra trial: "Compared with placebo, 3 months of L-arginine supplementation reduced the prevalence of erectile dysfunction in Caucasian men… the prevalence of moderate ED decreased (from 16/51 to 4/50)" — [Examine study summary](https://examine.com/research-feed/study/db24rd/)
- Gallo et al. 2020 (Sex Med 8:178–185; 4 Italian centres; prospective randomized three-arm study, not placebo-controlled): 300 men (100 per arm) treated 12 weeks with daily L-arginine 2,500 mg, tadalafil 5 mg, or both; IIEF-EF rose +3.1 points with arginine (15 → 18.1), +6.0 with tadalafil (14.8 → 20.8) and +7.1 with the combination (14.9 → 22); by severity, arginine gave +5.4 (mild, 22.1 → 27.5; not significantly different from tadalafil +5.7), +2.5 (moderate, 13.5 → 16) and +0.1 (severe, 5.1 → 5.2); SEP3 "yes" responses rose from 43% to 54% on arginine vs 41% → 62.6% tadalafil and 42% → 63% combination; adverse events 11 vs 53 vs 67 (P < .0001), the commonest with arginine being insomnia (5%) — [Gallo 2020, PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)
- Barbonetti et al. 2024 network meta-analysis (J Sex Med 21:1054–1063; Italian academic andrology): 15 placebo-controlled RCTs / 1,000 men; ranked by SUCRA, L-arginine alone (52%) was the only single nutraceutical with statistically significant efficacy, below sildenafil (79%) and tadalafil (72%); "against a background of general ineffectiveness of most nutraceutical interventions, L-Arginine and the mix PLC + ALC appeared to be of some usefulness" — [Barbonetti 2024, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39279185)
- Petre et al. 2023 (Nutrients 15:3677; systematic review/meta-analysis of 23 RCTs plus analysis of 25 supplements sold in Italy): Panax ginseng, Tribulus terrestris and L-arginine were identified as able to improve male sexual function; arginine was the most commonly included effective ingredient (63% of products) "but it was always included in a dose below the minimally effective amount"; 80% of marketed products matched the "no expected efficacy" cluster — [Petre 2023 PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37686709/); [Examine summary of Petre 2023](https://examine.com/research-feed/study/9kKr39/)
- EAU Sexual and Reproductive Health guideline (weak recommendation): "Use supplements with L-arginine or ginseng daily in men with mild ED who refuse pharmacological treatment after counselling them that the improvement of EF could be mild" — [EAU guideline, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)

### Inferences
- The only placebo-controlled trials with validated IIEF outcomes for arginine alone at adequate dose (Menafra 6 g/day) show about a 4-point IIEF-6 median gain, i.e. roughly the minimal clinically important difference, with most men moving one severity category rather than normalizing (only 24% normalized, mostly from mild ED).
- The Rhim 2019 OR of 3.37 is driven by a mix of arginine-alone and combination products (Prelox, arginine + yohimbine); its abstract dose range (1.5–5 g) and the Menafra description (2.8–8 g) disagree, so "1.5 g/day works" cannot be inferred from it; the one trial at 1.5 g/day (Klotz) was negative.
- Dose-response is suggested but unproven: 1.5 g/day (negative), 2.5 g/day (+3.1 IIEF-EF, open-label), 5 g/day (31% vs 12% subjective responders), 6 g/day (+4 IIEF-6 vs placebo). No trial has compared arginine doses head-to-head.

### Gaps
- Pooled IIEF-EF weighted mean difference for arginine monotherapy vs placebo: not reported in any accessible abstract; Rhim 2019 full text and the 2020 correction could not be retrieved.
- Funding sources for Rhim 2019, Chen 1999, Klotz 1999 and Gallo 2020 were not stated in the material retrieved.
- Adverse-event results of the Menafra trial were systematically recorded but the extracted text did not include them.
- No Cochrane review of arginine for ED was found; Cochrane evidence cited by EAU concerns ginseng only.

## Key Question 2: What is the evidence for L-citrulline alone and combined with other agents?

### Takeaway
L-citrulline monotherapy rests on a single 24-man, single-blind, 1-month study (1.5 g/day) in which 50% vs 8.3% improved erection hardness from EHS 3 to 4; everything since (citrulline + resveratrol as PDE5i add-on, multi-ingredient "Icarifil", Pycnogenol combinations) is small or not citrulline-specific, and the 2024 network meta-analysis did not identify citrulline as effective.

### Cited Findings
- Cormio et al. 2011 (Urology 77:119–122; Foggia, Italy): single-blind pilot, 24 men (mean age 56.5 ± 9.8) with mild ED (EHS 3) took placebo for 1 month then L-citrulline 1.5 g/day for 1 month; EHS improved from 3 to 4 in 2/24 (8.3%) on placebo vs 12/24 (50%) on citrulline (P < .01); intercourses/month 1.37 ± 0.93 (baseline) → 1.53 ± 1.00 (placebo, P = .57) → 2.3 ± 1.37 (citrulline, P < .01); all responders "very satisfied"; no adverse events; authors state it is "less effective than phosphodiesterase type-5 enzyme inhibitors, at least in the short term" — [Cormio 2011, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21195829/)
- Shirai et al. 2018 (Sex Med 6:291–296; Juntendo University, Japan): randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover pilot (no washout) in men with SHIM < 16 despite on-demand PDE5i; 20 enrolled, 13 completed; L-citrulline 800 mg/day + trans-resveratrol 300 mg/day for 1 month (PDE5i continued); SHIM rose to 10.96 ± 1.21 vs 8.32 ± 1.21 baseline and 8.31 ± 1.23 placebo (both P < .05); EHS 2.31 → 2.56 (not significant, P = .79); Aging Male Symptoms sexual-domain unchanged; no adverse events; funding "None", no conflicts declared; authors note the citrulline dose was deliberately lower than Cormio's 1.5 g — [Shirai 2018, PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6302103/)
- Shirai et al. 2021 (World J Mens Health 39:e8; same group): randomized double-blind crossover pilot of a drink containing Testofen 600 mg/day plus L-citrulline, resveratrol and caffeine for 14 days in men with sexual dysfunction using PDE5i; reported improved sexual function (details limited to the abstract excerpt) — [Shirai 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8443991/)
- Cai et al. 2024 (J Clin Med 13:2564; Italian Society of Andrology, 3 urology units, Sept 2022–Mar 2023): randomized controlled trial of Icarifil (L-citrulline, L-carnitine, Eruca vesicaria, Panax ginseng, Tribulus terrestris, Turnera diffusa, taurine, vitamin E, zinc; 1 sachet/day) alone (n = 52), Icarifil + tadalafil 5 mg daily (n = 55), or tadalafil 5 mg daily (n = 57) for 3 months; IIEF-5 improved +4.1, +7.4 and +5.1 respectively (combination superior to each, p < 0.001); SEP improvement in 56.9%, 94.0% and 82.3%; no placebo arm; citrulline dose not stated in abstract — [Cai 2024, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11084434/)
- Stanislavov & Rohdewald 2015 (Minerva Urol Nefrol 67:27–32): randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind crossover in 50 men with moderate ED; a combination of Pycnogenol, roburins (oak-wood extract), L-arginine and L-citrulline for 1 month "restored erectile function to normal" (IIEF); no unwanted effects; doses not given in abstract; second author affiliated with University of Münster and a long-standing Pycnogenol researcher — [Stanislavov 2015, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25664962)
- Barbonetti 2024 NMA (15 RCTs, 1,000 men): apart from L-arginine, PLC + ALC combinations and PDE5i, "No other treatment regimen showed efficacy with statistical significance" — [Barbonetti 2024](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39279185); a 2026 consumer-health summary notes "citrulline was not among the regimens the authors identified as useful" — [GetOPT summary](https://getopt.com/l-citrulline-erectile-dysfunction)
- Examine.com's citrulline database lists exactly one ED study (24 participants) and grades citrulline for erectile dysfunction on that basis — [Examine citrulline page](https://examine.com/supplements/citrulline/)
- A registered randomized trial (NCT07642505, "L-Citrulline and Tadalafil for Mild to Moderate Erectile Dysfunction") plans to compare L-citrulline 1,500 mg/day, tadalafil and the combination; the registry page could not be opened from this session (HTTP 403), so status and sample size are unverified — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07642505](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07642505)
- Pharmacokinetic rationale (healthy volunteers, not ED patients): Schwedhelm et al. 2008 (Br J Clin Pharmacol 65:51–59; 20 volunteers, double-blind crossover, 1 week per regimen) found oral L-citrulline raised plasma L-arginine AUC and Cmax more effectively than oral L-arginine itself (P < 0.01); citrulline 3 g twice daily raised the arginine/ADMA ratio from 186 ± 8 to 278 ± 14 and increased urinary nitrate (92 → 125 µmol/mmol creatinine) and cGMP (38 → 50 nmol/mmol creatinine); no regimen improved flow-mediated dilation — [Schwedhelm 2008, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2291275/)
- Moinard et al. 2008 "Citrudose" (Br J Nutr 99:855–862; 8 healthy men): single oral doses of 2, 5, 10 or 15 g citrulline caused no side effects at any dose; peak plasma arginine rose from 146 to 303 µmol/L depending on dose; at the highest doses citrulline accumulated and arginine "increased less than expected", suggesting saturation of renal conversion — [Moinard 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17953788/)
- Mechanism context (in vitro / in vivo, labelled as such): citrulline is not catabolized by intestinal/hepatic arginase and acts as an allosteric arginase inhibitor, which is why it bypasses the "arginine paradox" of poor oral arginine bioavailability — [MDPI Applied Sciences review 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/11/7/3293); a 2026 rat pharmacokinetic study estimated oral bioavailability 82.2% for citrulline vs 52.6% for arginine (animal data) — [Song 2026, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13581722/)

### Inferences
- The entire human ED-specific evidence base for citrulline alone is one unblinded-to-investigator, non-randomized sequence (placebo month then active month) in 24 men with mild ED; a 50% "responder" rate on a 4-point hardness scale is encouraging but has never been replicated, and the 2024 NMA could not show efficacy.
- The 3–6 g/day citrulline doses recommended by consumer sources come from blood-pressure and exercise studies, not ED trials; the only ED trial used 1.5 g/day.
- Pharmacokinetic data make citrulline a plausible, better-tolerated way to raise plasma arginine (3 g twice daily roughly doubles the arginine-to-ADMA ratio), but no study has shown that this translates into erectile benefit beyond Cormio's pilot.

### Gaps
- No double-blind, parallel-group, placebo-controlled RCT of citrulline monotherapy for ED with IIEF outcomes exists; no dose-ranging data for ED.
- The citrulline dose in Icarifil and in the Stanislavov 2015 combination is not reported in the abstracts retrieved.
- Status, enrolment and results of NCT07642505 could not be verified.

## Key Question 3: What is the evidence for Pycnogenol alone and for Prelox (L-arginine + Pycnogenol), including trial sizes, doses and funding?

### Takeaway
No RCT of Pycnogenol monotherapy for ED was identified; the Prelox combination (typically 80 mg Pycnogenol + 2.8–3 g L-arginine aspartate per day) has three small RCTs (n = 24–124, 1–6 months) reporting large IIEF-EF gains (pooled MD +8.9 points vs placebo), but the trials are manufacturer-linked (Horphag Research), small, and judged to carry unclear-to-high risk of bias.

### Cited Findings
- Stanislavov & Nikolova 2003 (J Sex Marital Ther 29:207–213; Sofia, Bulgaria; open-label, uncontrolled): 40 men aged 25–45 "without confirmed organic erectile dysfunction" took Sargenor (arginyl aspartate, 1.7 g L-arginine/day) for 3 months; 40 mg Pycnogenol twice daily was added in month 2 and three 40-mg tablets (120 mg) in month 3; normal erections in 5% after arginine alone (not significant), 80% after month 2 and 92.5% after month 3; "without any side effects" — [Stanislavov 2003, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12851125)
- Stanislavov, Nikolova & Rohdewald 2008 (Int J Impot Res 20:173–180): randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover, 50 men with mild-to-moderate ED, 1 month per phase, Prelox (L-arginine aspartate + Pycnogenol); "Intake of Pycnogenol for 1 month restored erectile function to normal. Intercourse frequency doubled"; sperm e-NOS and blood testosterone increased, cholesterol and blood pressure fell; no unwanted effects — [Stanislavov 2008, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/17703218); the daily Prelox dose in this trial was 80 mg Pycnogenol + 3 g L-arginine aspartate (two tablets morning and evening) — [Frontiers 2023 meta-analysis, Table 1 footnote](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/); the manufacturer's site states that after a one-month washout "this effect largely faded" — [Prelox.com research page](https://www.prelox.com/mobile/research)
- Ledda et al. 2010 (BJU Int 106:1030–1033; Chieti-Pescara, Italy): double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-arm study of Prelox ("a formulation of pine bark extract and l-arginine aspartate; Horphag Research UK Ltd, London, UK") in 124 men aged 30–50 with moderate ED for 6 months; IIEF erectile domain rose from 15.2 (SD 6.6) to 25.2 (2.1) at 3 months and 27.1 (2.1) at 6 months with Prelox vs 15.1 (7.0) → 19.1 (3.0) → 19.0 (3.1) with placebo (P < 0.05); total testosterone rose 15.9 → 18.9 nmol/L vs 16.9 → 17.3; authors conclude the effect "persists on continuous therapy for up to 6 months" and "continues to improve the longer the therapy is used" — [Ledda 2010, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20184576); dose: each tablet 20 mg Pycnogenol + 700 mg L-arginine aspartate, two tablets morning and evening (80 mg + 2.8 g/day), 54 active vs 57 placebo analysed — [Frontiers 2023 meta-analysis Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)
- Aoki et al. 2012 (Phytother Res 26:204–207; Japan; co-author F. Schönlau also co-authored the Horphag-linked Prelox review): double-blind parallel-group study in Japanese men with mild-to-moderate ED (12 active vs 12 placebo) of Pycnogenol 60 mg/day + L-arginine 690 mg/day + aspartic acid 552 mg/day for 8 weeks; IIEF-5 total improved with "marked improvement" in hardness of erection and intercourse satisfaction items, blood pressure and liver enzymes fell, salivary testosterone rose slightly; no adverse reactions — [Aoki 2012, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21618639/); the 2023 meta-analysis notes that in this trial IIEF erectile domain, satisfaction, desire and IIEF-5 differences vs placebo were not statistically significant, "which may be related to the low dose" — [Frontiers 2023 meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)
- Frontiers in Endocrinology 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis of Pycnogenol + L-arginine (PAL) vs placebo (Chengdu, China; funded by Chengdu Science and Technology Project 2019-YF09-00185-SN; authors declare no commercial conflicts): 3 RCTs, 184 patients (91 active / 93 placebo); IIEF erectile-domain MD +8.9 points (95% CI 4.14–13.66, P = 0.0002, I² = 0%, 2 studies: Ledda 2010 and Stanislavov 2008); intercourse satisfaction MD +4.96 (2.17–7.75); orgasmic function +3.63 (1.74–5.51); overall satisfaction +3.61 (1.72–5.50); sexual desire +3.99 (2.03–5.95); testosterone MD +2.73 (−2.70 to 8.16, not significant); by duration, MD +13.14 at 1 month, +9.34 at 1–3 months and +6.95 at 3–6 months; risk of bias: one study low, one unclear, one high; no adverse events in any trial; Egger's test for the erectile domain P = 0.033 although authors state no publication bias — [Frontiers 2023 meta-analysis, PMC10614297](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)
- Examine.com's summary of the arginine + Pycnogenol evidence: "Supplementation with arginine and Pycnogenol seems to have improved erectile function to a clinically significant degree" — [Examine study summary](https://examine.com/research-feed/study/15xM8d/)
- Horphag Research (the Pycnogenol and Prelox owner) describes Prelox as "a proprietary blend of L-Arginine and Pycnogenol" whose synergy "is patented"; its research page cites the 40-man study (5% → 80%), an unpublished-in-PubMed study by Steven Lamm (81% improved after 6 weeks, 78% judged it effective), the 50-man crossover (effect "largely faded" after discontinuation) and a 6-month study it describes as 110 men showing "no habituation effects" — [Prelox.com research page](https://www.prelox.com/mobile/research)
- Pycnogenol alone (not ED outcomes): a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled crossover in patients with stable coronary artery disease tested Pycnogenol 200 mg/day for 8 weeks on endothelial function — [Enseleit 2012, Eur Heart J, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22240497/); Pycnogenol augmented endothelium-dependent forearm vasodilation in a human study — [Nishioka 2007, Hypertens Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18037769/)
- Examine.com lists Pycnogenol's usual dose as 100–200 mg/day split into two or three doses, and its database for "Male Sexual Dysfunction" contains a single study — [Examine Pycnogenol page](https://examine.com/supplements/pycnogenol/)
- Petre 2023 graded Pinus pinaster (the Pycnogenol source) as one of six "A"-level ingredients (benefit reported in at least 2 studies), alongside arginine, yohimbine, tongkat ali, Panax ginseng and Tribulus — [Examine summary of Petre 2023](https://examine.com/research-feed/study/9kKr39/)

### Inferences
- The Prelox IIEF-EF gain (+8.9 pooled; +8 at 6 months in Ledda vs placebo) is numerically as large as that reported for on-demand PDE5 inhibitors, which is implausible for a supplement whose arginine component alone yields about +3–4 points; the two trials driving the estimate come from the same manufacturer-linked network (Rohdewald/Schönlau/Horphag), one is a 1-month crossover with no washout data in the abstract, and the placebo arm in Ledda showed almost no change at 6 months (19.0 vs 15.1 baseline is itself a 4-point placebo gain but far below typical). Independent replication is lacking.
- The Japanese trial at roughly one-quarter of the Prelox arginine dose (690 mg) and 60 mg Pycnogenol was not significantly better than placebo on the IIEF erectile domain, consistent with the arginine dose-response seen elsewhere.
- Pycnogenol's own contribution cannot be isolated: no RCT tests Pycnogenol alone for ED, and the only sequential design (Stanislavov 2003) was uncontrolled.

### Gaps
- Funding statements for Stanislavov 2003/2008/2015 and Aoki 2012 were not available in abstracts; Horphag involvement is inferred from product naming, authorship and the manufacturer's own citation of the trials.
- The Lamm study cited by Horphag (European Endocrinology 2009; European Bulletin of Drug Research 2003) is not indexed in PubMed and could not be assessed.
- No data on Prelox or Pycnogenol in severe ED, diabetic ED, or men over 50 (Ledda's cohort was 30–50 years).

## Key Question 4: Is there human evidence that dietary nitrate or beetroot improves erectile function?

### Takeaway
No randomized or controlled human trial of beetroot or inorganic nitrate with an erectile-function outcome was found in PubMed-indexed literature; the only human evidence is indirect (improved brachial/femoral flow-mediated dilation and lower blood pressure in some, but not all, nitrate trials, plus observational links between vegetable intake and lower ED risk).

### Cited Findings
- Paper-index searches for "dietary nitrate / beetroot" combined with "erectile" returned only vascular-function trials and no ED trial; a consumer-health review states plainly: "There is no evidence that beetroot works for erectile dysfunction (ED); however… beets can increase the amount of nitric oxide" — [Ro Health, beetroot for men](https://ro.co/erectile-dysfunction/beetroot-beet-juice-for-men/)
- Endothelial-function evidence: a systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs found inorganic nitrate/beetroot improved endothelial function with the effect size significantly associated with nitrate dose — [Lara 2016, Eur J Nutr](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25764393/); 6 weeks of daily nitrate-rich beetroot juice in 69 untreated hypercholesterolaemic adults increased FMD by an absolute 1.1% (about 24% relative) vs a 0.3% worsening on nitrate-depleted juice (P < 0.001), reduced platelet-monocyte aggregates and caused no adverse effects — [Velmurugan 2016, Am J Clin Nutr](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4691670/); a single 140 mL (800 mg nitrate) dose in 15 healthy men aged 69 ± 4 increased superficial femoral artery FMD (+1.18% vs +0.23%, p = 0.002) 2.5 h later — [Walker 2019, Nutrients](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6566150/)
- Null evidence: 4 weeks of 2 × ~400 mg/day nitrate from beetroot juice in 15 treated hypertensive adults aged 56–71 increased plasma nitrate/nitrite but did not improve forearm blood-flow responses or 24-h, home or clinic blood pressure — [Fejes 2024, Food Funct](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11034575/); a single dose of beetroot powder (200 mg nitrate) with or without 0.8–3 g L-arginine did not change postprandial FMD in 18 abdominally obese men — [Smeets 2020, Nutrients](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7697292/)
- The only chronic trial combining citrulline with a nitrate source in men (Burgos et al.; 32 men, 9 weeks; citrulline 3 g/day and/or beetroot extract providing 100 mg nitrate/day) measured testosterone/cortisol ratio, not erectile function — [summarized in a 2025 Nutrients systematic review](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/17/24/3954)
- Long-term nitrate supplementation data exist only for vascular/physiological endpoints: 12 weeks of beetroot juice with 380 mg nitrate/day — [Miller 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10148922/); 13-week dose-ranging pilot in older overweight adults in which moderate doses had greater BP/endothelial effects than the high dose — [Babateen 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36958967/)
- Observational (not nitrate-specific): in a Mediterranean cohort, vegetables at least once daily (OR 0.47, 95% CI 0.28–0.77) and nuts more than twice weekly (OR 0.41, 0.25–0.67) were inversely associated with ED — [Ramírez 2016, Intern Med J](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26482327/); in 589 men with coronary disease (76% with ED), each additional daily vegetable serving was associated with about a 0.7-point higher IIEF score and a low-fat diet with about 2 points — [Biernikiewicz 2026, J Clin Med](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13115890/); healthy plant-based diet indices were associated with lower incident ED in the Health Professionals Follow-Up Study (men 60–<70) and lower ED prevalence in NHANES — [Yang 2022, BJU Int](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474604/); [Carto 2022, Urology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34979217/)
- NIH ODS: most beetroot studies use about 500 mL/day (about 2 cups) of juice, once 2.5–3 h before exercise or daily for up to 15 days; "Studies have not identified any safety concerns with the consumption of beetroot juice in moderate amounts (about 2 cups/day) for several weeks," the nitrate in which is "less than half the total nitrate consumption from a diet rich in vegetables and fruits"; beeturia (pink/red urine) is harmless; "No research has assessed longer term supplementation… beyond several weeks" — [NIH ODS, Exercise and Athletic Performance fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/)
- Examine.com gives the ergogenic dose as 70–500 mL beetroot juice (about 250–1,000 mg nitrate) — [Examine nitrate page](https://examine.com/supplements/nitrate/)

### Inferences
- Claims that beetroot is a "natural Viagra" are extrapolated from endothelial and blood-pressure studies; the magnitude of FMD change (about +1 percentage point) is similar to that seen with other NO-pathway nutrients that have modest or no erectile benefit, so any effect on erections is unproven and likely small.
- Because dietary nitrate works through the nitrate–nitrite–NO pathway (independent of NOS and arginine), it is mechanistically the most relevant option when the problem is reduced NOS activity, but this remains hypothesis, not evidence, for ED.

### Gaps
- No RCT, cohort or case series with an erectile outcome for beetroot or inorganic nitrate was identified; no data on tolerance or loss of effect for erectile endpoints.
- No study of dietary nitrate combined with PDE5 inhibitors in men with ED (relevant because both potentiate the NO–cGMP pathway).

## Key Question 5: What is the evidence for L-arginine combined with other nutrients or added to PDE5 inhibitors?

### Takeaway
Adding daily L-arginine (2.5–5 g) to daily tadalafil 5 mg or sildenafil consistently gives a further 1–1.5 IIEF-EF/IIEF-5 points over the drug alone in randomized (mostly open or single-blind, Egyptian/Italian) trials, and the 2024 network meta-analysis ranks arginine + tadalafil above either alone in organic ED; older on-demand combinations (arginine + yohimbine, arginine aspartate + AMP) showed small benefits in mild-to-moderate but not severe ED.

### Cited Findings
- Xu et al. 2021 meta-analysis (Andrologia 53:e14007): 4 RCTs, 373 patients; arginine + PDE5i improved sexual-function index more than arginine alone (p < 0.00001) and PDE5i alone (p = 0.005) and raised total testosterone more; PDE5i alone beat arginine alone (p < 0.00001); adverse events did not differ between combination and PDE5i alone — [Xu 2021, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33587304)
- Barbonetti 2024 NMA: SUCRA ranking PLC + ALC + sildenafil 97% > L-arginine + tadalafil 84% > sildenafil 79% > tadalafil 72% > L-arginine 52%; in organic ED "the efficacy of Sildenafil and Tadalafil was significantly improved by PLC + ALC and L-Arginine, respectively," whereas in non-organic ED nutraceuticals did not improve daily tadalafil — [Barbonetti 2024](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39279185)
- Gallo 2020 (300 men, 12 weeks, randomized, no placebo): arginine 2.5 g + tadalafil 5 mg gave IIEF-EF +7.1 vs +6.0 for tadalafil alone; in severe ED +6.6 vs +4.9; adverse events 67 vs 53 (dyspepsia 14% vs 11%, insomnia 7 vs 0) — [Gallo 2020, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)
- El Taieb et al. 2019 (J Sex Med 16:1390–1397; Aswan, Egypt): double-blind RCT in 108 diabetic men with mild-to-moderate ED; daily L-arginine + tadalafil improved IIEF-5 and total testosterone more than either alone (P < .001); pharmaco-penile duplex results did not differ between groups — [El Taieb 2019, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31326304)
- Abu El-Hamd & Hegazy 2020 (Andrologia 52:e13640; Sohag, Egypt): single-blind placebo-controlled trial, 120 men aged ≥60 randomized to L-arginine 5 g/day, tadalafil 5 mg/day, both, or placebo (n = 30 each) for 6 weeks; SHIM and total testosterone rose in all active groups (p = .001) and were highest with the combination — [Abu El-Hamd 2020, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32441833)
- El-Wakeel et al. 2020 (Andrology 8:143–147; Cairo/Sohag): randomized parallel trial of sildenafil 50 mg every other day with or without L-arginine 3 g/day for 8 weeks in organic ED (mean age 56); IIEF-5 reached 19.8 ± 2.2 with the combination vs 18.5 ± 2.3 with sildenafil alone (p = 0.05) from a baseline of 15.3 ± 2.5; side effects similar except gastritis, more common with the combination — [El-Wakeel 2020, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31267684)
- Gallo et al. 2022 (Investig Clin Urol; single-blind RCT with 1-year follow-up): six weekly low-intensity shock-wave sessions with or without adjuvant daily tadalafil 5 mg + L-arginine 2,500 mg; IIEF-EF 16.0 → 24.8 (1 month) → 23.3 (6 months) → 21.6 (12 months) with adjuvant therapy vs 16.5 → 22.7 → 21.5 → 19.5 without; described as safe — [Gallo 2022, Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34983126)
- Lebret et al. 2002 (Eur Urol 41:608–613): double-blind, placebo-controlled three-way crossover, 45 men, on-demand 6 g L-arginine glutamate + 6 mg yohimbine 1–2 h before intercourse for 2-week periods; IIEF-EF 17.2 ± 7.2 (combination) vs 15.4 ± 6.5 (yohimbine) vs 14.1 ± 6.6 (placebo), p = 0.006; in men with baseline IIEF-EF > 14 the combination reached 22.2 vs 16.9 placebo, but in those ≤ 14 there was no benefit (12.4 vs 11.4) — [Lebret 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12074777/)
- Akhondzadeh et al. 2010 (Iran J Psychiatry; multicentre double-blind RCT): 40 married men aged 25–50 with mild-to-moderate ED (IIEF-EF 15–25) took an on-demand yohimbine + L-arginine capsule ("SX") or placebo for 4 weeks; difference significant at week 4 (P = 0.03); 4 adverse events, no difference between groups — [Akhondzadeh 2010, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3430403/)
- Neuzillet et al. 2013 (Andrology 1:223–228; Hôpital Foch, France): double-blind, placebo-controlled two-way crossover (phase II) in 26 men with mild-to-moderate ED; on-demand L-arginine aspartate 8 g + adenosine monophosphate 200 mg 1–2 h before intercourse improved all IIEF domains except desire and orgasm, plus EHS (p = 0.015) and EDITS (p = 0.017); tolerance no different from placebo — [Neuzillet 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23413135/)
- Gianfrilli et al. 2012 (Andrologia 44 Suppl 1:600–604; Sapienza, Rome): single-blind, single-arm study of propionyl-L-carnitine + L-arginine + niacin for 3 months in 54 treatment-naive men; 51 completed; IIEF improved by 5.7 ± 4.1 points (P < 0.01); erections improved in 40% by global assessment, partial response in up to 77% — [Gianfrilli 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21966881/); the same trio was tested with vardenafil in diabetic ED — [Gentile 2009, Curr Med Res Opin](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19624286/)
- Tahvilian et al. 2024 (J Pharmacopuncture): 98 men with ED randomized to a 500 mg herbal capsule of ginseng + Tribulus terrestris + L-arginine or placebo for 3 months (double-blind) — [Tahvilian 2024, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11194517/)
- Rhim 2019 noted that arginine was often tested in combination with Pycnogenol or yohimbine and ran subgroup analyses for arginine alone vs combined — [Rhim 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30770070)

### Inferences
- The incremental benefit of arginine on top of a PDE5 inhibitor (about +1 to +1.5 IIEF points; 19.8 vs 18.5 IIEF-5 in El-Wakeel, 22 vs 20.8 IIEF-EF in Gallo) is below the 4-point MCID, statistically fragile (p = 0.05 in El-Wakeel), and comes almost entirely from open-label or single-blind trials in two Italian and Egyptian groups; it is nonetheless the most consistent "positive" finding for arginine in the modern literature.
- Combination products add gastrointestinal and insomnia complaints (Gallo: insomnia 5–7% with arginine; El-Wakeel: more gastritis) rather than hypotension-type events, at these doses.
- On-demand high-dose arginine salts (6–8 g) with yohimbine or AMP work only in mild-to-moderate ED; severe ED (IIEF-EF ≤ 14) did not respond in Lebret 2002, mirroring the arginine-alone data.

### Gaps
- The arginine dose in El Taieb 2019 is not given in the abstract; the Mirone 2021 nutraceutical + tadalafil RCT (86 patients) and the 2024 ginseng/Tribulus/arginine trial results were available only as titles or truncated abstracts.
- No placebo-controlled trial of arginine + PDE5i uses a double-dummy design with blinded IIEF assessment beyond El Taieb 2019.

## Key Question 6: How do these effect sizes compare with PDE5 inhibitors and with placebo?

### Takeaway
At maximum on-demand doses PDE5 inhibitors raise IIEF-EF about 7.5–9.7 points versus placebo and daily tadalafil 5 mg about 5.4 points, whereas arginine alone adds about 3–4 points, citrulline alone has no IIEF data, and only the manufacturer-linked Prelox trials report PDE5i-sized gains (+8.9); placebo itself produces a small-to-moderate improvement (Hedges g 0.35) that uncontrolled supplement studies cannot subtract.

### Cited Findings
- Berner et al. 2006 meta-analysis of 14 fixed-dose RCTs: IIEF improvement vs placebo was 9.65 points (95% CI 8.50–10.79) for sildenafil 100 mg, 8.52 (7.61–9.42) for tadalafil 20–25 mg and 7.50 (6.50–8.50) for vardenafil 20 mg; "At maximum dosage they improve erectile function 7–10 points on the IIEF compared to placebo-treatment" — [Berner 2006, Int J Impot Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16239897/)
- Porst et al. 2014 integrated analysis of 1,913 patients from 6 RCTs of once-daily tadalafil: least-squares mean IIEF-EF change vs placebo 4.2 (2.5 mg) and 5.4 (5 mg); SEP3 success +17.8% and +23.6%; the MCID for IIEF-EF is ≥4 and placebo groups did not reach it in any subpopulation — [Porst 2014, Eur Urol](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0302283813010245)
- Pyrgidis et al. 2021 overview of 23 systematic reviews (154,796 participants): sildenafil 25 mg WMD 13.08 (95% CI 10.1–16.06) vs placebo in the (few) low-dose studies; sildenafil 50/100 mg judged most effective overall; low-dose daily tadalafil may be slightly more effective than high-dose on-demand tadalafil (WMD 1.24, 0.03–2.44) — [Pyrgidis 2021, Front Pharmacol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8452927/)
- Chen et al. 2015 trade-off network meta-analysis (82 trials, 47,626 patients for efficacy): sildenafil 50 mg had the greatest efficacy but highest adverse-event rate; tadalafil 10 mg intermediate efficacy with the lowest adverse events — [Chen 2015, Eur Urol](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25817916)
- Salonia et al. 2026 dose-response network meta-analysis (83 double-blind RCTs, 6,029 participants): odds of achieving IIEF-EF > 26 vs placebo were 9.06 for sildenafil 100 mg, 7.90 for sildenafil 50 mg, 7.13 for tadalafil 20 mg, 3.14 for tadalafil 10 mg, 7.78 for vardenafil 10 mg, 3.42 for avanafil 200 mg and 1.75 for avanafil 100 mg; adverse-event odds highest for vardenafil and lowest for tadalafil — [Salonia 2026, J Sex Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42296271/)
- Placebo response: in 63 double-blind PDE5i trials (12,564 men, mean age 55) the placebo arm improved IIEF-EF with a small-to-moderate effect (Hedges g 0.35, SE 0.03, P < .001), larger in PTSD-related ED (g 0.78) — [Stridh 2020, JAMA Netw Open](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7084170/)
- Direct head-to-head within one trial: arginine 2.5 g/day vs tadalafil 5 mg/day gave IIEF-EF +3.1 vs +6.0 overall, +5.4 vs +5.7 in mild ED (not significantly different), +2.5 vs +6.7 in moderate and +0.1 vs +4.9 in severe ED, with 11 vs 53 adverse events; authors suggest arginine as "an equally effective, cheaper, and safer alternative to tadalafil 5 mg in patients affected by mild ED" — [Gallo 2020, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)
- Network ranking across nutraceuticals and drugs: sildenafil SUCRA 79%, tadalafil 72%, L-arginine 52%; no single nutraceutical other than arginine reached significance — [Barbonetti 2024](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39279185)
- Prelox pooled IIEF-EF MD +8.9 (4.14–13.66) vs placebo from 2 manufacturer-linked trials — [Frontiers 2023 meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/); Cormio describes citrulline as "less effective than phosphodiesterase type-5 enzyme inhibitors" — [Cormio 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21195829/)

### Inferences
- Ranking by placebo-adjusted IIEF-EF gain: on-demand sildenafil 100 mg / tadalafil 20 mg (+8.5 to +9.7) > daily tadalafil 5 mg (+5.4) > L-arginine 6 g/day (+4 median IIEF-6, Menafra) ≈ arginine 2.5 g/day in mild ED (+5.4, unadjusted) > arginine 2.5 g/day overall (+3.1, unadjusted) > citrulline (no IIEF data). The Prelox estimate (+8.9) is an outlier that should be weighted by its risk of bias and sponsorship.
- Because open-label supplement trials (Gallo, Cai, Gianfrilli) do not subtract the placebo response (g 0.35, roughly 2–3 IIEF-EF points in typical trials), their within-group changes of +3 to +6 points overstate true drug effect; the only placebo-controlled arginine estimate (Menafra) is the fairest comparison and lands at roughly half to two-thirds of daily tadalafil 5 mg.
- Tolerability favours arginine (11 vs 53 adverse events per 100 men over 12 weeks in Gallo), which is the main argument for it in mild ED or when PDE5i are contraindicated.

### Gaps
- No trial compares citrulline, Prelox or beetroot directly with a PDE5 inhibitor; NCT07642505 (citrulline vs tadalafil) is registered but unverified.
- IIEF-EF (not IIEF-5) placebo-adjusted estimates for arginine monotherapy are unavailable in point form from any meta-analysis abstract.

## Key Question 7: What is the evidence-based daily dose, time to effect, and who benefits most (mild vs moderate vs severe ED)?

### Takeaway
The best-supported regimens are L-arginine 2.5–6 g/day in divided doses for at least 6–12 weeks (mild and moderate, especially organic/vasculogenic ED; little or no effect in severe ED), L-citrulline 1.5 g/day for 4 weeks (mild ED only, single study), and Prelox-type dosing of 80 mg Pycnogenol with about 3 g L-arginine aspartate daily for 1–6 months (mild-to-moderate ED, industry-linked data); no ED dose exists for beetroot/nitrate.

### Cited Findings
- Negative at 1.5 g/day × 17 days (Klotz 1999) — [Klotz 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10743698/); positive at 2.5 g/day × 12 weeks in mild and moderate ED but +0.1 points in severe ED (Gallo 2020) — [Gallo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/); positive at 3 g/day × 8 weeks as sildenafil add-on (El-Wakeel 2020) — [El-Wakeel 2020](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31267684); positive at 5 g/day × 6 weeks only in men with low baseline NOx (Chen 1999) — [Chen 1999](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/10233492); positive at 5 g/day × 6 weeks in men ≥ 60 (Abu El-Hamd 2020) — [Abu El-Hamd 2020](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32441833); positive at 6 g/day (2 g × 3 after meals) × 3 months in vasculogenic ED, with normalization mainly in baseline-mild cases and no haemodynamic (PSV) gain in severe vasculogenic ED (Menafra 2022) — [Menafra 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-021-01704-3)
- Rhim 2019 restricted inclusion to mild-to-moderate ED and found efficacy at 1,500–5,000 mg; Menafra's reading of the same review: effective range 2.8–8 g/day over 2 weeks–6 months — [Rhim 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30770070); [Menafra 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40618-021-01704-3)
- Barbonetti 2024: arginine's add-on benefit to tadalafil appears in organic ED but not in non-organic ED — [Barbonetti 2024](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39279185); Lebret 2002: on-demand arginine glutamate + yohimbine helped only men with baseline IIEF-EF > 14 — [Lebret 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12074777/)
- Citrulline: 1.5 g/day for 1 month improved hardness in 50% of men with mild ED (EHS 3) — [Cormio 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21195829/); 800 mg/day + resveratrol 300 mg for 1 month improved SHIM about 2.6 points in PDE5i-refractory men — [Shirai 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6302103/); Cleveland Clinic's general "safe dosage" is 3–6 g/day starting low — [Cleveland Clinic citrulline](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/citrulline-benefits); Examine: studied range 1–24 g/day — [Examine citrulline](https://examine.com/supplements/citrulline/)
- Prelox: 80 mg Pycnogenol + 3 g L-arginine aspartate/day for 1 month (Stanislavov 2008) or 80 mg + 2.8 g/day for 6 months (Ledda 2010), both in mild-to-moderate ED aged 30–50; 60 mg + 690 mg/day for 8 weeks was not significantly better than placebo on the erectile domain (Aoki 2012) — [Frontiers 2023 meta-analysis Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/); original 2003 protocol used 1.7 g arginine with 80 then 120 mg Pycnogenol — [Stanislavov 2003](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12851125)
- Time course: Prelox IIEF-EF gains were present at 1 month (crossover) and 3 months (25.2) and larger at 6 months (27.1) — [Ledda 2010](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20184576); arginine effects were measured at 6 weeks (Chen, Abu El-Hamd), 8 weeks (El-Wakeel) and 12 weeks (Gallo, Menafra); Cleveland Clinic states full effects of regular arginine "could take up to three months" — [Cleveland Clinic L-arginine](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/drugs/22536-l-arginine)
- Examine on arginine formulation: splitting the daily dose "can help reduce adverse effects while maintaining elevated arginine levels throughout the day"; doses studied 1.5–30 g/day — [Examine arginine](https://examine.com/supplements/arginine/)

### Inferences
- A practical evidence-based arginine regimen is 2.5–3 g/day (lowest dose with randomized positive data, 11 adverse events per 100 men over 12 weeks) up to 6 g/day in three divided doses after meals (only placebo-controlled positive trial), judged at 8–12 weeks; 1.5 g/day should be regarded as sub-therapeutic.
- Men most likely to benefit: mild ED (IIEF-EF 17–25), vasculogenic/organic aetiology with intact NOS capacity, and men already on daily tadalafil wanting a small further gain; men with severe ED, diabetic severe vasculopathy or psychogenic ED have little evidence of benefit.
- For citrulline the only evidence-based ED dose is 1.5 g/day; the 3–6 g/day doses widely recommended are extrapolated from pharmacokinetic (3 g twice daily doubled arginine/ADMA ratio) and blood-pressure studies.

### Gaps
- No dose-ranging ED trial for arginine, citrulline or Pycnogenol; no trial of citrulline ≥ 3 g/day in ED; no data on Prelox beyond 6 months or in men over 50.

## Key Question 8: Is there evidence of tolerance, loss of effect or safety problems with long-term daily use, and what are the side effects and drug interactions per ingredient?

### Takeaway
No ED trial exceeds 6 months (Prelox) or 3 months (arginine alone), and within that window no tachyphylaxis was reported (Prelox benefit grew to 6 months but faded within a month of stopping); the serious safety signal is L-arginine after acute myocardial infarction (VINTAGE MI: 6 deaths vs 0 at 9 g/day), while everyday risks are dose-related GI upset (arginine > 9 g single doses), additive hypotension with nitrates, antihypertensives or PDE5 inhibitors, hyperkalaemia with potassium-sparing diuretics, and, for citrulline, mainly GI symptoms even at 15 g.

### Cited Findings
- Durability: Prelox effect "persists on continuous therapy for up to 6 months" and "continues to improve the longer the therapy is used" — [Ledda 2010](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20184576); manufacturer states "no habituation effects occur… the benefits do not fade" over 6 months, but also that after a 1-month washout in the crossover study "this effect largely faded" — [Prelox.com](https://www.prelox.com/mobile/research); adjuvant arginine 2.5 g + tadalafil 5 mg after shock-wave therapy was "safe" over 12 months of follow-up (treatment duration not specified in abstract) — [Gallo 2022](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34983126)
- NIH ODS on arginine: "Most study results suggest that up to 9 g/day arginine for several days or weeks is safe and well tolerated. At doses of 9–30 g/day, the most commonly reported adverse reactions are gastrointestinal discomfort, such as diarrhea and nausea, and slightly reduced blood pressure… The safety of taking high-dose arginine supplements for more than 3 months is not known" — [NIH ODS fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/)
- Grimble 2007 (J Nutr): single arginine doses of 3–6 g rarely provoke side effects; most GI side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea) occur at single doses > 9 g (> 140 mg/kg), often within regimens > 30 g/day, and "disappeared if divided doses were ingested"; oral arginine and citrulline raise intestinal NO, the mechanism of many laxatives — [Grimble 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17513449/)
- Examine on arginine safety: usually well tolerated; diarrhoea most often with single doses ≥ 9 g; a one-time dose up to 7.5 g or a daily dose up to 20 g "is unlikely to cause side effects"; probable minor interaction with blood-pressure-lowering drugs (additive effect); listed interactions also include blood thinners and drugs that cause hyperkalaemia; caution in people prone to hypotension — [Examine arginine](https://examine.com/supplements/arginine/)
- VINTAGE MI (Schulman et al., JAMA 2006; single-centre double-blind RCT, NCT00051376): 153 patients after a first ST-elevation MI randomized to L-arginine (goal 3 g three times daily) or placebo for 6 months; no improvement in vascular stiffness or ejection fraction; 6 deaths (8.6%) in the arginine group vs none on placebo (P = .01), five in patients ≥ 60 years; enrolment closed for safety; "L-arginine should not be recommended following acute myocardial infarction" — [VINTAGE MI, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16391217/); [JAMA full text](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/202136)
- Mayo Clinic (page dated March 16, 2026): L-arginine "isn't recommended for people who have had a recent heart attack due to concerns that the supplement might increase the risk of death"; can worsen allergies or asthma; oral side effects nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, bloating, gout, headache; interactions: anticoagulants/antiplatelets (bleeding), blood-pressure drugs (hypotension), diabetes drugs (lower glucose), isoproterenol, nitrates ("might cause your blood pressure to become too low"), potassium-sparing diuretics (amiloride, spironolactone, triamterene: hyperkalaemia), and sildenafil ("might cause your blood pressure to become too low") — [Mayo Clinic L-arginine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-l-arginine/art-20364681); Mayo's ED-supplement table adds "Don't take L-arginine with sildenafil" — [Mayo Clinic, dietary supplements for ED](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/erectile-dysfunction/in-depth/erectile-dysfunction-herbs/art-20044394)
- Cleveland Clinic (updated 03/22/2022): lists interactions with ACE inhibitors, alpha-blockers, ARBs, beta-blockers, calcium-channel blockers, nitrates (isosorbide, nitroglycerin), propranolol and fish oil; "You shouldn't take l-arginine if you: Recently had a heart attack. Have a guanidinoacetate methyltransferase deficiency…"; side effects bloating, diarrhoea, dizziness, nausea/vomiting, rare allergic reaction; states the "average dosage" is 6 g up to a maximum of 30 g/day in three divided doses — [Cleveland Clinic L-arginine](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/drugs/22536-l-arginine)
- Trial-level tolerability: arginine adverse-effect rate 8.3% vs 2.3% placebo, none severe (Rhim 2019) — [Rhim 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30770070); 11 events per 100 men over 12 weeks at 2.5 g/day (insomnia 5%, headache, dyspepsia, flushing 2% each) vs 53 with tadalafil 5 mg — [Gallo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/); gastritis more common when 3 g/day was added to sildenafil — [El-Wakeel 2020](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31267684); no drug-related adverse effects at 1.5 g/day — [Klotz 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10743698/)
- Citrulline tolerability: 15 g single doses caused no side effects in healthy men — [Moinard 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17953788/); "citrulline… did not induce gastrointestinal disorders at a high dose (i.e., 15 g)" and, unlike arginine, is not catabolized by arginase — [MDPI review 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/11/7/3293); Examine: "Generally safe, with some (but not all) studies reporting gastrointestinal symptoms as the main side effect… Theoretically, citrulline may interact with other agents that lower blood pressure"; quality concerns include citrulline-malate products with lower-than-claimed citrulline and a 2014 recall of a product containing no citrulline — [Examine citrulline](https://examine.com/supplements/citrulline/); NIH ODS: 6 of 41 weightlifters reported stomach discomfort after 8 g citrulline malate; up to 6 g/day for 4 weeks and 1.35 g/day for 6 weeks caused no adverse effects; "Studies have not adequately assessed the safety of citrulline… for months at a time" — [NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/); Cleveland Clinic: bloating, cramping, diarrhoea, sweating; start at the lowest dose — [Cleveland Clinic citrulline](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/citrulline-benefits)
- Pycnogenol safety (Examine): well tolerated with some reports of dizziness, drowsiness, rash and stomach complaints (the latter also seen with placebo); theoretical bleeding risk via platelet inhibition although "over 27 studies of pycnogenol had no reports of increased bleeding"; possible small reductions in fasting glucose (caution with hypoglycaemia) and listed interactions with blood-glucose- and blood-pressure-lowering drugs — [Examine Pycnogenol](https://examine.com/supplements/pycnogenol/); no adverse events in the three Prelox RCTs (184 men, 1–6 months) — [Frontiers 2023 meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)
- Beetroot/nitrate: no safety concerns at about 2 cups/day for several weeks; beeturia; no long-term data — [NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/); 6 weeks of nitrate-rich juice produced "No adverse effects" and shifted the oral microbiome toward nitrate-reducing genera — [Velmurugan 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4691670/)
- Harvard Health on L-arginine: "a study of the possible benefits of L-arginine to treat heart attack survivors was stopped midway when early data showed six deaths among volunteers assigned to L-arginine, compared with none in the placebo group. Men—especially those at risk for heart disease—should avoid these supplements" — [Harvard Health, ED scams](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/beware-of-erectile-dysfunction-scams)

### Inferences
- There is no human evidence of pharmacological tolerance to arginine, citrulline or Prelox for erectile endpoints, but equally no evidence of durable benefit after stopping (Prelox faded within a month), so these appear to be maintenance therapies.
- The VINTAGE MI signal (9 g/day, post-STEMI, mortality 8.6% vs 0) is the only mortality-level concern and has made "recent MI" a contraindication in Mayo, Cleveland Clinic and Harvard guidance; it has not been replicated or refuted, so cardiovascular screening before daily arginine is prudent, particularly in men ≥ 60.
- The pharmacologically plausible interactions (nitrates, antihypertensives, PDE5 inhibitors → hypotension) have not produced documented hypotensive events in the ED trials that combined arginine 2.5–5 g with tadalafil or sildenafil (adverse events were GI/insomnia), so the clinical magnitude of the interaction at supplement doses appears small but unmeasured.
- Citrulline's GI tolerability advantage over arginine is well documented pharmacokinetically and is the main practical reason to prefer it when > 3 g/day of arginine equivalents is desired.

### Gaps
- No ED trial of any NO-pathway supplement beyond 6 months; no long-term safety registry; no study of arginine or citrulline in men with recent MI, heart failure or on nitrate therapy for ED outcomes.
- The interaction study of an arginine/yohimbine combination with intravenous nitroglycerine in healthy men exists but its results were not retrievable — [Kernohan 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1884955/).
- Herpes reactivation (a commonly cited arginine concern) was not addressed by any retrieved authority or trial.

## Key Question 9: What do Examine.com, NIH ODS, Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, Harvard Health and urology professional sources say?

### Takeaway
Mainstream authorities converge on a cautious position: arginine "might" help ED of physical cause and is the best-evidenced supplement ingredient (EAU gives a weak recommendation for daily arginine or ginseng in mild ED), citrulline is "promising but the jury's still out," Pycnogenol/Prelox evidence is thin and industry-linked, beetroot has no ED evidence, and marketed products usually under-dose arginine.

### Cited Findings
- EAU Guidelines on Sexual and Reproductive Health (Management of ED, section 5.6.9): "There has been an exponential growth in the market of medicinal herbs and natural supplements for the treatment of ED, but with very little available evidence of robust scientific data to support their efficacy and safety"; a Cochrane review found ginseng "may only have trivial effects"; recommendation (strength: weak): "Use supplements with L-arginine or ginseng daily in men with mild ED who refuse pharmacological treatment after counselling them that the improvement of EF could be mild" — [EAU guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- AUA Erectile Dysfunction Guideline (2018, amended 2022): notes that "For some clinical issues there was little or no evidence from which to construct evidence-based statements"; no specific statement on L-arginine or citrulline was found in the material retrieved — [AUA ED guideline](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/erectile-dysfunction-(ed)-guideline)
- Mayo Clinic: "Erectile dysfunction. Taking oral L-arginine might improve sexual function in men with erectile dysfunction due to a physical cause"; "Our take: Generally safe… if you take a blood pressure drug, talk to your doctor before using L-arginine" — [Mayo Clinic L-arginine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-l-arginine/art-20364681); its ED-supplement table rates L-arginine "Studied in people, positive results, generally safe": "Some research shows that taking high doses improves erectile dysfunction by helping blood vessels open wider for better blood flow… Don't take L-arginine with sildenafil" — [Mayo Clinic, supplements for ED](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/erectile-dysfunction/in-depth/erectile-dysfunction-herbs/art-20044394)
- Cleveland Clinic lists erectile dysfunction among conditions L-arginine "can treat," gives a working dose of 6 g (max 30 g) per day divided three times, and says full effects may take up to 3 months — [Cleveland Clinic L-arginine](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/drugs/22536-l-arginine); on citrulline (Feb 2023, dietitian Devon Peart): "It's been suggested as a possibility… But the jury's still out on that," citing the 24-man Cormio study; safe dose 3–6 g/day; "Scientists don't yet agree on the benefits of citrulline" — [Cleveland Clinic citrulline](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/citrulline-benefits)
- Harvard Health: L-arginine "has the theoretical potential to improve erectile function in some patients," but citing VINTAGE MI, "Men—especially those at risk for heart disease—should avoid these supplements" — [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/beware-of-erectile-dysfunction-scams); a second Harvard page on sexual-function supplements was under maintenance and could not be read
- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements has no ED-specific fact sheet; its exercise-performance fact sheet covers arginine ("doses of 2–20 g/day arginine have little to no effect on performance"; up to 9 g/day safe for days–weeks; safety > 3 months unknown), citrulline ("limited and conflicting"; safety for months "not adequately assessed"; citrulline malate is 56.64% citrulline by weight) and beetroot (about 500 mL/day used in studies; about 2 cups/day for several weeks raised no safety concerns) — [NIH ODS fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/)
- Examine.com: arginine dosing studied 1.5–30 g/day with GI distress above about 10 g at once; "Citrulline may also be a good option for increasing arginine and nitric oxide levels and is less likely to cause gastrointestinal side effects" — [Examine arginine](https://examine.com/supplements/arginine/); citrulline's ED entry rests on 1 study of 24 participants — [Examine citrulline](https://examine.com/supplements/citrulline/); Pycnogenol's male-sexual-dysfunction entry rests on 1 study and typical dosing is 100–200 mg/day — [Examine Pycnogenol](https://examine.com/supplements/pycnogenol/); Examine's summary of a review of ED supplements: "L-arginine has the most robust data supporting its safety for use in alleviating ED, compared to placebo. Low-quality evidence indicates Panax ginseng is…" — [Examine study summary](https://examine.com/research-feed/study/v9Qe59/); Examine's summary of Petre 2023: 33 of 41 marketed ingredients lacked evidence; arginine was in 63% of products "but it was always included in a dose below the minimally effective amount"; 24 of 27 products contained at least one under-dosed ingredient — [Examine summary](https://examine.com/research-feed/study/9kKr39/)
- Italian academic authors (Barbonetti 2024) frame the field as "general ineffectiveness of most nutraceutical interventions" with L-arginine and carnitine mixtures "of some usefulness," especially with PDE5i in organic ED — [Barbonetti 2024](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39279185)

### Inferences
- The most authoritative professional position (EAU, weak recommendation) accepts daily L-arginine only for mild ED in men who decline drugs, with explicit counselling that improvement "could be mild"; no guideline endorses citrulline, Pycnogenol/Prelox or beetroot.
- Consumer-facing clinical authorities (Mayo, Cleveland Clinic) are more permissive than Harvard Health, which emphasises the post-MI mortality signal; all agree on avoiding arginine after a recent heart attack and on caution with blood-pressure drugs and PDE5 inhibitors.
- The under-dosing finding (Petre 2023) means most multi-ingredient "nitric oxide boosters" sold for ED cannot be expected to reproduce trial results that used 2.5–6 g arginine or 1.5 g citrulline daily.

### Gaps
- The AUA guideline's text on supplements could not be retrieved; whether it contains a formal statement on arginine/herbals is unverified.
- Examine.com's full grade and effect-size tables for arginine/ED and Pycnogenol/ED are behind a paywall; only the study counts and summaries were accessible.
- NIH ODS has no ED-specific guidance; its statements apply to exercise-performance use.
