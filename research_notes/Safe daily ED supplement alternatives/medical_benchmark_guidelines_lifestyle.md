# Medical benchmark for daily ED supplements: PDE5 inhibitors, guidelines, lifestyle, cardiovascular warning signs, medical causes, BPH

Research date: 7 November 2026. Scope: human clinical evidence only. Prices are US and dated inline. Guideline texts quoted verbatim where available (AUA 2018 ED guideline, full PDF; EAU Sexual and Reproductive Health guideline, 2026 web edition).

## 1. In numbers, how much do sildenafil (on demand) and tadalafil 2.5-5 mg once daily improve IIEF-EF versus placebo, and what share of men reach normal erectile function?

### Takeaway
Across the pooled trial literature, placebo raises the IIEF erectile-function domain (6-30 scale) by roughly +1.8 points while sildenafil raises it by about +9 and tadalafil by about +8 from a baseline near 14; in the pivotal once-daily tadalafil trials the placebo-subtracted gain was about 5-9 IIEF-EF points, 63-82% of sildenafil users report improved erections versus 24% on placebo, and roughly 30-66% of men on daily tadalafil 5 mg return to the normal IIEF-EF range (>=26) depending on baseline severity.

### Cited Findings

**Pooled IIEF-EF changes (AUA systematic review, general ED population)**
- AUA 2018 guideline Table 2 (change in IIEF-EF from baseline to post-treatment, general ED population): placebo +1.78 (62 study arms; range -1.60 to +7.10); sildenafil +9.00 (26 arms; +1.70 to +11.75); tadalafil +7.82 (37 arms; +1.98 to +12.00); vardenafil +8.80 (26 arms); avanafil +8.10 (5 arms). "The magnitude of change across medications is similar." — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)
- In studies of 50 mg sildenafil, "pre-treatment IIEF-EF scores averaged 14.0 and post-treatment scores averaged 22.6; in studies in which men were administered 100 mg sildenafil, pre-treatment IIEF-EF scores averaged 14.4 and post-treatment scores averaged 23.8." Dose-response across PDE5i is "small and non-linear (i.e., doubling the dose does not double the effect)... e.g., a one or two point increase on the IIEF-EF." — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)
- EAU summary of evidence: "PDE5Is are associated with significant improvement of EF with a good overall safety profile" (Level of evidence 1a); "There are no demonstrated differences among different PDE5Is in terms of treatment efficacy" (1a); recommendation: "Use phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors (PDE5Is) as first-line therapy for the treatment of ED" (Strong). — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)

**Sildenafil (on demand)**
- FDA label (Viagra), four fixed-dose placebo-controlled studies (1,797 patients, 12-24 weeks): "Sixty-three percent, 74%, and 82% of the patients on 25 mg, 50 mg and 100 mg of VIAGRA, respectively, reported an improvement in their erections, compared to 24% on placebo." Diary data (about 1,600 patients): "per patient weekly success rates averaged 1.3 on 50-100 mg of VIAGRA vs 0.4 on placebo; similarly, group mean success rates (total successes divided by total attempts) were about 66% on VIAGRA vs about 20% on placebo." In the 1-year open-label study, "88% of patients reported that VIAGRA improved their erections." — [Viagra US prescribing information (Pfizer)](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?format=PDF&id=652)
- Subgroups in the Viagra label: diabetes 57% improved vs 10% placebo (48% vs 12% of intercourse attempts successful); spinal cord injury 83% vs 12%; radical prostatectomy 43% vs 15%; psychogenic ED 84% vs 26% (70% vs 29% of attempts successful). — [Viagra US prescribing information (Pfizer)](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?format=PDF&id=652)
- Fink et al. 2002 meta-analysis (27 trials, 6,659 men): successful intercourse attempts 57% with sildenafil vs 21% placebo (weighted mean difference 33.7; 95% CI 29.2-38.2; 2,283 men); at least one intercourse success 83% vs 45% (relative benefit 1.8; 95% CI 1.7-1.9). — [Fink HA et al., Arch Intern Med 2002 (PubMed 12076233)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12076233/)
- EAU: "In a 24-week dose-response study, improved erections were reported by 56%, 77% and 84% of general ED patients taking 25, 50 and 100mg sildenafil, respectively, compared to 25% of men taking placebo." Window of effectiveness "ranges from 30-60 minutes after administration up to 12 hours." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- 2026 dose-response network meta-analysis of on-demand PDE5i (83 double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs, 6,029 participants; primary outcome = proportion achieving IIEF-EF score >26): odds ratio vs placebo for sildenafil 100 mg 9.06, sildenafil 50 mg 7.90, tadalafil 20 mg 7.13, tadalafil 10 mg 3.14, vardenafil 10 mg 7.78, avanafil 200 mg 3.42, avanafil 100 mg 1.75; threshold analysis OR 2.20 (95% CI 1.78-2.63) for sildenafil 100 mg; "odds of treatment-related adverse events were highest for vardenafil, followed by avanafil and sildenafil, and lowest for tadalafil." — [Salonia A et al., J Sex Med 2026;23:qdag176 (PubMed 42296271)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42296271/)

**Tadalafil on demand (for context)**
- Cialis label, two primary US trials (20 mg): IIEF-EF change +6.9 vs -0.2 placebo (endpoint 19.5 vs 13.5) and +9.3 vs +0.3 (22.5 vs 13.6); SEP3 (maintenance of erection) endpoint 50% vs 25% and 64% vs 23%. EAU: in a 12-week dose-response study improved erections in 67% (10 mg) and 81% (20 mg) vs 35% placebo. — [Cialis US prescribing information (Lilly)](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf); [EAU Guidelines](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)

**Tadalafil once daily (2.5-5 mg)**
- Cialis label Table 17 (once-daily use for ED): 24-week US study (Study H): IIEF-EF change from baseline +1.2 placebo (endpoint 14.6) vs +6.1 with 2.5 mg (19.1) and +7.0 with 5 mg (20.8), p<.001; SEP3 success 31% placebo vs 50% (2.5 mg) and 57% (5 mg). 12-week ex-US study (Study I): +0.9 placebo (15.0) vs +9.7 with 5 mg (22.8); SEP3 37% vs 67%. "Timing of sexual activity was not restricted relative to when patients took Cialis"; "In the 6 month double-blind study, the treatment effect of CIALIS did not diminish over time." — [Cialis US prescribing information (Lilly)](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf)
- Daily tadalafil in diabetes (Table 18, n=298): IIEF-EF +1.3 placebo vs +4.8 (2.5 mg) and +4.5 (5 mg); endpoints 14.7 vs 18.3 and 17.2. — [Cialis US prescribing information (Lilly)](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf)
- Integrated analysis of six placebo-controlled once-daily trials (1,913 men: placebo n=596, 2.5 mg n=394, 5 mg n=923): least-squares mean change vs placebo in SEP3 17.8% (2.5 mg) and 23.6% (5 mg) and in IIEF-EF 4.2 and 5.4 points (p<0.01). Severity-specific minimal clinically important difference reached by 54.3%/74.8% (mild), 51.3%/63.1% (moderate) and 33.7%/44.5% (severe) on 2.5/5 mg; in severe ED 44.5% on 5 mg vs 11.6% on placebo. IIEF-EF normalization with 5 mg daily: 66.1% (mild), 48.0% (moderate), 29.3% (severe baseline ED). — [Porst H et al., Eur Urol 2014 (integrated analysis)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0302283813010245)
- In 623 men with suboptimal response to maximum-dose on-demand sildenafil, tadalafil or vardenafil, 12 weeks of daily tadalafil 2.5 or 5 mg returned "approximately 40% of men in the tadalafil groups" to normal erectile function (IIEF-EF >=26) "compared to 12% in the placebo group." — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF (Kim, Seftel et al. 2013)](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)
- EAU: "Regardless of the ED population, there is no clinically significant difference between continuous (once daily) and on-demand tadalafil treatment"; daily 5 mg "may improve EF in men who have a partial response to on-demand PDE5I therapy"; "The appropriateness of the continuous use of a daily regimen should be reassessed periodically." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- Label dosing: once-daily starting dose 2.5 mg "taken at approximately the same time every day, without regard to timing of sexual activity," may be increased to 5 mg. — [Cialis US prescribing information (Lilly)](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf)

**Onset and duration**
- Sildenafil: take "approximately 1 hour before sexual activity"; "may be taken anywhere from 30 minutes to 4 hours before sexual activity." — [Viagra US prescribing information (Pfizer)](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?format=PDF&id=652)
- EAU Table 5.3 pharmacokinetics: sildenafil 100 mg Tmax 0.8-1 h, half-life 2.6-3.7 h; tadalafil 20 mg Tmax 2 h, half-life 17.5 h; vardenafil Tmax 0.9 h, half-life 3.9 h; avanafil Tmax 0.5-0.75 h. Tadalafil "window of effectiveness ranges from 30 minutes after administration (peak efficacy after approximately two hours) up to 36 hours." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- Cialis label stopwatch study: successful erections at or before 30 minutes in 35% (placebo), 38% (10 mg) and 52% (20 mg); at 36 hours after a 20 mg dose, 64% vs 37% (placebo) reported at least one successful intercourse. — [Cialis US prescribing information (Lilly)](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf)
- Placebo response is substantial: a JAMA Network Open meta-analysis of 12,564 men found "a significant association between placebo and improved erectile function." — [JAMA Netw Open 2020](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2762993)

### Inferences
- The clinically relevant benchmark for any daily supplement is a placebo-subtracted IIEF-EF gain of roughly 5-9 points (daily tadalafil) or 6-8 points (sildenafil, i.e., about +9 total minus about +1.8 placebo) within 4-12 weeks, with 30-66% normalization. The MCID for IIEF-EF is 2 (mild), 5 (moderate) and 7 (severe ED) points (Rosen 2011, cited in the psychiatrist.com commentary on Khera 2023), so a supplement adding 2-3 points would only clear the MCID for mild ED.
- Because about 20% of placebo-treated men report improved erections and placebo alone adds about 1.8 IIEF-EF points, uncontrolled or open-label supplement data cannot be compared to these numbers.

### Gaps
- The Porst 2014 abstract wording ("LS mean change: 4.2; 5.4 compared with placebo") does not make fully explicit whether 4.2/5.4 are placebo-subtracted differences; the Cialis label trial tables (which are explicit) are the safer primary source.
- No single head-to-head RCT of daily tadalafil 5 mg vs on-demand sildenafil with IIEF-EF normalization as the primary endpoint was located.

## 2. Safety: side-effect rates, contraindications (nitrates, alpha-blockers, riociguat) and whether daily tadalafil has safety advantages over on-demand sildenafil

### Takeaway
Daily tadalafil 2.5-5 mg has the mildest adverse-event profile of the PDE5 inhibitors (headache 3-6% vs 5% placebo, back pain 2-3%, myalgia 1-2%, flushing about 3%, no visual disturbance signal), versus sildenafil flexible-dose rates of headache 16%, flushing 10%, dyspepsia 7% and abnormal vision 3%; the trade-offs are a 48-hour (vs 24-hour) nitrate washout and continuous drug exposure. Nitrates in any form and riociguat are absolute contraindications for every PDE5 inhibitor.

### Cited Findings

**Sildenafil adverse reactions**
- Viagra label Table 2 (flexible-dose trials, N=734 vs 725 placebo): headache 16% vs 4%; flushing 10% vs 1%; dyspepsia 7% vs 2%; nasal congestion 4% vs 2%; abnormal vision 3% vs 0%; back pain 2% vs 2%; dizziness 2% vs 1%; rash 2% vs 1%. Fixed-dose Table 1 at 100 mg: headache 28%, flushing 18%, dyspepsia 17%, abnormal vision 11%, nasal congestion 9%, back pain 4%, myalgia 4% (placebo 7%, 2%, 2%, 1%, 2%, 2%, 1%). — [Viagra US prescribing information (Pfizer)](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?format=PDF&id=652)
- Fink 2002 pooled AE rates for sildenafil: flushing 12%, headache 11%, dyspepsia 5%, visual disturbances 3%; "Sildenafil was not significantly associated with serious cardiovascular events or death." — [Fink HA et al., Arch Intern Med 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12076233/)
- EAU Table 5.4 (EMA product characteristics): sildenafil headache 12.8%, flushing 10.4%, dyspepsia 4.6%, nasal congestion 1.1%, dizziness 1.2%, abnormal vision 1.9%; tadalafil headache 14.5%, flushing 4.1%, dyspepsia 12.3%, nasal congestion 4.3%, dizziness 2.3%, back pain 6.5%, myalgia 5.7% (abnormal vision not listed for tadalafil). — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)

**Tadalafil adverse reactions (once-daily vs on-demand)**
- Cialis label Table 2 (once-daily 2.5/5 mg for ED, 12-week trials; placebo N=248, 2.5 mg N=196, 5 mg N=304): headache 5%/3%/6%; dyspepsia 2%/4%/5%; back pain 1%/3%/3%; flushing 1%/1%/3%; myalgia 1%/2%/2%; nasal congestion 0%/2%/2%. Table 3 (24-week once-daily ED study): back pain 3%/5%/2%, myalgia 2%/4%/1%, hypertension 0%/1%/3%, nasal congestion 0%/0%/4%. Table 4 (5 mg once daily in BPH and ED/BPH 12-week trials; placebo N=576, tadalafil N=581): headache 4.1% vs 2.3%; dyspepsia 2.4% vs 0.2%; back pain 2.4% vs 1.4%; myalgia 1.2% vs 0.3%; dizziness 1.0% vs 0.5%. — [Cialis US prescribing information (Lilly)](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf)
- On-demand Cialis Table 1 (10/20 mg): headache 11%/15% (placebo 5%); dyspepsia 8%/10% (1%); back pain 5%/6% (3%); myalgia 4%/3% (1%); flushing 3%/3% (1%). — [Cialis US prescribing information (Lilly)](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf)
- "In tadalafil clinical pharmacology trials, back pain or myalgia generally occurred 12 to 24 hours after dosing and typically resolved within 48 hours... diffuse bilateral lower lumbar, gluteal, thigh, or thoracolumbar muscular discomfort and was exacerbated by recumbency... severe back pain was reported with a low frequency (<5% of all reports)." Discontinuation for back pain/myalgia "<1% across all indications." — [Cialis US prescribing information (Lilly)](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf); [FDA Cialis label 2011](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2011/021368s20s21lbl.pdf)
- EMA: "The most commonly reported adverse reactions... were headache, dyspepsia, back pain and myalgia, in which the incidences increase with increasing dose... The majority of headaches reported with CIALIS once-a-day dosing are experienced within the first 10 to 30 days of starting treatment." — [EMA Cialis product information](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information/cialis-epar-product-information_en.pdf)
- Tadalafil is "700-fold more potent for PDE5 than for PDE6, which is found in the retina" and ">10,000-fold more potent for PDE5 than for PDE3, an enzyme found in the heart and blood vessels." — [FDA Cialis label 2017](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2017/021368s029lbl.pdf)

**Contraindications and interactions**
- Viagra: "administration to patients who are using nitric oxide donors such as organic nitrates or organic nitrites in any form either regularly and/or intermittently is therefore contraindicated"; "Do not use VIAGRA in patients who are using a GC stimulator, such as riociguat"; "VIAGRA can potentiate the hypotensive effects of nitrates, alpha blockers"; seek emergency care for erections lasting >4 hours. — [Viagra US prescribing information (Pfizer)](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?format=PDF&id=652)
- Cialis: "Administration of CIALIS to patients who are using any form of organic nitrate, either regularly and/or intermittently, is contraindicated"; "Do not use CIALIS in patients who are using a GC stimulator, such as riociguat"; "CIALIS can potentiate the hypotensive effects of nitrates, alpha-blockers, antihypertensives or alcohol"; if nitrates become medically necessary "at least 48 hours should elapse after the last dose of CIALIS before nitrate administration is considered." — [Cialis US prescribing information (Lilly)](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf)
- Mayo Clinic drug monograph: "Do not use this medicine if you are also using riociguat (Adempas) or a nitrate medicine... If you need to use a nitrate medicine, take it at least 48 hours after your last dose of tadalafil." — [Mayo Clinic, tadalafil oral route](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements/tadalafil-oral-route/description/drg-20067204)
- ACC summary of Princeton IV: "Short-acting PDE5i (sildenafil, vardenafil, avanafil) should be avoided within 24 hours of nitrates, whereas longer-acting PDE5i (tadalafil) should be avoided within 48 hours of nitrate-containing substances." — [ACC Expert Analysis, Sept 2024](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2024/09/23/10/45/erectile-disfunction-as-an-ascvd-risk-enhancing-factor)
- EAU: "An absolute contraindication to PDE5Is is the concomitant use of any form of organic nitrate or nitric oxide donors, including recreational use of amyl nitrite or nitrate (poppers)... Concurrent use of nicorandil and PDE5Is is contraindicated." Alpha-blockers: "A meta-analysis concluded that concomitant treatment with alpha-blockers... and PDE5Is may produce changes in haemodynamic parameters, but it does not increase the rate of adverse events due to hypotension. Therefore, there is no current limitation in the simultaneous use of alpha-blockers and PDE5I." Antihypertensives: "small additive decreases in blood pressure, which are usually minor." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- FDA 2011 Cialis label: "CIALIS is not recommended in combination with alpha blockers for the treatment of BPH"; 2017 label doxazosin studies show tadalafil 5 mg placebo-subtracted maximal standing systolic BP decrease of -0.5 to 1.1 mm Hg vs 9.8 mm Hg (95% CI 4.1-15.5) for tadalafil 20 mg. — [FDA Cialis label 2011](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2011/021368s20s21lbl.pdf); [FDA Cialis label 2017](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2017/021368s029lbl.pdf)
- AUA: "men taking nitrates regularly should not use PDE5i medications. Men who carry sublingual nitroglycerin for angina should be advised not to use this medication within 24 hours of taking a PDE5i, and possibly longer in the case of use of a PDE5i with a long half-life (i.e., tadalafil)... In men with severe renal or liver disease, use of PDE5i is generally not recommended." — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)

**Cardiovascular safety and possible benefit**
- EAU: "No RCTs or open-label studies have demonstrated an increase in myocardial infarction (MI) rates in patients receiving PDE5Is... in men with stable coronary artery disease, treatment with PDE5I is associated with lower risks of death, MI, heart failure, and revascularisation compared with alprostadil treatment." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- ACC/Princeton IV: "Phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors are safe in patients with overt or latent CVD and may exhibit a cardioprotective profile"; a retrospective claims study of >70,000 men with ED found 13% lower major CV events, 25% lower all-cause death and 39% lower CV death with PDE5i exposure, but "these studies... are retrospective and have some limitations, including indication bias, not providing causality." — [ACC Expert Analysis, Sept 2024](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2024/09/23/10/45/erectile-disfunction-as-an-ascvd-risk-enhancing-factor)
- Low-dose daily vardenafil (10 mg) "did not worsen OSA" in a 12-week RCT of men with moderate-severe OSA. — [Melehan KL et al., J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2018 (PubMed 29409064)](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/4/1601/4834040)
- EAU: "Two meta-analyses demonstrated that ED patients who prioritise high efficacy should use sildenafil 50mg whereas those who optimise tolerability should initially use tadalafil 10mg." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)

### Inferences
- Safety advantages of daily low-dose tadalafil over on-demand sildenafil that are supported by the data: lower per-dose headache/flushing/dyspepsia rates and no visual-disturbance signal (PDE6 selectivity), lowest treatment-related AE odds in the 2026 network meta-analysis, negligible interaction with alpha-blockers at 5 mg, and decoupling of dosing from sexual activity. Disadvantages: tadalafil-specific back pain/myalgia (about 2-6%), a 48-hour nitrate washout, and continuous exposure that guidelines say should be re-evaluated periodically.
- The "cardioprotective" signal is observational only; it should be described as hypothesis-generating, not as a reason to take a PDE5 inhibitor.

### Gaps
- Head-to-head randomized safety comparisons of daily tadalafil 5 mg versus on-demand sildenafil were not located; the comparison above relies on separate label tables and network meta-analysis.

- Hims vs Roman comparison (Hims blog): "Daily use plans at Hims start at $39 per month; Ro Sparks starts at $48 per month (and goes up to $120 per month)." — [Hims, "Hims vs. Roman for ED 2025"](https://www.hims.com/blog/hims-vs-roman)

### Inferences
- A realistic all-in monthly cost for FDA-approved generic daily tadalafil 5 mg in late 2026 is about $12-20 (coupon pharmacy) or about $11 (Cost Plus) plus an amortized $25-45 prescription visit once or twice a year; this is the price floor against which a $30-80/month supplement subscription should be judged.
- Telehealth "daily" products are often not the FDA-approved 5 mg tablet (BlueChew 9 mg compounded chew with vitamins; Ro 7 mg gummy), so their prices and dosing are not directly comparable to the trial evidence above.

### Gaps
- Hims' public pages are bot-protected; its tier prices come from Hims' own blog (Nov 2025) and third-party checks (Oct 2026), and the 12-month prepaid tier availability was described as "conditional" by Bidwell.
- BlueChew's plan page could not be retrieved; the DailyTAD "From $2/ea" figure is the only directly verified BlueChew price.

## 4. What exactly do the AUA and EAU guidelines (and major academic centers) state about supplements, herbal remedies and L-arginine?

### Takeaway
The AUA 2018 guideline (still the current AUA ED guideline in 2026, with a successor "Disorders of Penile Erection" guideline due 2027) explicitly lists yohimbine, ginseng and L-arginine "alone or in combination with other substances" among oral treatments that "either are ineffective, are not safe, or lack a sufficient body of evidence," while the EAU 2026 guideline quantifies L-arginine's effect at +2.74 IIEF-EF points over placebo and gives only a Weak recommendation to use L-arginine or ginseng daily in men with mild ED who refuse drugs after counselling that improvement "could be mild."

### Cited Findings

**AUA (Erectile Dysfunction: AUA Guideline, 2018)**
- The AUA guideline has no numbered statement on supplements. Its "Other Treatments" section reads verbatim: "Many additional oral treatments for ED have been evaluated in the peer-reviewed literature but, in the Panel's view, these treatments either are ineffective, are not safe, or lack a sufficient body of evidence from which to make generalizations. These treatments include apomorphine as an oral preparation, yohimbine, statins as monotherapy for ED, trazodone, ginseng, and l-arginine alone or in combination with other substances as well as various types of topical treatments. The Panel notes that the use of these treatments may preclude the use of other treatments known to be effective. The Panel will revisit these treatments each time the guideline is updated and re-evaluate the available evidence base." — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)
- Guideline Statement 7: "Clinicians should counsel men with ED who have comorbidities known to negatively affect erectile function that lifestyle modifications, including changes in diet and increased physical activity, improve overall health and may improve erectile function. (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)". Statement 8: "Men with ED should be informed regarding the treatment option of an FDA-approved oral phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor (PDE5i), including discussion of benefits and risks/burdens, unless contraindicated. - The two guideline bodies differ in tone but not substance: AUA (2018 evidence base) considers these agents unproven or unsafe; EAU (2026) acknowledges a small effect and permits L-arginine/ginseng only as a weak fallback in mild ED when drugs are refused.

## 5. Which lifestyle changes have the largest measured effect on IIEF, and over what time frame?

### Takeaway
Pelvic-floor muscle training with biofeedback (+6.7 IIEF-EF vs control at 3 months; 40% regained normal function by 6 months), structured aerobic exercise (+2.8 IIEF-EF overall, +4.9 in severe ED, over a median 6 months), 10% weight loss with increased activity in obese men (+3 points vs control at 2 years; 31% vs 5% regained IIEF >=22) and statin therapy (+3.4 IIEF-5) have the largest measured effects; smoking cessation improved ED in about 25% of quitters within a year versus none who kept smoking, while alcohol and CPAP data are mixed.

### Cited Findings

**Guideline framing**
- AUA Statement 7 (Moderate Recommendation; Grade C) quoted in section 4; AUA discussion conclusion: "dietary changes, weight loss, and physical activity increases improve overall health, ameliorate comorbidities associated with ED, and result in small improvements in erectile function overall and may lead to clinically significant improvements in a subset of men... men who smoke should be counseled regarding the overall health benefits of smoking cessation." — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)
- EAU 5.6.2 "Modifiable risk factors": "ED may be associated with concomitant and underlying conditions (e.g. endocrine and metabolic disorders, such as diabetes, and some CV problems, such as hypertension) which should always be well-controlled as the first step of any ED treatment. Overall, several studies have shown that lifestyle modifications, including physical activity (particularly aerobic exercise), weight loss (including bariatric surgery), and treatment of CVD risk factors, may improve sexual function in men with ED. Regarding the use of statins, previous meta-analyses have demonstrated an improvement in EF." Summary of evidence: "Lifestyle modification (regular exercise and decrease in BMI) can improve EF" (LE 1b). — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)

**Aerobic exercise**
- Khera, Bhattacharyya & Miller 2023 meta-analysis of 11 RCTs: aerobic exercise improved IIEF-EF vs non-exercising controls by a mean 2.8 points (95% CI 1.7-3.9; P<.001; I2=53%); gains were 2.3, 3.3 and 4.9 points for mild, moderate and severe baseline ED (P=.02). — [Khera M et al., J Sex Med 2023;20:1369-1375 (PubMed 37814532)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37814532/)
- Trial characteristics (pooled N=1,147; sample sizes 20-372; mean ages 39-69): "30-60 minutes sessions of activity, 3-5 times a week, for a median duration of 6 months" (range 2-24 months). Against MCIDs of 2, 5 and 7 points for mild, moderate and severe ED, "the required threshold for improvement with aerobic exercise was met only for subjects with mild ED." — [Psychiatrist.com commentary on Khera 2023](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/aerobic-exercise-for-erectile-dysfunction-rct-data/)
- Gerbild 2018 systematic review of intervention studies: "Recommendations of PA to decrease ED should include supervised training consisting of 40 minutes of aerobic exercise of moderate to vigorous intensity 4 times per week" (i.e., 160 min/week) for six months. — [Gerbild H et al., Sex Med 2018 (PubMed 29661646)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29661646/)
- Hypertensive men (Lamina 2009, n=50, interval training 3 sessions/week for 8 weeks): IIEF-EF 11.5 to 15.1 vs 8.1 to 8.9 in controls. Post-MI home walking (Begot 2015, n=86, 1 month): 12% of walkers had ED (all mild) vs 93% of controls (44% severe). Cardiac rehabilitation (Kalka 2013/2015, n=138, 6 months): mean 4-item IIEF-5 score 14.4 vs 12.4. — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)
- A 2026 mini-review notes meta-analytic IIEF improvements "ranging from clinically negligible to comparable with phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors," with moderate-to-vigorous aerobic exercise improving IIEF scores by 3-5 points. — [Physical Exercise for Erectile Dysfunction mini review, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405456926000696)

**Weight loss**
- Esposito 2004 JAMA RCT (110 obese men, BMI >=30, IIEF <=21, no diabetes/hypertension/hyperlipidemia; 2 years): intervention BMI 36.9 to 31.2 (control 36.4 to 35.7); physical activity 48 to 195 min/week; IIEF improved from 13.9 to 17 (P<.001) vs 13.5 to 13.6 in controls (P=.89); 17 vs 3 men (31% vs 5%) reached IIEF >=22 (P=.001); changes in BMI, activity and CRP independently associated with IIEF change; "improvement in sexual function in about one third of obese men." Corrected between-group difference in ED score at 2 years: 3 points (95% CI 1.2-4.8; P=.008). — [Esposito K et al., JAMA 2004;291:2978-2984 (PubMed 15213209)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15213209/); [JAMA full text](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/198993)
- Collins 2013 (SHED-IT, 185 overweight/obese men, 6 months, no face-to-face contact): weight loss 4.7 and 3.7 kg; among men with ED at baseline, IIEF-5 rose a mean 3.3 points vs a 0.9-point fall on the wait list. Khoo 2010 (8-week low-calorie diet): IIEF-5 17.8 to 20.0 (non-diabetic) and 8.1 to 10.3 (diabetic). Khoo 2013 (90 obese men, 6 months): high-intensity exercise (200-300 min/week) IIEF-5 18.1 to 20.7 vs moderate (<150 min/week) 18.3 to 20.1 (difference 0.8). Look AHEAD subset (Wing 2010, 372 overweight men with type 2 diabetes, 1 year): IIEF-EF 17.3 to 18.6 (intensive) vs 18.3 to 18.4 (support), "magnitude of improvement was small." — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)

**Mediterranean diet**
- Esposito 2006 (65 men with metabolic syndrome and ED; 35 Mediterranean diet vs 30 control; 2 years): endothelial function and CRP improved in the intervention group; 13 vs 2 men reported IIEF >=22 (P=0.015). — [Esposito K et al., Int J Impot Res 2006;18:405-410 (PubMed 16395320)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16395320/). AUA's summary of the same trial: IIEF rose "from 14.4 to 18.1" vs "14.9 to 15.2" in controls. — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)
- MEDITA trial (106 men with type 2 diabetes, 8.1-year follow-up): decline in IIEF was significantly greater on a low-fat diet than on the Mediterranean diet (p=0.024), i.e., the diet delayed deterioration of sexual function. — [Di Francesco & Tenaglia, Mediterranean diet and ED: a current perspective (PMC5510347)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5510347/)
- Angelis et al., ESC Congress 2021 (250 hypertensive men with ED, mean age 56; cross-sectional): men with Mediterranean diet score >29 had higher coronary flow reserve and testosterone, lower arterial stiffness and better SHIM scores (above 14); men with exercise capacity >10 METs had higher diet scores and SHIM >12. Dr Athanasios Angelis (University of Athens): "consuming a Mediterranean diet was linked with better exercise capacity, healthier arteries and blood flow, higher testosterone levels, and better erectile performance." — [ESC press release via EurekAlert, 25 Aug 2021](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/926161)
- Esposito 2009 intensive lifestyle trial (209 men with ED or ED risk factors, 2 years): 58 vs 40 men had scores indicating no ED. — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)

**Pelvic floor muscle training**
- Dorey 2005 RCT (55 men, median age 59.2, ED >=6 months; 28 PFMT + manometric biofeedback + lifestyle advice vs 27 lifestyle advice only): at 3 months the intervention group showed a mean IIEF-EF increase of 6.74 points vs controls (P=0.004; ANCOVA difference 7.0, 95% CI 2.4-11.6); controls then crossed over; by 6 months blinded assessment found 22 (40.0%) attained normal function, 19 (34.5%) improved and 14 (25.5%) failed to improve; the lifestyle-only control arm showed no significant improvement at 3 months (P=0.658). The authors compared their 3-month gain (about 8 points to a score of 17) with the 10-point gain to 21 in the sildenafil trial. — [Dorey G et al., Br J Gen Pract 2005 (PubMed 15527607)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15527607/); [PMC1324914 full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1324914/)
- EAU: "pelvic floor muscle training combined with bio-feedback is a promising alternative to pharmacological treatments, although there is a need for future well-powered, rigorously designed RCTs." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)

**Smoking cessation**
- Pourmand 2004 prospective study (281 smokers with ED and no other risk factors; IIEF-5 at baseline and 1 year): ED severity correlated with pack-years; after 1 year ED improved in >=25% of 118 ex-smokers but in none of 163 continuing smokers; deterioration in 2.5% of ex-smokers vs 6.8% of smokers (P=0.009 for better status); older men and those with advanced ED improved less. — [Pourmand G et al., BJU Int 2004;94:1310-1313 (PubMed 15610111)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15610111/)
- Updated 2023 review: erectile function "improved in 25% of the 118 patients who stopped smoking but in none of the 163 patients who continued smoking." — [Allen & Tostes, Sex Med Rev 2023](https://academic.oup.com/smr/article/11/1/61/6985842)

**Alcohol**
- Meta-analysis (Li 2021): regular alcohol consumption OR for ED 0.89 (95% CI 0.81-0.97); light-to-moderate 0.82 (0.72-0.94); high 0.82 (0.67-1.00); nonlinear dose-response (p<0.001). — [Li S et al., Urol Int 2021 (PubMed 34521090)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34521090/)
- Meta-analysis (Wang 2018): light-to-moderate consumption (<21 drinks/week) OR 0.71 (0.59-0.86) for ED; nonlinear relationship. — [Wang XM et al., Int J Impot Res 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41443-018-0022-x)
- EAU lists "excessive alcohol intake" among drug-induced causes of ED. — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)

**Sleep apnea and CPAP**
- Systematic review/meta-analysis (26 studies): "CPAP significantly ameliorated the international index of erectile function (IIEF) score, total erectile events (TEE) and nocturnal penile rigidity (NPR)"; "CPAP was inferior to PDE5i in improving IIEF-erectile function"; "CPAP combined with PDE5i was superior to CPAP alone in improving IIEF score." — [Li Z et al., Sleep Med Rev 2019 (PubMed 31715462)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31715462/)
- Only sham-controlled RCT (61 men, 12 weeks, 2x2 CPAP/sham and vardenafil/placebo): CPAP "increased the frequency of sleep-related erections, overall sexual satisfaction, and arterial stiffness but did not change erectile function"; "Adherent CPAP improved erectile function." — [Melehan KL et al., J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2018](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/4/1601/4834040)
- 2026 meta-analysis: ED prevalence in OSA 59.0-69.0%; uncontrolled series: IIEF-5 16.6 to 20.9 after 3 months of CPAP (Husnu); ED "completely resolved in 42.6%" with IIEF-15 17.0 to 24.2 (Irer); total IIEF 23.8 to 27.9 at 1 year (Kyrkou). — [Int J Impot Res 2026 meta-analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41443-026-01315-7)

**Blood pressure, lipid and glucose control**
- Statins meta-analysis (11 RCTs): IIEF-5 increased 3.4 points (95% CI 1.7-5.0; P=0.0001) vs control, "approximately one-third to one-half of that previously reported with phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors and larger than the effect of lifestyle modification"; benefit increased with decreasing lipophilicity. — [Kostis JB & Dobrzynski JM, J Sex Med 2014 (PubMed 24684744)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24684744/)
- Diabetes/hypertension review: systolic BP inversely correlated with IIEF-5; significant SBP/DBP reductions improved erectile function in men treated for coronary heart disease (more so after excluding diabetics), but other studies found BP control did not improve erectile outcomes even after 6 years; "diuretics and beta-blockers (with the exception of nebivolol) are mainly associated with ED." PDE5i trials in diabetics show smaller gains (e.g., sildenafil +5.0 vs +2.5 IIEF points with lifestyle/glycemic control; tadalafil 10/20 mg improved erections in 56%/64% vs 25% placebo). — [Defeudis G et al., Diabetes Metab Res Rev 2022 (PMC9286480)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9286480/)
- ED prevalence is 53% in men with diabetes (meta-analysis cited in commentary). — [Psychiatrist.com commentary](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/aerobic-exercise-for-erectile-dysfunction-rct-data/)

### Inferences
- Ranked by measured IIEF-EF gain versus control: pelvic-floor training with biofeedback (+6.7 at 3 months, small trial) > statins (+3.4 IIEF-5) ≈ weight loss/activity in obese men (+3 at 2 years) ≈ aerobic exercise (+2.8; +4.9 in severe ED at about 6 months) ≈ L-arginine (+2.74). All are well below the +6-9 point placebo-subtracted gains of PDE5 inhibitors, but they treat the underlying vascular disease and are additive to drugs.
- Time frames: exercise and pelvic-floor benefits appear at 2-6 months; weight-loss and Mediterranean-diet trials ran 2 years; smoking cessation benefits were measured at 1 year.

### Gaps
- The Silva 2017 meta-analysis of physical activity was not retrieved; the "2023 Angelis/Fountoulakis" Mediterranean-diet publication referenced in the assignment could not be identified—the Angelis data located are the 2021 ESC Congress abstract (cross-sectional, no IIEF change data).
- No randomized trial of alcohol reduction with an erectile-function endpoint was found; alcohol evidence is observational and J-shaped.

## 6. How strong is the link between ED and future cardiovascular events (hazard ratios, lead time of 2-5 years), and what do Princeton III/IV recommend?

### Takeaway
Prospective cohorts and meta-analyses show men with ED have about a 1.25-1.9-fold higher risk of cardiovascular events (RR ~1.4-1.5 for CVD/CHD, ~1.6 for MI, ~1.2 for all-cause death), ED precedes coronary symptoms by a mean of about 2-3 years (38.8 months in Montorsi 2003; 24 months in COBRA) and events by up to 5 years, and Princeton IV (2024) therefore tells clinicians to "treat men with ED as being at risk for cardiac events until proven otherwise" with ASCVD risk scoring and coronary artery calcium scoring before ED is simply treated.

### Cited Findings
- Thompson 2005 (Prostate Cancer Prevention Trial placebo arm; 9,457 men >=55; 1994-2003; evaluated every 3 months): 47% of men free of CVD had ED at entry and 57% of the rest developed it within 5 years; adjusted HR for CV events 1.25 (95% CI 1.02-1.53; P=.04) for incident ED and 1.45 (1.25-1.69; P<.001) for incident or prevalent ED; unadjusted CV event rate 0.024 vs 0.015 per person-year; "This association was in the range of risk associated with current smoking or a family history of myocardial infarction... Erectile dysfunction should prompt investigation and intervention for cardiovascular risk factors." — [Thompson IM et al., JAMA 2005;294:2996-3002 (PubMed 16414947)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16414947/); [JAMA full text](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/202047)
- Uddin 2018 (MESA cohort): unadjusted HR 2.5 (95% CI 1.3-4.8) for hard CHD and 2.6 (1.6-4.1) for CVD events; fully adjusted HR 1.9 (1.1-3.4) for hard CVD events. — [Uddin SMI et al., Circulation 2018;138:540-542](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.033990)
- Dong 2011 meta-analysis (12 prospective cohorts, 36,744 men): RR 1.48 (1.25-1.74) for CVD, 1.46 (1.31-1.63) CHD, 1.35 (1.19-1.54) stroke, 1.19 (1.05-1.34) all-cause mortality; "probably independent of conventional cardiovascular risk factors." — [Dong JY et al., J Am Coll Cardiol 2011;58:1378-1385 (PubMed 21920268)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21920268/)
- Vlachopoulos 2013 meta-analysis (14 studies, 92,757 men, mean follow-up 6.1 years): RR 1.44 (1.27-1.63) total CV events, 1.19 (0.97-1.46) CV mortality, 1.62 (1.34-1.96) MI, 1.39 (1.23-1.57) cerebrovascular events, 1.25 (1.12-1.39) all-cause mortality; RR higher at younger ages and in intermediate-risk populations. — [Vlachopoulos CV et al., Circ Cardiovasc Qual Outcomes 2013;6:99-109 (PubMed 23300267)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23300267/)
- Lead time: in 300 men with acute chest pain and angiographic CAD, ED prevalence was 49%; ED became evident before CAD symptoms in 99/147 (67%) with a mean interval of 38.8 months (range 1-168); all type 1 diabetics with ED developed it before CAD. — [Montorsi F et al., Eur Urol 2003;44:360-364 (PubMed 12932937)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12932937/)
- COBRA trial (285 CAD patients vs 95 angiographically normal controls): ED prevalence 22% (acute coronary syndrome, 1-vessel) to 65% (chronic coronary syndrome) vs 24% in controls; in chronic coronary syndrome patients, ED preceded CAD in 93% with a mean interval of 24 [12-36] months; "ED comes before CAD in the majority by an average of 2 up to 3 years." — [Montorsi P et al., Eur Heart J 2006;27:2632-2639 (PubMed 16854949)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16854949/)
- "The time interval among the onset of ED symptoms and the occurrence of CAD symptoms and cardiovascular events is estimated at 2-3 years and 3-5 years, respectively." — [Jackson G et al., Int J Clin Pract 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1742-1241.2010.02410.x)
- AUA background: "Symptoms of ED may precede a cardiovascular event by up to five years. Further, when ED is present in younger men, it predicts a marked increase (up to 50 fold) in the risk of future cardiac events"; QRISK incorporated ED as an independent risk factor "with the presence of ED conferring a 25% increased risk for the average middle-aged man." AUA Statement 3: "Men should be counseled that ED is a risk marker for underlying cardiovascular disease (CVD) and other health conditions that may warrant evaluation and treatment. (Clinical Principle)"; "If there is uncertainty regarding a man's exercise tolerance and fitness for sexual activity, then he should be referred for in-depth evaluation of cardiac reserve by a cardiologist"; under Princeton III, "All other men with cardiovascular conditions [beyond low-risk] require a cardiology consultation and additional cardiac evaluation." — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)
- Princeton IV (Mayo Clin Proc 2024; conference 10-11 March 2023): "Mounting evidence supports the need for providers to treat men with ED as being at risk for cardiac events until proven otherwise. Algorithms for the diagnosis and treatment of ED are updated with new recommendations for coronary artery calcium scoring for advanced cardiovascular risk stratification." — [Köhler TS et al., Mayo Clin Proc 2024;99(9) (PubMed 39115509; DOI 10.1016/j.mayocp.2024.06.002)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39115509/)
- ACC summary of Princeton IV: ED "should be considered a risk-enhancing factor when considering the intensity of risk-factor reduction"; "Patients with ED who are otherwise at low to intermediate 10-year ASCVD risk should be considered for coronary artery calcium measurement"; ED "may precede clinically evident CVD by several years"; sexual intercourse equals about 2-3 METs; men who complete 4 minutes of a Bruce protocol (5-6 METs) without ischemia can safely have sex; high-risk patients (NYHA IV, uncontrolled hypertension, unstable angina, recent untreated MI, high-risk arrhythmias) should defer sex and see a cardiologist. — [ACC Expert Analysis, Sept 2024](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2024/09/23/10/45/erectile-disfunction-as-an-ascvd-risk-enhancing-factor)
- EAU 2026 adopts Princeton IV: "ED should be considered a precursor of CVD, and more severe and longer standing ED yields greater risk"; Princeton IV "recommended the use of the 2019 ACC/AHA ASCVD risk score for all men undergoing evaluation for predominantly vasculogenic ED" and CAC scoring "for all men in the borderline to intermediate ASCVD risk category," ending with "the CAC score as the single strongest predictor of CVD-risk." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- Urology Times on Thompson 2005: ED "raising the risk of heart disease by 45%." — [Urology Times](https://www.urologytimes.com/view/significant-link-found-between-ed-heart-disease)

### Inferences
- The consistent message of AUA, EAU and Princeton IV is that new-onset ED, especially in men under about 60, is an indication for a clinician visit (history, BP, lipids, HbA1c, testosterone, ASCVD risk calculation, possibly CAC score) before any self-treatment; a supplement purchased online bypasses this window of 2-5 years in which vascular risk could be modified.

### Gaps
- Full-text Princeton IV documents (Mayo Clin Proc and J Sex Med) are paywalled; recommendations above come from the abstracts, the ACC expert analysis and the EAU's adoption of the algorithm.

## 7. Which hormonal and other medical causes will a supplement not fix (low testosterone, medications, diabetes, Peyronie's), and what testing is appropriate?

### Takeaway
Guidelines require a morning total testosterone (AUA: all men with ED; deficiency = <300 ng/dL on at least two morning samples with symptoms), fasting glucose/HbA1c and lipids, a medication review (thiazides and beta-blockers are the commonest antihypertensive culprits; SSRIs cause sexual dysfunction in up to about 80% of users) and a genital exam for Peyronie's disease (present in about 8% of men presenting with ED and 20% of diabetic men with ED), none of which an over-the-counter supplement addresses.

### Cited Findings
- AUA Statement 1: "thorough medical, sexual, and psychosocial history; a physical examination; and selective laboratory testing (Clinical Principle)". Statement 4: "In men with ED, morning serum total testosterone levels should be measured. (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)". Discussion: "Total testosterone should be measured in all men with ED to determine if TD, defined as total testosterone <300 ng/dL with the presence of symptoms and signs, is present... At least two morning serum total testosterone measures should be obtained before making the diagnosis of TD... Men should not have testosterone measured during acute illness." Late-afternoon levels can be about 20% lower than morning in young men, "as high as 50%." — [AUA ED Guideline 2018 full PDF](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf)
- AUA Statement 12: "Men with ED and testosterone deficiency (TD) who are considering ED treatment with a PDE5i should be informed that PDE5i may be more effective if combined with testosterone therapy. (Moderate Recommendation; Evidence Level: Grade C)". — [AUA ED Guideline (web)](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/erectile-dysfunction-(ed)-guideline)
- AUA Testosterone Deficiency guideline (2018, validity confirmed 2024): low testosterone defined "as being consistently <300 ng/dL on at least two serum total testosterone measurements obtained in an early morning fashion." — [Mulhall JP et al., J Urol 2018 (AUA Journals)](https://www.auajournals.org/doi/10.1016/j.juro.2018.03.115); [AUA guideline list](https://helpdesk.auanet.org/hc/en-us/articles/40948838469143-Guidelines)
- EAU laboratory work-up: "Patients should undergo a fasting blood glucose or haemoglobin A1c and lipid profile measurement if they have not been assessed in the previous 12 months. Hormonal tests should include early morning total testosterone in a fasting state. The bio-available or calculated free testosterone values may sometimes be needed... Additional laboratory tests may be considered in selected patients (e.g. total PSA, PRL and LH)." Recommendation (Strong): "Evaluate laboratory tests, including glucose and lipid profile and total testosterone, to identify and treat any reversible risk factors and lifestyle factors that can be modified." Physical examination "may reveal unsuspected diagnoses, such as Peyronie's disease (PD), pre-malignant or malignant genital lesions, prostatic enlargement or irregularity/nodularity, or signs and symptoms suggestive of hypogonadism." EAU total-testosterone threshold for therapy: 12.1 nmol/L (about 350 ng/dL), two morning (7:00-11:00) samples. — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction); [Park HJ et al., J Clin Med 2019 (PMC6406807) comparing thresholds](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406807/)
- EAU: "patients with hypogonadism who do not respond to PDE5Is may improve their response to PDE5Is after initiating testosterone therapy. Therefore, when diagnostic criteria suggestive of testosterone deficiency are met, testosterone therapy may be more appropriate, even in ED patients." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- EAU pathophysiology table, drug-induced ED: "Antihypertensives (i.e. thiazide diuretics, beta-blockers); Antidepressants (e.g. selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, tricyclics); Antipsychotics; Antiandrogens (GnRH analogues and antagonists; 5-ARIs); Recreational drugs (e.g. heroin, cocaine, marijuana, methadone, synthetic drugs, anabolic steroids, excessive alcohol intake)." Other risk factors listed include diabetes, dyslipidaemia, hypertension, obesity, metabolic syndrome, smoking, depression/anxiety, chronic kidney disease, COPD, sleep disorders, LUTS/BPH and prior COVID-19. — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- MedlinePlus: "Thiazides are the most common cause of erectile dysfunction among the high blood pressure medicines. The next most common cause is beta blockers. Alpha blockers tend to be less likely to cause this problem." Listed drugs include hydrochlorothiazide, metoprolol, labetalol, clonidine-type agents, sertraline, tricyclics, benzodiazepines, H2 blockers (cimetidine, ranitidine), diphenhydramine, ketoconazole, metoclopramide, cyproterone and estrogen. — [MedlinePlus, Drugs that may cause erection problems](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/004024.htm)
- Harvard Health: "ED is an occasional side effect of BP drugs like thiazide diuretics, loop diuretics, and beta-blockers, all of which can decrease blood flow to the penis." — [Harvard Health, Blood pressure drugs and ED](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mens-health/blood-pressure-drugs-and-ed-what-you-need-to-know)
- Beta-blockers in randomized trials (Ko 2002, >35,000 patients): "a small, significant annual increase in risk of reported sexual dysfunction (5 per 1000 patients; 95% CI, 2-8)"; risk of withdrawal for sexual dysfunction RR 4.89 but absolute increase 2 per 1,000 per year. — [Ko DT et al., JAMA 2002](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/195125); [Nicolai MP et al., Heart Fail Rev 2014 (PMC3890007)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3890007/)
- SSRIs: in a meta-analysis of 31 studies (10,130 patients) sexual dysfunction occurred in 80.3% on sertraline (OR 27.4 vs placebo), 78.6% on citalopram (OR 20.3) and 37.0% on escitalopram (OR 3.4). — [J Fam Pract 2017 clinical inquiry summarizing Serretti & Chiesa 2009](https://cdn.mdedge.com/files/s3fs-public/Document/September-2017/6211_JFP_ClinInquiry1.pdf); 63% of sertraline-treated men vs 15% of bupropion-treated men developed sexual dysfunction. — [Higgins A et al., Drug Healthc Patient Saf 2010 (PMC3108697)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3108697/)
- Peyronie's disease prevalence: 0.5-20.3% depending on population; 7.9% among men presenting with ED (El-Sakka 2006) and 20.3% among diabetic men with ED (Arafa 2007); "prevalence rates historically have been under-estimated." — [AUA Peyronie's Disease Guideline 2015](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/peyronies-disease-guideline)
- Diabetes: ED prevalence 53% in men with diabetes and 74% in diabetics with depressive symptoms (meta-analyses). — [Psychiatrist.com commentary](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/aerobic-exercise-for-erectile-dysfunction-rct-data/). Daily tadalafil in diabetics gains only +4.5-4.8 IIEF-EF vs +1.3 placebo, and sildenafil improves erections in 57% of diabetics vs 10% placebo (versus 63-82% in the general ED population). — [Cialis US prescribing information](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf); [Viagra US prescribing information](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?format=PDF&id=652)
- AUA background: in surveys of men with ED, depression reported by 11% and premature ejaculation by 30-60%; LUTS/BPH "reported in up to 72% of men with ED." — [AUA ED Guideline (web)](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/erectile-dysfunction-(ed)-guideline)
- Mayo Clinic on supplements: L-arginine "Don't take... with sildenafil"; yohimbe linked to "increased blood pressure, fast or irregular heartbeat, heart attack and seizures"; DHEA long-term use "may be linked with some cancers." — [Mayo Clinic, Dietary supplements for ED](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/erectile-dysfunction/in-depth/erectile-dysfunction-herbs/art-20044394)

### Inferences
- A minimum clinician work-up for new ED, per AUA/EAU: history incl. medication review, genital/vascular exam, morning total testosterone (repeat if <300 ng/dL), fasting glucose or HbA1c, lipid panel, blood pressure, ASCVD risk estimate; selected men need PSA, prolactin/LH, penile duplex ultrasound.
- Supplements cannot correct hypogonadism, drug-induced ED (which may be fixed by switching, e.g., thiazide/beta-blocker to ARB or nebivolol, SSRI to bupropion), diabetic neuropathy or Peyronie's plaque, and yohimbe-containing products add cardiovascular risk in exactly the population most likely to have occult CVD.

### Gaps
- Prevalence of biochemical hypogonadism specifically among men presenting with ED was not quantified in the sources retrieved.
- Quantitative RCT evidence that switching antihypertensives improves IIEF was not retrieved (only review-level statements).

## 8. Daily low-dose tadalafil's additional evidence for BPH/urinary symptoms, and whether any supplement has comparable evidence

### Takeaway
Tadalafil 5 mg once daily is the only drug licensed for both ED and BPH/LUTS, lowering IPSS by about 2.3-2.5 points more than placebo (e.g., -6.1 vs -3.8 at 12 weeks) while raising IIEF-EF by about 4.6 points more than placebo in men with both conditions; the best-studied supplement for LUTS, saw palmetto, was no better than placebo in a 369-man JAMA trial at up to three times the standard dose and in Cochrane reviews of more than 5,000 men.

### Cited Findings
- Cialis label Table 21 (men with ED and BPH, 12 weeks; placebo N=193, tadalafil 5 mg N=206): total IPSS change -3.8 vs -6.1 (P<.001; baseline about 18); IIEF-EF change +1.9 vs +6.5 (endpoint 17.6 vs 22.9; P<.001); 2.5 mg "did not result in statistically significant improvement in the total IPSS." — [Cialis US prescribing information (Lilly)](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf)
- Dose-finding study (1,058 men, 12 weeks): IPSS change -3.9 (2.5 mg), -4.9 (5 mg), -5.2 (10 mg), -5.2 (20 mg) vs -2.3 placebo; no significant effect on peak flow, PVR or PSA; "tadalafil 5 mg... is the only dose approved for this indication." Porst 2011 (325 men): IPSS -5.6 vs -3.6 (p=0.004), significant by week 4; IIEF-EF +6.7 vs +2.0 in sexually active men with ED. Pooled analyses: IPSS -6.0 vs -3.6; benefit independent of ED status; 1-year open-label extension maintained improvement. — [Gacci M et al., Ther Adv Urol 2014 (PMC4054509)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4054509/); [Porst H et al. 2011 (PubMed 21871706)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21871706/)
- EAU: "Tadalafil 5mg is currently the only licensed drug for the treatment of both ED and LUTS, demonstrating overall good efficacy in relieving urinary symptoms and improving EF... should be considered in patients suffering from mild to moderate LUTS associated with ED, either alone or in combination with alpha-blockers"; "40% of men aged >45 years were combined responders for ED and LUTS/BPH when treated with tadalafil 5mg once daily, with symptom improvement after 12 weeks." — [EAU Guidelines, Management of ED](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)
- Medscape's report on the FDA approval of tadalafil 5 mg once daily for BPH: "In 2 clinical trials, men with BPH who took 5 mg of tadalafil daily experienced a significant improvement in these symptoms compared with men" on placebo. — [Medscape, Tadalafil Approved for BPH](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/751150). The once-daily 5 mg BPH indication appears in the 2011 FDA label revision (supplements s020/s021). — [FDA Cialis label 2011](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2011/021368s20s21lbl.pdf)
- Saw palmetto: CAMUS RCT (369 men, 72 weeks, 320 to 960 mg/day): AUA Symptom Index fell 2.20 points with saw palmetto vs 2.99 with placebo (difference 0.79 favoring placebo; one-sided P=.91); "Saw palmetto extract was no more effective than placebo for any secondary outcome." — [Barry MJ et al., JAMA 2011;306:1344-1351 (PubMed 21954478)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21954478/)
- Bent 2006 NEJM RCT: "saw palmetto did not improve symptoms or objective measures of benign prostatic hyperplasia." — [Bent S et al., N Engl J Med 2006 (PubMed 16467543)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16467543/)
- Cochrane: "Serenoa repens alone results in little to no difference in urinary tract symptoms or quality of life compared to placebo at three to six months"; the 2012 review of 32 RCTs (5,666 men) found no improvement at two and three times the usual dose. — [Cochrane CD001423](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD001423_serenoa-repens-benign-prostatic-hyperplasia); [NCCIH, Spotlight on Saw Palmetto](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/providers/digest/spotlight-on-saw-palmetto-science)

### Inferences
- The placebo-subtracted IPSS benefit of daily tadalafil (about 2.3-2.5 points) is modest but reproducible across >3,000 trial participants and comes with a simultaneous erectile benefit; no dietary supplement has shown either effect against placebo, and the most popular one (saw palmetto) has failed in the largest trials.

### Gaps
- Exact FDA approval date for the BPH indication was not confirmed from a primary FDA document in this search (the 2011 label revision reflects it).
- No controlled data were found for any supplement claiming combined ED-plus-LUTS benefit.
