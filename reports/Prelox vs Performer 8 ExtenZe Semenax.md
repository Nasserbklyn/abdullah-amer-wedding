# Prelox Beats Its Rivals, Trails Tadalafil

Of the four products, **Life Extension Prelox** is the only one worth buying, and only under one condition: that it is taken at the four-tablet-a-day dose used in its trials (about **80 mg Pycnogenol plus roughly 2.8 g of arginine material**) rather than the two-tablet maintenance dose the label drops to after two weeks. That means either budgeting **$64–72 a month** for two bottles, or keeping the two-tablet dose ($32–39 a month) and adding **1.5–3 g a day of certified L-citrulline (about $7 a month) or 2–3 g of L-arginine (about $12)** to restore the arginine half of the formula. Prelox wins largely by default: it is the only one of the four with placebo-controlled trials of its own formula (three RCTs, 184 men, a pooled **+8.9 points** on the 30-point IIEF erectile-function domain — a manufacturer-linked, small-study figure that should be read as a ceiling, not a promise), the only one whose maker offers a 365-day refund regardless of how many doses are left, and the only one with no stimulant, no hormone and no admission by its own maker that its doses are a fraction of those studied. **Performer 8** is a 2026 reformulation whose own press release concedes the arginine studies it cites used "approximately 12 times the 500 mg quantity listed," whose panel (750 mg KSM-66 ashwagandha and **200 mg caffeine** per four capsules) contradicts its "non-stimulant, no known side effects" FAQ, and whose owner has changed twice since December 2025 while its "lifetime" guarantee shrank to 60 days with a 5% fee. **Semenax** discloses every amount but is a semen-volume product with **250 mg of arginine** — one-twelfth of the ED trial dose — whose only trial found no difference from placebo on erectile function and was published a decade late in an OMICS-group journal with the company's COO as first author. **ExtenZe** is disqualified outright: every current tablet hides yohimbe, DHEA and pregnenolone inside proprietary blends, FDA found sildenafil in ExtenZe-labelled product in 2018, the brand's makers have paid about **$8 million** in false-advertising penalties and cash settlements plus a $6 million merchandise fund, and four published case reports link it to hepatitis, cardiomyopathy and priapism. No combination of the four makes sense: stacking triples the pine-bark dose, buys the same nitric-oxide pathway three times, pushes zinc toward or past the 40 mg upper limit on one reading of Semenax's label, and has no trial behind it; Korean red ginseng is better run as a separate 8–12-week experiment than added on top. A fair trial of Prelox is **12 weeks at the full dose**, scored with the SHIM/IIEF-5 before and after against a minimal clinically important difference of 4 points (2 in mild ED). It should be abandoned in favour of a cardiovascular work-up and **generic tadalafil 5 mg at $6–14 a month** if the ED is moderate-to-severe at baseline, if 12 weeks deliver less than that difference, if the man takes nitrates or had a heart attack within six months, or at the first chest pain, fainting spell, jaundice or unusual bleeding.

## Only Semenax prints every amount; Prelox hides its Pycnogenol and ExtenZe hides 18 of 20 ingredients

The first test any ED supplement must pass is whether a buyer can tell what he is swallowing, and the four products split cleanly. Life Extension's Supplement Facts panel for Prelox (item 01373) reads, in full, "Prelox® Proprietary Blend 720 mg — L-Arginine HCl, Aspartic Acid, Pycnogenol® Dried French Maritime Pine extract (bark)" per tablet, with no milligram figure for any component ([Life Extension](https://www.lifeextension.com/vitamins-supplements/item01373/prelox-enhanced-sex)). Horphag Research, which owns the Prelox and Pycnogenol trademarks and licenses the blend to regional sellers, does not disclose the split either ([prelox.com](https://www.prelox.com/)). The one licensee that does is Pharma Nord in Europe, whose label states **40 mg Pycnogenol, 1,400 mg L-arginine aspartate and 100 mg taurine per two tablets** — 20 mg Pycnogenol per tablet, the same ratio as the trial tablet of "20 mg of Pycnogenol® plus 700 mg of L-arginine aspartate" ([Pharma Nord UK](https://www.pharmanord.co.uk/all-products/mens-health/prelox); [Tian 2023 meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)). Because US rules list blend components in descending weight order and the 720 mg total matches 700 mg of arginine material plus 20 mg of extract, the reasonable inference is that Life Extension's tablet also carries about 20 mg Pycnogenol, giving **roughly 80 mg a day during the two-week loading phase and about 40 mg a day thereafter** — but that is an inference the company has never confirmed, and Life Extension uses arginine hydrochloride plus free aspartic acid rather than the arginine aspartate salt in the trials, so the free-arginine content per tablet cannot be computed from the label ([Life Extension](https://www.lifeextension.com/vitamins-supplements/item01373/prelox-enhanced-sex); [Stanislavov 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17703218/)). An older label for the same UPC, still displayed by one retailer, also listed 30 mg icariin from Epimedium; the current panel does not, so the product has been reformulated at least once ([Blue Sky Vitamin](https://blueskyvitamin.com/products/prelox-natural-sex-60-tablets-life-extension)).

Performer 8 went the other way in 2026: the nine-herb proprietary formula archived in October 2023 (3,000 mg muira puama, 6,000 mg Panax ginseng, 1,000 mg barrenwort, 300 mg pine bark, 500 mg KSM-66, 12 mg ferrous bisglycinate, 30 mg maca, 600 mg glucuronolactone, 30 mg grape seed) has been replaced by an itemised 11-ingredient panel with "0 Proprietary blends" ([Wayback Machine, 15 Oct 2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20231015163908/https://performer8.com/); [performer8.com product page](https://performer8.com/products/performer-8)). Per four-capsule serving it lists **750 mg KSM-66 ashwagandha, 500 mg L-arginine, 500 mg L-lysine, 200 mg caffeine anhydrous, 50 mg SENACTIV (Panax notoginseng and Rosa roxburghii), 80 mg magnesium as oxide, 80 mg vitamin C, 10 mg zinc as oxide, 1.4 mg B6, 1 mg copper and 200 µg folic acid** ([performer8.com product page](https://performer8.com/products/performer-8)). The panel is internally inconsistent: a four-capsule serving with 30 servings implies 120 capsules, yet the bottle holds 90 and the directions say "Take three capsules each morning" — so a man following directions takes 75% of every printed figure (about 562 mg ashwagandha, 375 mg arginine, 150 mg caffeine, 7.5 mg zinc), the "%NRV" column is a UK/EU convention on a product sold as "Made in the USA," and the capsule-shell ingredients are not published anywhere ([performer8.com product page](https://performer8.com/products/performer-8); [performer8.com home](https://performer8.com/)). Innerbody's guide, updated November 2025 and still ranking Performer 8 "best overall," describes the discontinued formula ([Innerbody](https://www.innerbody.com/best-male-enhancement-pills)).

ExtenZe is the opaque extreme. The current "The Original" 30-tablet label on file with the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database quantifies only folate (400 mcg DFE) and **zinc at 25 mg as zinc oxide (227% of the Daily Value)**; everything else sits in three blends — a 60 mg "Male Prohormone Blend" of micronized DHEA and pregnenolone, a 25 mg pepper-and-ginger "Bio-Enhancement Blend," and a 468 mg "Male Enhancement Blend" of 13 components in which yohimbe bark extract is fourth by weight and L-arginine hydrochloride fifth ([DSLD label 305000](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/305000)). The Maximum Strength Extended Release gelcap label lists yohimbe bark extract, DHEA (twice, in both blends), pregnenolone and isolated yohimbine hydrochloride in two blends of 135 mg and 223 mg with no amounts at all ([DSLD label 304999](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/304999)), and the Testosterone Boost tablet adds L-citrulline and D-aspartic acid to a 684 mg blend that still leads with yohimbe and still contains DHEA and pregnenolone ([DSLD label 328965](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/328965)). The only quantitative benchmark ever printed is the 2014 Biotab label, which declared **50 mg DHEA, 10 mg pregnenolone and 12.5 mg yohimbine alkaloids per tablet**; the prohormone blend weight (60 mg) has not changed since, but the sub-amounts have vanished ([DSLD label 34209](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/34209)). The brand's current owner concedes the point in a 2026 "label-literacy guide": consumers "cannot calculate the individual amount of DHEA, pregnenolone, L-arginine, maca, yohimbe, Tribulus, Epimedium or the other components within those blends" ([USA Today press release, Leading Edge Health](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/45414/extenze-reviews-leading-edge-health-publishes-2026-label-literacy-guide-for-current-mens-wellness-formula/)).

Semenax is the only product whose regulated label discloses every amount. The August 2025 DSLD entry gives, per four-capsule daily serving, vitamin E 60 IU; **zinc aspartate 30 mg at "20% elemental zinc" (6 mg zinc)**; Butea superba 500 mg; L-carnitine 500 mg; maca 400 mg; pine bark extract 300 mg; **L-arginine HCl 250 mg**; L-lysine 250 mg; catuaba 200 mg; Epimedium 150 mg; muira puama 100 mg; and 50 mg each of hawthorn, cranberry seed, Tribulus, Avena sativa, sarsaparilla and Swedish flower pollen plus 30 mg pumpkin, in gelatin capsules ([DSLD label 336130](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/336130)). The problem is that the marketing does not match the label: the brand's ingredient page prints no amounts, lists "Zinc Oxide & Zinc Aspartate" when no zinc oxide appears on the label or the Health Canada licence, and omits the Epimedium and Tribulus that the label contains ([Semenax Ingredients](https://www.semenax.com/ingredients/); [Health Canada licence PL-630484](https://www.semenax.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/PL-630484.pdf)). The dose is also contested within the company's own documents — the label says four capsules daily, the Leading Edge Health FAQ says "Take 2 Semenax pills," and the clinical trial used four capsules twice a day, with the study report presenting today's four-capsule amounts as the content of eight capsules ([DSLD 336130](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/336130); [LEH Semenax page](https://www.leadingedgehealth.com/products/semenax/); [Semenax Clinical Study Report](http://www.semenax.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Semenax_CSR_v1_1-Oct-2012.pdf)).

## Prelox owns three small RCTs at a dose its label abandons after 14 days; the other three have none

The Prelox evidence base is real but narrow, and its most important feature for a buyer is dose. Stanislavov and Nikolova's 2003 open-label pilot gave 40 men 1.7 g of L-arginine a day for a month (5% regained normal erections), added 80 mg Pycnogenol in month two (80%) and raised it to 120 mg in month three (92.5%), with no placebo arm — these are the figures Life Extension's ED protocol repeats ([Stanislavov 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12851125/); [Life Extension ED protocol](https://www.lifeextension.com/protocols/male-reproductive/erectile-dysfunction)). The two controlled trials that matter both used **four tablets a day for the whole study**: a 50-man, one-month double-blind crossover at 80 mg Pycnogenol plus 3 g L-arginine aspartate that "restored erectile function to normal" and doubled intercourse frequency, co-authored by Horphag's Rohdewald ([Stanislavov 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17703218/)), and Ledda's 124-man, six-month parallel RCT in which the IIEF erectile domain rose from **15.2 to 25.2 at three months and 27.1 at six months versus 15.1 to 19.1 and 19.0 on placebo**, using Prelox supplied by Horphag Research UK and co-authored by Horphag's Schönlau ([Ledda 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20184576/)). The third RCT, Aoki's 24-man Japanese trial, used a lower dose (60 mg Pycnogenol, 690 mg arginine, 552 mg aspartic acid) for eight weeks and reported an improved total IIEF-5 score, but the 2023 meta-analysis notes that its erectile-domain, satisfaction and desire differences versus placebo were not statistically significant, "which may be related to the low dose" ([Aoki 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21618639/); [Tian 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)). Pooled across the two four-tablet trials the erectile-domain gain was **+8.9 points (95% CI 4.14–13.66)**, with one trial rated low, one unclear and one high risk of bias, an Egger's test suggesting small-study effects (P = 0.033), and no adverse events in any of the 184 men ([Tian 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)). Examine's independent reading is that the result "should be interpreted with caution" given only two studies ([Examine](https://examine.com/research-feed/study/15xM8d/)), and Horphag's own research page states that after a one-month washout the effect "largely faded" ([prelox.com research](https://www.prelox.com/mobile/research)). An effect as large as on-demand sildenafil from a nutrient whose arginine component yields +2.7 to +4 points on its own — the EAU's pooled arginine estimate is +2.74 and a 6 g/day RCT moved IIEF-6 from 20 to 24 — is implausible enough that the +8.9 should be treated as an upper bound awaiting independent replication ([EAU Guidelines](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction); [Menafra 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8995264/)).

Two mismatches separate the trials from the bottle. First, formula: the trial tablet is what Pharma Nord sells; Life Extension's tablet has the same total weight but a different arginine salt and an undisclosed Pycnogenol content, so an exact match cannot be confirmed ([Pharma Nord UK](https://www.pharmanord.co.uk/all-products/mens-health/prelox); [Life Extension](https://www.lifeextension.com/vitamins-supplements/item01373/prelox-enhanced-sex)). Second, and more consequential, dose: the positive RCTs kept four tablets a day for one and six months, whereas Life Extension and Pharma Nord both direct four tablets for only two weeks and then halve the dose, so the maintenance regimen — about 40 mg Pycnogenol and roughly 1.4 g of arginine material — has never itself been tested in a controlled trial, and the one trial closest to it in dose (Aoki) was the one that did not beat placebo on the erectile domain ([Life Extension](https://www.lifeextension.com/vitamins-supplements/item01373/prelox-enhanced-sex); [Tian 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)). Life Extension's claim of "five independent clinical studies" also counts two sperm-quality papers in infertile men and the uncontrolled 2003 pilot, none of which is an ED RCT ([Life Extension](https://www.lifeextension.com/vitamins-supplements/item01373/prelox-enhanced-sex)).

Performer 8 has no trial of any version of its formula, and its maker now says so. The September 2026 launch release states that the arginine studies it discusses gave "6 grams of L-arginine," that "These studies used approximately 12 times the 500 mg quantity listed in Performer 8," that the ashwagandha evidence it cites is a 600 mg safety-and-tolerability study, that the Panax notoginseng trial it mentions used 1,350 mg a day for endurance exercise and "did not evaluate SENACTIV or Performer 8," and that "No individual-ingredient study cited in this announcement evaluated the complete Performer 8 formula" ([USA Today press release](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/43980/performer-8-reviews-wolfson-brands-announces-u-s-launch-of-current-11-ingredient-mens-wellness-formula/)). At the directed three capsules a day the arginine exposure is 375 mg, roughly one-eighth of the 2.8–3 g in the Prelox trials, one-sixteenth of the 6 g that improved vasculogenic ED, and below the 1.5 g that proved ineffective over 17 days ([Tian 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/); [Menafra 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8995264/); [Klotz 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10743698/)). The 2023 site had cited a 1990 conference presentation for muira puama, in-vitro and animal work for icariin and grape seed, a 2013 oligospermia pilot for ashwagandha, and — for ingredients the formula did not even contain — a high-dose arginine ED trial and a pomegranate paper, which suggests the reference list was never built from the formula ([Wayback Machine, 15 Oct 2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20231015163908/https://performer8.com/)). The reformulation removed the one botanical with any ED trial data (pine bark) and replaced it with a stimulant.

ExtenZe has no published trial of any kind. A Europe PMC search for "Extenze" returns six records, none a trial — two priapism case reports, one takotsubo cardiomyopathy case, a penile-lipogranuloma series on an injected product and two unrelated hits ([Europe PMC search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%28TITLE%3AExtenze%20OR%20ABSTRACT%3AExtenze%20OR%20TITLE%3A%22Extenze%22%29&format=json&pageSize=15&resultType=lite)). The 2014 label nevertheless claimed "Doctor Approved, Clinically Tested" ([DSLD label 34209](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/34209)), and the brand's current owner acknowledges that "No independent, peer-reviewed clinical trial evaluating the current retail formula and current serving conditions was identified," the only research being "a historical U.S. patent filing describing a limited pilot investigation" of 20 volunteers ([USA Today press release, Leading Edge Health](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/45414/extenze-reviews-leading-edge-health-publishes-2026-label-literacy-guide-for-current-mens-wellness-formula/)). The one ingredient in ExtenZe with placebo-controlled ED evidence of its own is yohimbine — a 1998 meta-analysis of seven trials found it superior to placebo (odds ratio 3.85) — but the amount in the current tablet is undisclosed, most US yohimbe products misstate their yohimbine content, and NCCIH notes it is illegal to market an over-the-counter yohimbine product for ED in the United States ([Ernst & Pittler 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9649257/); [NCCIH Yohimbe](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yohimbe)).

Semenax has one trial, and it is evidence against the product for this purpose. A six-site Indian study run by the CRO Vedic Lifesciences for "Leading Edge Marketing, Nassau, Bahamas" in 2011–12 randomised 78 men with ED and self-perceived low ejaculate to **four capsules twice daily** or placebo for two months; in the 63 per-protocol completers ejaculate volume rose 0.49 mL versus a 0.21 mL fall on placebo and 50% versus 16% gained at least 20%, but sperm parameters, total IIEF, the IIEF erectile-function domain and orgasm-intensity responder rates "were not statistically significant ... across 2 treatment arms," the sample size was "arbitrarily chosen," the effect was confined to men with normal baseline volume, and 15 adverse events were recorded, five "probably related" (hyperacidity, abdominal pain, bloating) ([Semenax Clinical Study Report](http://www.semenax.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Semenax_CSR_v1_1-Oct-2012.pdf); [iMedPub paper](https://clinical-nutrition.imedpub.com/effect-of-a-polyherbal-dietary-supplement-on-semen-characteristics-a-randomized-doubleblind-placebocontrolled-clinical-trial.php?aid=46123)). The report sat unpublished for a decade and then appeared in May 2022 — received 7 April, published 9 May — in the Journal of Clinical Nutrition & Dietetics, an iMedPub title; iMedPub is an OMICS brand, and OMICS was blacklisted by PubMed Central in 2014 and enjoined after an FTC suit in 2017; the first author is Leading Edge Health's chief operating officer and the company funded the work ([iMedPub paper](https://clinical-nutrition.imedpub.com/effect-of-a-polyherbal-dietary-supplement-on-semen-characteristics-a-randomized-doubleblind-placebocontrolled-clinical-trial.php?aid=46123); [Wikipedia: OMICS Publishing Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OMICS_Publishing_Group)). On that basis the brand markets Semenax as "Clinically Proven* To Increase Semen Volume And Orgasm Intensity" with "No Reported Side Effects" ([Semenax](https://www.semenax.com/)). Its ingredient doses are far below anything studied for erections — 250 mg arginine against 1,500–6,000 mg in ED trials, and 300 mg of a pine bark extract that the Canadian licence identifies as Pinus massoniana (Masson pine) rather than the French maritime pine of the Prelox trials ([Health Canada licence](https://www.semenax.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/PL-630484.pdf); [Rhim 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30770070)). Urologist Jessica Yih's comment to Healthline — "the dosages may be too low to be effective" — is the gentlest independent verdict; Healthline's own is "We do not recommend Semenax" ([Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/erectile-dysfunction/semenax-review)).

## Life Extension's single FDA letter looks tame beside ExtenZe's $8 million in settlements and Performer 8's vanishing owner

Corporate history predicts how a refund request, a bad batch or a side effect will be handled, and here the gap between the four is wide. Life Extension, founded in 1980 in Fort Lauderdale, has one regulatory blemish: FDA Warning Letter 17-07 of 1 February 2017, which found that its online disease "protocols" — including an erectile-dysfunction protocol — made drug claims for "over 400 products"; it concerned marketing language, not adulteration or manufacturing, and no FDA, FTC, recall or hidden-drug notice naming Prelox was found ([FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/life-extension-foundation-buyers-club-inc-500619-02012017); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lifeextension.com)). Its quality assurance is a checkable practice rather than a seal: the company holds NSF GMP registration for its facilities and says it will provide "a summary of the reports we receive from the contract manufacturers and the independent laboratories" for any product on request, though no USP, NSF or Informed Choice product certification exists for Prelox and Medical News Today reports that obtaining a certificate of analysis "can be difficult" ([Life Extension safety page](https://www.lifeextension.com/about/life-extension-research/safety); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/life-extension-reviews)). The refund policy is the most generous of the four: "a 100% no-hassle return policy valid for up to a full year," no receipt required, used product accepted "depending on the reason," with some exclusions and a $5.50 label fee on certain items ([Life Extension Returns FAQ](https://www.lifeextension.com/returns-faq)). Company-level Trustpilot is 4.3/5 from 823 reviews and BBB shows four complaints in three years, mostly billing confusion, with one reviewer calling AutoShip "nearly impossible to cancel" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lifeextension.com); [BBB complaints](https://www.bbb.org/us/fl/fort-lauderdale/profile/vitamins-and-supplements/life-extension-0633-23000430/complaints)). ## Yohimbe, DHEA and a sildenafil finding put ExtenZe in a different risk class; caffeine and ashwagandha are Performer 8's quiet problems

The hazards do not rank the products the way the marketing does. The dominant danger in this market is undeclared PDE5 inhibitors — FDA's sexual-enhancement notification list held **441 products** on 15 October 2026 and the agency says it "covers only a small fraction" of contaminated products — and of the four brands only ExtenZe has had its own labelled product test positive, in 2018 ([FDA notifications](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud-notifications/sexual-enhancement-and-energy-product-notifications); [FDA Extenze notice](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/medication-health-fraud/public-notification-extenze-nutritional-supplement-contains-hidden-drug-ingredient)). Whether that lot was genuine or counterfeit is genuinely unresolved, but the practical conclusion is the same: a product whose box has repeatedly carried hidden sildenafil or tadalafil, whose labels never mention nitrates, and whose own formula is pharmacologically active cannot be assumed safer than the prescription drug it is bought to avoid. Semenax has a counterfeit finding (yohimbine, Australia 2022) and sells only through its own channels for that reason; Prelox and Performer 8 have none ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/news/safety-alerts/semenax-capsules-counterfeit-version); [Semenax Counterfeit Warning](https://www.semenax.com/counterfeit/)).

Among legal ingredients, yohimbe stands alone, and ExtenZe is the only one of the four that contains it. NCCIH ties yohimbine to "cardiac arrhythmia (irregular heartbeat), blood pressure problems, heart attacks, and seizures," notes that callers to California poison control about yohimbe "were generally more likely to need medical care than other callers," and reports that a 2015 analysis of 49 US brands found most did not state yohimbine content ([NCCIH Yohimbe](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yohimbe)); only 4.1% of those brands stated it accurately, with actual content ranging from undetectable to 12.1 mg ([Cohen 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26391406/)). Memorial Sloan Kettering lists six deaths attributed to quantified yohimbine concentrations, hypertensive urgency, seizures and surgically treated priapism, calls yohimbe products "among supplements with the largest number of documented contraindications, toxic effects, and severe events requiring hospitalization," and records interactions with antihypertensives (effect diminished), antidepressants, bupropion (toxic co-ingestion), opioids and CYP2D6-substrate drugs ([MSKCC Yohimbe](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/yohimbe)). The published ExtenZe harms track this pharmacology: a 54-year-old hospitalised with heart failure from reverse takotsubo cardiomyopathy after an ExtenZe overdose, two priapism case reports, and a 45-year-old with suspected drug-induced autoimmune hepatitis (AST 1,110, ALT 1,771 U/L) after a month of "maximum strength" ExtenZe — the last confounded by binge drinking and a nodular liver on CT ([Europe PMC records](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID%3A25552809%20OR%20EXT_ID%3A29553994&resultType=core&format=json); [Cureus case report](https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/case_report/pdf/201592/20240107-3153-73uhh2.pdf); [Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/association-found-between-otc-male-enhancement-supplementation-and-liver-injuries)). Because the same tablet pairs an alpha-2 blocker that raises blood pressure with vasodilators (arginine, Epimedium, cnidium) in unknown proportions, its net haemodynamic effect in any given man is unpredictable.

ExtenZe's hormones are the second disqualifier. DHEA is legal in US supplements only because Congress carved it out of the 2004 Anabolic Steroid Control Act; it is a controlled drug in Canada and banned by WADA and the NCAA, and Olympic champion LaShawn Merritt was suspended in 2010 after testing positive from ExtenZe ([Nutritional Outlook](https://nutritionaloutlook.com/view/dhea-caught-headlock); [Health Canada 2010](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/alert-recall/unauthorized-extenze-sexual-enhancement-supplements-may-pose-health-risks); [Wikipedia: ExtenZe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExtenZe)). Mayo Clinic's verdict is "red light: Avoid," because DHEA "might raise the risk of hormone-sensitive cancers, such as prostate and breast cancers," may lower HDL, may provoke mania in men with mood disorders, and should not be combined with estrogen, testosterone, certain antidepressants or triazolam ([Mayo Clinic DHEA](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-dhea/art-20364199)); MSKCC adds a reported cancer flare in a prostate-cancer patient and tamoxifen resistance ([MSKCC DHEA](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/dehydroepiandrosterone)). ExtenZe's own label lists acne, hair loss, aggressiveness, irritability and "increased levels of estrogen" as possible side effects and tells men with prostate cancer, prostate enlargement, heart disease, blood-pressure disorders or antidepressant use to consult a physician first — warnings that describe a drug, not a vitamin ([DSLD 305000](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/305000)). The only quantitative anchor, 50 mg DHEA per tablet on the 2014 label, is a pharmacological dose, and the current 60 mg blend weight gives no reason to think it has fallen ([DSLD 34209](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/34209)).

Performer 8's risks are subtler because they hide behind an honest-looking label. The first is a stimulant the brand denies: **200 mg caffeine anhydrous per serving (150 mg at the directed three capsules)** while the FAQ states "Performer 8 is a non-stimulant" and "entirely safe, with no known side effects" ([performer8.com product page](https://performer8.com/products/performer-8); [performer8.com home](https://performer8.com/)). The press release's own defence — that FDA considers 400 mg a day "not generally associated with negative effects for most healthy adults" — concedes the point, and it means a man already drinking two or three coffees crosses that line, that the product is taken every morning indefinitely ("It does not need to be cycled"), and that reported dizziness and raised blood pressure in users now have an obvious candidate cause ([USA Today press release](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/43980/performer-8-reviews-wolfson-brands-announces-u-s-launch-of-current-11-ingredient-mens-wellness-formula/); [Promescent](https://www.promescent.com/blogs/learn/performer-8-review)). The second is ashwagandha at 562–750 mg a day. A five-case series described cholestatic or mixed liver injury with jaundice, nausea and prolonged pruritus "after a latency of 2–12 weeks," with liver tests normalising over one to five months ([Björnsson 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31991029/)); LiverTox lists ashwagandha as "implicated in cases of clinically apparent liver injury" with fatal acute liver failure described ([LiverTox](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548536/)); Denmark banned it from supplements on thyroid and sex-hormone grounds and Sweden followed ([NutraIngredients](https://nutraingredients.com/Article/2023/05/17/Growing-concerns-over-safety-of-ashwagandha-in-EU-member-states)). The 2–12-week latency is exactly the window of a fair supplement trial, so any itching, dark urine or yellowing during a Performer 8 trial is a stop signal, not a coincidence. The remaining minerals are benign: 7.5–10 mg zinc is below the 11 mg RDA, and the 12 mg iron of the old formula — 150% of a man's RDA, supplied to a population that rarely needs it and dangerous in hereditary hemochromatosis — has been removed from the US formula, though a buyer should check the panel in hand because both formulas may still be in circulation ([NIH ODS Zinc](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/); [NIH ODS Iron](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-Consumer/); [USA Today press release](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/43980/performer-8-reviews-wolfson-brands-announces-u-s-launch-of-current-11-ingredient-mens-wellness-formula/)).

Zinc is where the labels and the marketing diverge dangerously. ExtenZe's **25 mg** per tablet is below the 40 mg adult Tolerable Upper Intake Level on its own, but combining The Original with the brand's own Testosterone Boost tablet (also 25 mg) reaches 50 mg, the level at which NIH says weeks of intake "can inhibit copper absorption, reduce immune function, and lower high-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels" ([DSLD 305000](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/305000); [DSLD 328965](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/328965); [NIH ODS Zinc](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/)). Semenax's "Zinc Aspartate 30 mg (20% elemental zinc)" is 6 mg of zinc if read literally — the label's 40% DV is arithmetically consistent with the pre-2020 15 mg Daily Value — but the brand, Healthline and review sites present it as "30 mg zinc"; a stack of Semenax with ExtenZe and Performer 8 therefore totals **31 mg on the literal reading or 65 mg on the marketing reading**, and a buyer cannot resolve which from the bottle ([DSLD 336130](https://api.ods.od.nih.gov/dsld/v9/label/336130); [Semenax Ingredients](https://www.semenax.com/ingredients/); [ScienceInsights](https://scienceinsights.org/whats-in-semenax-ingredients-doses-and-evidence/)). Prelox contains no minerals at all.

Semenax's own hazards are moderate and mostly theoretical, which is why it ranks above Performer 8 on safety despite being useless for erections. Its largest ingredient, 500 mg Butea superba, is the subject of a published case of hyperandrogenemia ([Europe PMC search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%28%28TITLE%3A%22Butea%20superba%22%29%20OR%20%28TITLE%3Azinc%20AND%20TITLE%3A%28semen%20OR%20seminal%29%20AND%20TITLE%3A%28meta-analysis%20OR%20systematic%29%29%20OR%20%28TITLE%3A%22Muira%20puama%22%29%20OR%20%28TITLE%3Acernilton%29%29%20AND%20SRC%3AMED&format=json&pageSize=15&resultType=lite)); its 300 mg pine bark plus vitamin E — which the brand itself calls "a mild blood thinner" — adds to bleeding risk with anticoagulants, since pine bark extract "can inhibit platelet aggregation" ([Semenax Ingredients](https://www.semenax.com/ingredients/); [MSKCC Pine Bark Extract](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/pine-bark-extract)); its 500 mg L-carnitine feeds the gut-microbial TMAO pathway linked to atherosclerosis in mice and humans ([Koeth 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3650111/)); and its Epimedium carries MSKCC's cautions for hormone-sensitive cancers and heart disease, with case reports of tachyarrhythmia and hypomania ([MSKCC Epimedium](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/epimedium)). The Canadian licence adds cautions for men on antidepressants, blood thinners, cardiac glycosides or blood-pressure medication, and a contraindication for bee-product or pollen allergy ([Health Canada licence](https://www.semenax.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/PL-630484.pdf)). In the trial, 15 adverse events — mostly hyperacidity, abdominal pain and bloating — occurred despite the "No Reported Side Effects" banner ([iMedPub paper](https://clinical-nutrition.imedpub.com/effect-of-a-polyherbal-dietary-supplement-on-semen-characteristics-a-randomized-doubleblind-placebocontrolled-clinical-trial.php?aid=46123)).

Prelox's risks are those of its two ingredients, and they are the risks of any nitric-oxide product. The trials recorded no adverse events in 184 men, but customer reviews report headache and dizziness, consistent with vasodilation, and Pharma Nord's own Trustpilot widget carries an unverified anecdote of a five-hour erection requiring an emergency visit ([Tian 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/); [Walmart reviews](https://www.walmart.com/reviews/product/47438877); [Pharma Nord UK](https://www.pharmanord.co.uk/all-products/mens-health/prelox)). Arginine "may potentiate the hypotensive effects of nitrates and vasodilators," an interaction Drugs.com rates moderate with a PDE5 inhibitor or tamsulosin but which becomes serious with nitrates, where the consequence is the same hypotension that makes nitrates an absolute contraindication for Viagra ([Drugs.com, arginine and sildenafil](https://www.drugs.com/drug-interactions/arginine-with-sildenafil-232-0-2061-0.html?professional=1); [Drugs.com, arginine and tamsulosin](https://www.drugs.com/drug-interactions/l-arginine-with-tamsulosin-232-4042-2146-0.html?professional=1); [Viagra prescribing information](https://www.drugs.com/pro/viagra.html)). The one mortality signal for arginine is the VINTAGE MI trial, in which **6 of 70 heart-attack survivors on 9 g a day died within six months versus none on placebo**, so Mayo Clinic says arginine "isn't recommended for people who have had a recent heart attack" and also warns against combining it with potassium-sparing diuretics (hyperkalaemia), anticoagulants (bleeding) and diabetes drugs (glucose) ([Schulman 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16391217/); [Mayo Clinic L-arginine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/drugs-supplements-l-arginine/art-20364681)). Pycnogenol's antiplatelet effect is documented in humans — 100 mg prevented smoking-induced platelet aggregation as effectively as 500 mg aspirin — which is a benefit for most men and a hazard for those on warfarin or clopidogrel ([Pütter 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10498385/); [MSKCC Pine Bark Extract](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/pine-bark-extract)).

## Verdict: buy Prelox at four tablets a day or top it up, never stack, score it at 12 weeks, and switch to tadalafil if it fails

The ranking for a man who wants a safe daily supplement for erectile function is: **first, Life Extension Prelox**, the only product with controlled trials of its formula, a clean adulteration record, a stable 46-year-old seller and a 365-day refund; **second, Semenax**, which is not an ED product and should not be bought as one, but is the least hazardous and most transparent of the remaining three — fully disclosed, hormone- and stimulant-free, batch-screened once, sold by an A+-rated company — with the honest caveat that its own trial showed no erectile benefit; **third, Performer 8**, an under-dosed arginine pill carrying a stimulant it denies and a liver-signal botanical, sold by a company whose ownership, billing name and guarantee have all changed in a year; and **fourth, ExtenZe, which should not be bought at all**. For a man in the UK or EU the same verdict points to Pharma Nord's Prelox, which prints the 40 mg Pycnogenol and 1,400 mg arginine aspartate per two tablets that Life Extension hides, at £36.25 per 60 ([Pharma Nord UK](https://www.pharmanord.co.uk/all-products/mens-health/prelox)).

No combination of these four products makes sense, for three reasons the research makes concrete. First, there is no trial of any such combination, and both Wolfson Brands and Leading Edge Health say so about their own products: "studies conducted on individual ingredients do not establish the effects of the complete Performer 8 formula," and no trial of ExtenZe's "current retail formula and current serving conditions" exists ([USA Today press release](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/43980/performer-8-reviews-wolfson-brands-announces-u-s-launch-of-current-11-ingredient-mens-wellness-formula/); [USA Today press release, ExtenZe guide](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/45414/extenze-reviews-leading-edge-health-publishes-2026-label-literacy-guide-for-current-mens-wellness-formula/)). Second, stacking buys redundancy rather than coverage: Prelox, Performer 8 and Semenax supply three separate arginine routes to the same nitric-oxide pathway, two or three pine bark extracts totalling roughly **680 mg a day against the 80–120 mg used in every positive Prelox trial** and above the 200 mg a day at which published safety labs exist, two Epimedium sources, two Tribulus sources and two muira puama sources, while the total free arginine still lands at only about 2–2.3 g — below the 5–6 g used in the positive arginine monotherapy trials ([Tian 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/); [Scherg 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13696226/); [Menafra 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8995264/)). Third, any stack containing ExtenZe cannot be dose-checked at all, because its DHEA, pregnenolone and yohimbine amounts are hidden, and it adds the one ingredient (yohimbine) whose blood-pressure effect runs opposite to everything else in the stack ([USA Today press release, ExtenZe guide](https://www.usatoday.com/press-release/story/45414/extenze-reviews-leading-edge-health-publishes-2026-label-literacy-guide-for-current-mens-wellness-formula/); [MSKCC Yohimbe](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/yohimbe)). The EAU's appraisal of the whole category is that antioxidant and herbal combinations yield improvements that "may not be perceived as significant by patients," and a 24-RCT review found several herbal formulations associated with a *decrease* in IIEF scores ([EAU Guidelines](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction); [Borrelli 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29633089/)).

Combining Prelox with a single-ingredient top-up is a different matter, because the goal is simply to reach the dose that was studied. The cleanest route is to **stay on four tablets a day for the whole trial** — the only regimen that reproduces both the 80 mg Pycnogenol and the 2.8 g arginine aspartate of the positive RCTs — at a cost of about $64–72 a month (two bottles, $32 each on AutoShip or at four-plus units) ([Life Extension](https://www.lifeextension.com/vitamins-supplements/item01373/prelox-enhanced-sex); [Tian 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)). The budget route is to take the label's two tablets and add **1.5–3 g of L-citrulline or 2–3 g of L-arginine a day**, which brings total arginine-equivalent exposure into the 3–5 g range the ED literature supports and mirrors Horphag's own later "Prelox R" formulation of 80 mg Pycnogenol with 1.92 g arginine and 1.2 g citrulline ([Rhim 2019](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30770070); [OA Text review](https://www.oatext.com/review-influence-of-combinations-of-pycnogenolr-and-l-arginine-aspartate-on-mens-virility-and-fertility.php)). Citrulline is the better top-up per gram: it escapes intestinal arginase, raises plasma arginine roughly twice as effectively as arginine itself, and 1.5 g a day for a month moved erection hardness from grade 3 to 4 in 50% of men with mild ED versus 8% on placebo ([Schwedhelm 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17662090/); [Khalaf 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6683098/); [Cormio 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21195829/)). Kaged Fermented L-Citrulline, on Informed Sport's certified list, costs about **$7 a month at 3 g a day**; Life Extension's own L-Arginine 700 mg capsules reach 2.8 g for about $12 a month without a seal ([Innerbody citrulline guide](https://www.innerbody.com/best-l-citrulline-supplement); [Informed Sport list](https://sport.wetestyoutrust.com/node/144); [Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/ip/Life-Extension-L-Arginine-Caps-700-mg-Promotes-Arterial-Health-Gluten-Free-Non-GMO-200-Capsules/31169990)). The limitation is that a top-up restores only the arginine half: the two-tablet dose still delivers about 40 mg Pycnogenol (inferred), half the trial amount, so the "synergy" Horphag claims is itself halved unless a separate 100 mg Pycnogenol capsule is added — Healthy Origins sells one for $11–25 a month, bringing the total to roughly 140 mg, within the 100–200 mg range Examine lists as usual ([Healthy Origins](https://healthyorigins.com/products/pycnogenol-100mg-veggie); [Examine Pycnogenol](https://examine.com/supplements/pycnogenol/)). Divided dosing matters for tolerability: arginine causes GI upset above single doses of about 9 g, and the trials took their tablets morning and evening ([NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/); [Tian 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10614297/)).

Korean red ginseng should be run as a separate experiment, not stacked. It is the only herb with Cochrane-level evidence, and that evidence is modest — **+3.52 IIEF-EF points** across nine trials, which Cochrane calls "trivial effects on erectile function" at mostly low certainty — from regimens of **900 mg three times daily for 8 weeks** or **1,000 mg three times daily for 12 weeks** ([Lee et al., Cochrane 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33871063/); [Hong 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12394711/); [de Andrade 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16855773/)). The EAU's weak recommendation names "L-arginine or ginseng," not both, and no trial has combined ginseng with Pycnogenol-arginine; adding it to Prelox doubles the bleeding caution (ginseng "may increase your risk of bleeding"), adds insomnia as the commonest side effect and a glucose-lowering interaction with insulin and sulfonylureas, and makes it impossible to tell which product did what ([EAU Guidelines](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction); [MSKCC Ginseng](https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/integrative-medicine/herbs/ginseng-asian); [NCCIH Asian Ginseng](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/asian-ginseng)). The sensible sequence is Prelox for 12 weeks; if it fails, either move to the doctor or try 8–12 weeks of a standardised ginseng extract — BulkSupplements' 1 g extract at ≥7% ginsenosides (75 mg found) is ConsumerLab-approved and costs $6–12 a month, whereas unstandardised products ranged ten-fold in ginsenoside content ([ConsumerLab ginseng review](https://www.consumerlab.com/reviews/ginseng-supplements/ginseng/)).

A fair trial is **8–12 weeks at the studied dose, measured with the same instrument before and after**. One month was enough to show an effect in the Prelox crossover and the pilot, eight weeks is the shortest duration that worked for both the Pycnogenol-arginine supplement and ginseng, twelve weeks is the modal duration across the arginine, ginseng and tadalafil trials, and Ledda's IIEF kept rising from three to six months — so a three-month null result at the full dose is a strong but not absolute signal, while a three-month null at the half dose tells the buyer nothing ([Stanislavov 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17703218/); [Aoki 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21618639/); [Ledda 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20184576/)). The AUA states that "validated questionnaires are recommended to assess the severity of ED, to measure treatment effectiveness, and to guide future management," and the EAU names the IIEF and the Erection Hardness Score ([AUA Guideline](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/erectile-dysfunction-(ed)-guideline); [EAU Guidelines](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)). Practically: complete the five-question SHIM/IIEF-5 (22–25 no ED, 17–21 mild, 12–16 mild-to-moderate, 8–11 moderate, 5–7 severe) and grade erection hardness 1–4 before the first tablet, again at 8 weeks and at 12 weeks ([Rosen 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10637462/); [Mulhall 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17888069/)). The bar for "it worked" is the minimal clinically important difference, not any change: **4 points on the IIEF erectile-function domain overall, 2 if baseline ED is mild, 5 if moderate, 7 if severe** ([Rosen 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21855209/)). That bar matters because an unblinded self-trial inflates results — placebo alone raised IIEF-EF by 1.78 points across 62 trial arms — and because the pooled arginine effect of +2.74 clears the bar only in mild ED ([AUA 2018 Guideline](https://www.auanet.org/documents/guidelines/ed%20website%20final.pdf); [EAU Guidelines](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)). Life Extension's refund policy makes the experiment financially recoverable in a way no rival matches: a 12-week full-dose trial consumes about six bottles ($192–216), and the company states "We'll refund your purchase price for up to a full year — doesn't matter how many doses are left," subject to its exclusions ([Life Extension safety page](https://www.lifeextension.com/about/life-extension-research/safety); [Life Extension Returns FAQ](https://www.lifeextension.com/returns-faq)).

The supplement route should be abandoned for a doctor and generic tadalafil in five situations, and one of them applies before a single tablet. The first is new or unevaluated ED: among men with angiographic coronary disease, ED appeared before cardiac symptoms in 67%, by a mean of **38.8 months**, and by 24 months in 93% of chronic coronary patients, so Princeton IV tells clinicians "to treat men with ED as being at risk for cardiac events until proven otherwise"; a supplement ordered online spends that window doing nothing about the arteries, and the work-up also catches low testosterone, diabetes, thiazide or SSRI side effects and Peyronie's disease that no blend touches ([Montorsi 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12932937/); [Montorsi 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16854949/); [Princeton IV](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39115509/)). The second is moderate-to-severe ED at baseline: in the 300-man Gallo trial, 2.5 g arginine moved severe ED by **+0.1 IIEF-EF points** and produced no successful intercourse, versus +4.9 for daily tadalafil, so a man scoring 11 or below on the SHIM should not spend 12 weeks on any of these products ([Gallo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/)). The third is a sub-MCID result at 12 weeks at the full dose. The fourth is any red-flag symptom listed above. The fifth is simple arithmetic: FDA-approved generic tadalafil 5 mg daily costs **$5.97 for 30 tablets plus $5.25 shipping at Cost Plus Drugs, or $12–14 with a GoodRx coupon**, against $36–72 a month for Prelox at a studied dose ([Bidwell Health](https://bidwellhealth.com/ed-treatment-cost); [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/tadalafil-cialis)). The drug delivers **+5.4 IIEF-EF points over placebo** in 1,913 men, returning 66% of mild, 48% of moderate and 29% of severe cases to the normal range; it had the lowest treatment-related adverse-event odds of any PDE5 inhibitor in a 2026 network meta-analysis of 83 trials, with headache at 3–6% versus 5% on placebo in the once-daily studies; and it is the only drug licensed for both ED and prostate urinary symptoms ([Porst 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0302283813010245); [Salonia 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42296271/); [Cialis prescribing information](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf)). Its own constraints — an absolute contraindication with nitrates and riociguat, caution with alpha-blockers and antihypertensives, and periodic reassessment of continuous use — are the same constraints that apply to Prelox, which removes the last argument that the supplement is the "safer" option for the men those warnings describe ([Cialis prescribing information](https://pi.lilly.com/us/cialis-pi.pdf); [Viagra prescribing information](https://www.drugs.com/pro/viagra.html)). Men who want to keep arginine in the picture can: adding 2.5–3 g a day to daily tadalafil raised nuisance side effects (dyspepsia, headache, flushing) without serious events or dropouts in 100 men and added about one IIEF-EF point, a combination worth discussing with the prescriber rather than improvising ([Gallo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261690/); [Xu 2021](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33587304)).

## Conclusion

The most useful finding of this comparison is that the "best" of the four earns its place for reasons that have little to do with proven efficacy at the dose it is sold. Prelox wins on corporate hygiene — a traceable 46-year-old seller, a clean adulteration record, a refund that outlasts the trial — and on being the only formula anyone has tested against placebo; but the label's switch to a half dose after two weeks means the product most men actually take is one its own trials never studied, and the burden of reaching the evidence falls on the buyer, either by doubling the bottles or by adding a $7 citrulline powder. The three rivals fail in instructive ways: Performer 8 shows how an honest-looking itemised label can still conceal a stimulant and a liver signal while the company behind it dissolves and re-forms; Semenax shows how a genuinely placebo-controlled trial can be spun into "clinically proven" after it fails on the endpoint a buyer cares about; and ExtenZe shows that a product can survive two decades of settlements, seizures, hidden-drug findings and case reports and still sit on a Walgreens shelf, because DSHEA and the 2004 DHEA carve-out, not any safety review, keep it legal.

The second implication is that the honest competitor to Prelox is not Performer 8 or Semenax but a $6 generic. Once the supplement is held to the same standards a doctor applies to tadalafil — a baseline score, a 12-week horizon, a minimal clinically important difference, the same nitrate and recent-MI exclusions — the drug wins on effect size, on the quality of its safety data, on tolerability and on price, and the supplement's only remaining advantages are that it requires no prescription and carries no drug label. For a man who still prefers to start with Prelox, that is a legitimate choice if, and only if, he has had the cardiovascular work-up that new ED demands, takes the trial dose, measures the result, and treats a null at 12 weeks as information rather than a reason to buy a fifth bottle.
