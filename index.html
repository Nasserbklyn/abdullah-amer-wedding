Questions

Asked across this desk, daily.

What insurance do I need to drive a TLC vehicle?+

Most TLC-licensed vehicles need commercial auto coverage with $100,000/$300,000 bodily-injury liability, $10,000 property damage, uninsured motorist protection and New York no-fault (PIP) — currently $100,000 per person under TLC rules. The exact requirement follows your vehicle class and base — we confirm it before you bind, so there are no surprises at inspection.

What is an FH-1 certificate?+

The FH-1 is New York’s for-hire insurance certificate — the DMV’s proof that your vehicle carries the required coverage. TLC expects it on file and current. When you bind through us, we issue and file it as part of the job, not as an extra.

My coverage lapsed. How bad is it?+

Serious — a lapse can suspend your registration and park your TC plates for weeks, which for a working driver means lost income. Call us the moment you get a cancellation or non-renewal notice; caught early, most lapses can be prevented or fixed quickly.

I’m a brand-new TLC driver. Where do I start?+

Get your TLC driver license first, then your base affiliation letter. Then come to us: we quote the markets, issue your binder and FH-1, and hand you what you need for TLC and DMV plate registration. TLC rules change often — call us and we’ll walk you through the current path.

Do you only work with American Transit?+

No. We’re a listed broker in American Transit’s directory, and we also place coverage with other New York for-hire specialist markets. As independent brokers, our job is to find the market that fits your vehicle, your base and your budget.

Can you handle my personal car and my home too?+

Yes — that’s the point of having one broker. Personal auto, homeowners, condo, co-op and renters. One office that knows your whole picture usually finds gaps (and savings) that five separate 800-numbers never will.
